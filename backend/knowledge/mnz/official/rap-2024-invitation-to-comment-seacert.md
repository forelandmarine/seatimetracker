# RAP 2024 Invitation to comment: paraphrased notes on the SeaCert proposals

This is the original policy document behind the Skipper Two Nautical Miles (S2NM) certificate and behind Maritime NZ's plan for a pathway that lets skilled foreign domestic seafarers get New Zealand domestic certificates. The submissions summary that followed is in maritime-rules-amendments-2025.md (end of that file). These notes are paraphrased, with the paragraph numbers of the original.

## Q: Why was S2NM created? (paras 104 to 112)
Industry told Maritime NZ there was a gap below Skipper Restricted Limits (SRL) for low-risk work close to shore, for example a marine farm moving staff between sites in a sheltered estuary, or fishing close to shore. Without S2NM a new entrant had to get a full SRL. Stakeholders asked for something like the old Limited Launch Operator certificate or Australia's Coxswain Grade 3 Near Coastal (which allows work within 1 nautical mile of shore or near a parent vessel). Maritime NZ also noted that trainees often skip the entry-level Qualified Deck Crew (QDC) certificate and go straight to SRL, leaving a shortage of deckhands.

## Q: How many vessels did Maritime NZ think would use S2NM? (paras 106, 107, 116)
Maritime NZ counted active vessels operating within 2 nautical miles of shore: 198 vessels up to 6 m (35 under MOSS, 163 non-MOSS) and 65 more between 6 m and 12 m (49 MOSS, 16 non-MOSS), 264 in total. It chose the 6 m option (Option one) and rejected 12 m because that would overlap SRL and carry more risk.

## Q: What did Maritime NZ intend S2NM to allow? (paras 113 to 117, 122)
An entry-level skipper certificate for people with some maritime experience: master only (no other crew required), vessels up to 6 m, close to shore, daylight only, no passengers, but able to carry a limited number of others such as workers (the footnote example is ferrying workers to a marine farm or from a marina to a nearby vessel). Fishing privileges were also proposed, so that some small vessels under a Part 40D safe operational plan could use S2NM instead of SRL.

## Q: Was an S2NM exam or course planned? (paras 120, 121, 124)
Yes in principle. Maritime NZ listed its implementation work as guidance updates, website changes, IT changes, internal procedures and checklists, and "a new examination for the S2NM". No course was described: the document says applicants may need to pay for training only if they do not already have the required experience. The application fee would be the existing national certificate of competency charge of $368 (Maritime (Charges) Regulations 2014, Schedule 2).

## Q: When was S2NM meant to start? (para 166)
The rules were scheduled to take effect from September 2025 so that operational policy, practice guidance and information, and Maritime NZ's systems, would be ready first. (The rule did take effect 1 September 2025, but as of 25 October 2026 S2NM is still not in the online application form and no guidance, exam or course has been published. See news-2025-09-skipper-two-nautical-miles.md and COVERAGE.md.)

## Q: What is the "limited pathway for skilled foreign seafarers"? (paras 72 to 103)
A Maritime NZ policy change, needing no rule change, to let foreign seafarers with domestic (non-STCW) qualifications get a New Zealand domestic certificate without fully retraining. The reasons given were skill shortages, including skippers, and complaints that the SeaCert framework makes it hard to bring foreign domestic experience to New Zealand. Today Part 32 requires "relevant training", which in practice means New Zealand training or training the Director has approved, and recognition of foreign certificates is mostly limited to STCW and STCW-F certificates (plus TTMRA for Australians).

## Q: How would the foreign pathway work? (paras 83 to 94)
- Legal basis: section 41(2) of the Maritime Transport Act 1994 lets the Director recognise qualifications or certificates the Director thinks appropriate when granting a maritime document. Section 41(3) forbids recognising foreign qualifications whose requirements are lower than New Zealand's where the Director also thinks recognition would risk safety, property or the marine environment. Part 35's limits on approving non-NZQA training do not restrict section 41(2).
- It would start with a small number of countries whose domestic qualification systems Maritime NZ understands to be of a high standard.
- The seafarer applies for a specific New Zealand national certificate and asks for their foreign domestic qualification to be recognised. Maritime NZ technical staff check whether it is comparable and complete.
- If it falls short, Maritime NZ tells the applicant the gaps, the applicant does "gap-filling" training with a provider such as a maritime school, then continues the application.
- The applicant must still meet medical and eyesight standards, English language requirements, the fit and proper person test and the other certificate requirements, and must pass the relevant examination.
- The certificate may carry conditions, for example restricted to reflect the person's experience.
- It is not a finding that the foreign certificate is equivalent to a New Zealand certificate.

## Q: What would the foreign pathway cost? (paras 100, 101)
No new fee was planned. The technical review would most likely be charged at the hourly rate of $245 under "recognition of non-New Zealand certification/qualification not otherwise provided for" in Schedule 1 of the Maritime (Charges) Regulations 2014, on top of the usual application fee.

## Q: When was the foreign pathway expected? (para 169)
Maritime NZ said building it would be complex and it would not be fully available until November 2025 at the earliest, and that it would update the sector through media channels. After consultation (submissions summary in maritime-rules-amendments-2025.md) Maritime NZ said only that it would update the sector as the pathway is developed. No launch notice, guidance or application route for this pathway was found on 25 October 2026.

## Q: What else was in the SeaCert part of RAP 2024?
- Proposal one: a cut-down QDC competency framework and training record book (non-rule change). Submitters mostly disagreed and Maritime NZ said it would deal with this separately from the rules process. The current published QDC framework and TRB are the ones in this library.
- Proposal four: an extra SRL 500 GT sea service route (18 months in command of commercial ships of 12 m or more, with a TRB). This became rule 32.62 (see Part 32).
