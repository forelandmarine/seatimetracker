equipment training

charts, nautical publications
and ship particulars

nautical publications required
for the voyage are enumerated
and appropriate to the safe
conduct of the voyage
The reasons for the planned
route are supported by facts
and statistical data obtained
from relevant sources and
publications
Positions, courses, distances
and time calculations are
correct within accepted
accuracy standards for
navigational equipment
All potential navigational
hazards are accurately
identified

Approved competency framework for transition to Master <500GT from NZOW STCW with unit standards 5

Manoeuvre and handle a
ship in all conditions
Manoeuvring and handling a ship in all conditions, including:
1. application of constant-rate-of-turn techniques
2. manoeuvring in shallow water, including the reduction in under keel
clearance caused by squat, rolling and pitching
3. interaction between passing ships and between own ship and nearby
banks (canal effect)
4. ship and tug interaction
5. use of propulsion and manoeuvring systems
6. choice of anchorage; anchoring with one or two anchors in limited
anchorages and factors involved in determining the length of anchor cable
to be used
7. dragging anchor; clearing fouled anchors
8. dry-docking, both with and without damage
9. management and handling of ships in heavy weather, including
assisting a ship or aircraft in distress; towing operations; means of
keeping an unmanageable ship out of trough of the sea, lessening drift
and use of oil
10. precautions in manoeuvring to launch rescue boats and survival craft
in bad weather
11. methods of taking on board survivors from rescue boats and survival
craft
12. ability to determine the manoeuvring and propulsion characteristics of
common types of ships, with special reference to stopping distances and
turning circles at various draughts and speeds
13. importance of navigating at reduced speed to avoid damage caused
by own ship’s bow wave and stern wave
14. practical measures to be taken when navigating in or near ice or in
conditions of ice accumulation on board
15. use of, and manoeuvring in and near, traffic separation schemes and
in vessel traffic service (VTS) areas
2. approved simulator lying at
anchor training, where
appropriate
3. approved manned scale
ship model, where appropriate
All decisions concerning
berthing and anchoring are
based on a proper assessment
of the ship’s manoeuvring and
engine characteristics and the
forces to be expected while
berthed alongside or lying at
anchor
While under way, a full
assessment is made of
possible effects of shallow and
restricted waters, ice, banks,
tidal conditions, passing ships
and own ship’s bow and stern
wave so that the ship can be
safely manoeuvred under
various conditions of loading
and weather
