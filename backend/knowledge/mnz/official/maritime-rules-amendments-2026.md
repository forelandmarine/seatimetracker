Objective
The Maritime Rules Amendments 2026 relate to:
1 S2NM certificates:
2 Miscellaneous fixes.
S2NM certificates
The objective of the Maritime Rules Amendments 2026 relating to the S2NM certificate is to:
- change required sea service hours from being self-declared to being approved:
- clarify the operating limits that apply to the S2NM certificate.
Miscellaneous minor corrections to or clarifications in the Maritime Rules
The objective of the Maritime Rules Amendments 2026 relating to miscellaneous fixes to the maritime
rules is to correct minor errors in, or to clarify, existing rules.
The authority for making Maritime Rules Amendments 2026 is found in sections 34(2) and 36(1) of the
Maritime Transport Act 1994.
Maritime Rules (rules) are secondary legislation under the Legislation Act 2019. Under that Act, the
rules are required to be presented to the House of Representatives. The House may, by resolution,
disallow any rule. The Regulations Review Committee is the select committee responsible for
examining all secondary legislation and may consider any matter relating to secondary legislation and
report on it to the House. Anyone can make a complaint to the Regulations Review Committee about
the operation of secondary legislation.
Extent of consultation
Formal consultation on the Proposed Regulatory Amendments Package 3 (RAP 3): Maritime Rules
Amendments 2026 and Marine Protection Rules Amendments 2026 (from which this Maritime Rules
Amendments 2026 was developed) began on 9 April 2026. The consultation on the Maritime Rules
Amendments 2026 concluded on 15 May 2026. The public were invited to comment on the draft rules
via notification on the Maritime New Zealand website. A notice was also published in the Gazette .
Copies of the draft rules were made publicly available during the consultation. A summary of public
consultation is provided at the end of these rules.
Entry into force
The Maritime Rules Amendments 2026 comes into force on 21 September 2026

CONSULTATION DETAILS
The summary of public consultation below does not form part of the rules, but provides details of the
consultation undertaken in making the rules.
Summary of Public Consultation
1. Maritime New Zealand (Maritime NZ) published an intention to make rules in the New Zealand
Gazette on 9 April 2026 (2026-au1783). Publication coincided with the consultation process
commencing. An Invitation to Comment (ITC) document was posted on the Maritime NZ website
for a five week period. Information was also posted on the Government consultation website.
The consultation closed on 15 May 2026. Maritime NZ accepted requests for later submissions
after the closing date to ensure that all relevant submitters were able to be appropriately
consulted.
2. Twenty-three submissions were received from four individuals, one ferry company, one fishing
company, two government agencies, three industry associations, four international industry
associations, three international operators, one international shipping agency, three local
government authorities, and one operator.
Skipper Two Nautical Miles certificate
Proposal to remove the term “self-declared” from row 5 of Table 2A Table of
requirements for the Skipper Two Nautical Miles certificate
3. Six submitters supported the proposal to remove the term “self-declared” from row 5 of “Table 2A
Table of requirements for S2NM". Specific comments included:
- One submitter noted that the use of the term self-declared made it open to misuse.
- One submitter supported change it if improved clarity and reflects Maritime NZ process.
The change should not make it difficult for small vessel operators in low risk conditions
and that evidence requirements should be proportionate.
- One submitter noted that it improved clarity and the certificate’s credibility and aligned it
well with the rules.
- One submitter noted that if the limitation on carrying other persons at night did not exist,
then it could be useful for jetskis or similar craft. They noted that the limitation was
appropriate for safety and managed risk while keeping the certificate aligned to its intent.
- One submitter agreed that the Skipper Two Nautical Miles (S2NM) certificate’s
requirements should be aligned with the rules.
Maritime NZ comments
4. Maritime NZ has considered the submissions and no changes have been made to the proposal.
Maritime NZ notes submitters’ comments on improvements to clarity and alignment with the
rules.
Proposal to amend the privileges of the Skipper Two Nautical Miles certificate
5. Four submitters agreed with the proposal, comments included:
- One submitter considered that the rule must be practical for operators and supported by
guidance that notes when the certificate should be required.
- One submitter noted some costs for fishing seafarers moving to the certificate.
- One submitter noted that it made the certificate less ambiguous which would help
operators and seafarers.
6. One submitter considered that the SeaCert certificate structure should be fully reviewed and
that the S2NM seemed similar to the Limited Launch master certificate. They considered that
any changes to coastal waters and this change should be completed at the same time.
Specific costs, benefits, and other impacts
7. Four submitters commented on specific costs, benefits, or other impacts. Specific comments
included:
- One submitter commented that they saw no extra costs and the proposals should
formalise how they should be operating.
- One submitter noted that there was reduced ambiguity and it would mean clearer use of
the certificate by the sector, they recommended that:
o there be examples provided of operations inside and outside scope; and
o that the S2NM and the Specified Limits Permits be differentiated so as to direct
operators to the correct pathway.
- One submitter noted that there were no specific costs, benefits, or other impacts.
Maritime NZ comments
8. Maritime NZ has considered the submissions and no changes have been made to the proposal.
Maritime NZ notes that guidance will be provided to support the use of the S2NM certificate.
