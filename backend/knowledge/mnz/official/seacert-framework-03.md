# Seafarer Certification and Operational Limits Framework (3 of 4): certificate summaries: yacht (sailing vessels and superyachts) and engineering certificates

Source: https://www.maritimenz.govt.nz/seafarers/getting-certified/background-to-seacert/

Certificates for sailing vessels and superyachts
22 Chief Mate Yacht
Short name Chief Mate Yacht
Regulation STCW Regulation II/2
Privileges To perform the functions and duties of a chief mate on a superyacht of less than
3000 GT in any operating area
Minimum age 18
Minimum sea service Option 1 – 36 months
x operating in a deck capacity while holding an SRL certificate (or equivalent)
x includes at least 6 months performing navigational watchkeeping duties
under supervision of a Master or deck officer
x includes at least 12 months on ships 12 metres or more in length operating
beyond restricted limits (required)
x documented in an approved training record book
Option 2 – 24 months
Completion of approved training that the Director accepts as training in reduction
of sea service, and sea service which includes 500 days actual sea service
including:
x at least six months performing navigational watchkeeping duties while under
supervision of a Master or deck officer and
x 230 days of any combination of actual sea service, standby service or yard
service
Qualification NZ Diploma in Yacht Operations (Level 6)
Final examination Successful completion of any examination the Director may require,
assessed by an approved Maritime NZ examiner
x Medical care on board ship
x Generic ECDIS (required for ships fitted with ECDIS)
x GMDSS
Career progression If you hold a Chief Mate Yacht, you can progress to Master Yacht <500 GT or
Master Yacht

23 Master Yacht less than 500 gross tonnage
Short name Master Yacht <500 GT
Regulation STCW Regulation II/2
Privileges To perform the functions and duties of a master on a superyacht less than 500
GT in any operating area
Prior certification Chief Mate Yacht or equivalent
Minimum sea service 12 months while holding a Chief Mate Yacht Certificate
x onboard yacht service in a deck officer capacity and
x 120 days of watchkeeping service (may be included in the 12 months)
Qualification NZ Diploma in Yacht Operations (Level 6) with Master Yacht strand
Final examination Successful completion of any examination the Director may require,
assessed by an approved Maritime NZ examiner
x Medical care on board ship
x GMDSS
x Generic ECDIS (required for ships fitted with ECDIS)
Career progression If you hold a Master Yacht <500 GT, you can progress to Master Yacht

24 Master Yacht
Short name Master Yacht
Regulation STCW Regulation II/2
Privileges To perform the functions and duties of a master on a superyacht
(less than 3000 GT) in any operating area
Prior certification Chief Mate Yacht or equivalent
Minimum sea service Option 1 – 24 months
x onboard yacht service in a deck officer capacity while holding a Chief Mate
Yacht (or equivalent) including:
x either at last 12 months on ship >24 metres or >80 GT
x or 6 months on ships >500 GT
x includes 240 days of watchkeeping service (this may be part of the 24
months)

Option 2 – 12 months
x onboard yacht service in a deck officer capacity while holding a Master
Yacht <500 GT (or equivalent)
x includes either 6 months on ships >24 metres or >80 GT or 3 months on
ships >500 GT (required)
x includes 120 days of watchkeeping (this may be part of the 12 months)
Qualification NZ Diploma in Yacht Operations (Level 6) – Master Yacht strand
x Medical care on board ship
x Generic ECDIS (required for ships fitted with ECDIS)
x GMDSS
Career progression This is the most senior superyacht command certificate of competency

Engineering certificates
25 Engineroom Watch Rating
Short name EWR
Regulation Certificate of proficiency under STCW Regulation III/4
Privileges To perform the functions and duties as a crew member in a manned engine
room or a periodically unmanned engine room on ships powered by main
propulsion machinery of any propulsion power in any operating area
Minimum age 16
Minimum sea service Option 1 – 2 months (training required)
x on vessels of 750 kW or more (preferred)
x associated with engine room watchkeeping functions and involves the
performance of duties carried out under supervision of qualified engineer
officer or a qualified rating
Option 2 – 6 months (training not required)
x on vessels of 750 kW or more (preferred)
x associated with engine room watchkeeping functions and involves the
performance of duties carried out under supervision of qualified engineer
officer or a qualified rating
Qualification For Option 1 only – NZ Certificate in Maritime Crewing (Level 3) with Engineering
strand
Final examination Successful completion of any examination the Director may require, assessed
by an approved Maritime NZ examiner
Career progression If you hold an EWR, you can progress to AB Engine

26 Able Seafarer Engine
Short name AB Engine
Regulation Certificate of proficiency under STCW Regulation III/5
Privileges To perform the functions and duties as a crew member in a manned engine
room or a periodically unmanned engine room on ships powered by main
propulsion machinery of any propulsion power in any operating area
Prior certification Engineroom watch rating (EWR) or equivalent
Minimum age 18
Minimum sea service Option 1 – 6 months (training required)
x on vessels of 750 kW or more operating beyond restricted limits (preferred)
x in the engine department
Option 2 – 12 months (training not required)
x on vessels of 750 kW or more operating beyond restricted limits (preferred)
x in the engine department
Qualification NZ Certificate in Maritime Crewing (Level 4) with Engineering Strand
Final examination Successful completion of any examination the Director may require, assessed
by an approved Maritime NZ examiner
Career progression x If you hold an AB Engine, you can progress to Integrated Rating (IR)

27 Electro-technical Rating
Short name ETR
Regulation Certificate of proficiency under STCW Regulation III/7
Privileges To perform the functions and duties associated with electrical, electronic and
control engineering on ships of any propulsion power in any operating area
Minimum age 18
Minimum sea service Option 1 – 6 months (training required)
x on vessels of 750 kW or more (preferred)
Option 2 – 3 months
x on vessels of 750 kW or more (preferred)
x hold a Certificate in Electrical Engineering (Level 4) or equivalent (the
equivalent qualification to be assessed by Maritime NZ on a case-by-case
basis)
Option 3 – 12 months (training not required)
x on vessels of 750 kW or more (preferred)
Qualification x NZ Certificate in Maritime Crewing (Level 4) with electro-technology strand
(Option 1 only)
Final examination Successful completion of any examination the Director may require, assessed
by an approved Maritime NZ examiner
Career progression If you hold an ETR, you can progress to Electro-technical Officer

28 Electro-technical Officer
Short name ETO
Regulation STCW Regulation III/6
Privileges To perform the functions and duties associated with electrical, electronic
and control engineering at an operational level on a ship of any
propulsion power in any operating area
Minimum age 18
Minimum sea service Option 1 –12 months combined workshop skills and sea service
x at least 6 months on vessels of 750 kW or more (preferred)
x includes evidence of participation programme including onboard training and
training and record book

Option 2 – 36 months
x at least 6 months on vessels of 750 kW or more (preferred)
x includes at least 30 months in the engine department
Qualification NZ Diploma in Marine Electro-technology (Level 6) (Option 1 only)
x Medical first aid
Career progression This is the most senior electro-technical certificate

29 Marine Engineer Class 6
Short name MEC 6
Privileges To perform the functions and duties on a motor ship as:
(a) an engineer on fishing, passenger and non-passenger ships up to 3000 kW
propulsion power with systems or warranty conditions (or both) within
enclosed water limits
(b) an engineer on passenger ships of up to 750 kW propulsion power within
inshore limits for ships with fewer than four systems
(c) an engineer on non-passenger ships of less than 24 metres of any
propulsion power within coastal limits, that have engine and system
maintenance carried out ashore or under warranty
(d) a second engineer on fishing ships of propulsion power up to 750 kW in the
unlimited area; and
(e) an engineer on fishing ships of less than 24 metres in length of any
propulsion power that have less than 4 systems within offshore and
coastal limits
Minimum age 18
Minimum sea service 200 hours
x on motor ships of any kind (including pleasure craft)
x completion of approved training record book while on board ship
Qualification NZ Certificate in Marine Engineering Class 6 (Level 4)
Final examination Successful completion of any examination the Director may require assessed by
x First aid certificate
Career progression If you hold a MEC 6, you can progress to the following MEC 5 or MEC 4

30 Marine Engineer Class 5
Short name MEC 5
Regulation Aligned with STCW-F certificate Regulation II/5
Privileges To perform the functions and duties on a motor ship as:
(a) an engineer on passenger ships of less than 24 metres and any propulsion
power, operating within coastal limits
(b) an engineer on passenger ships of up to 3000 kW propulsion power with
fewer than four systems and their engine and system maintenance carried
out ashore or under warranty, operating within inshore limits
(c) an engineer on non-passenger ships of less than 24 metres and any
propulsion power, operating within offshore limits
(d) an engineer on non-passenger ships of any propulsion power operating
within restricted limits
(e) an engineer on fishing ships of less than 3000 kW propulsion power with
fewer than four systems and their engine and system maintenance carried
out ashore or under warranty, operating within inshore limits
(f) a second engineer on a fishing ship of any propulsion power in any
operating area.1
(g) a second engineer on fishing ships of under 750 kW propulsion power in
any operating area.
(h) a second engineer on fishing ships of any propulsion power within any of
the following operational areas:
- inshore fishing limits
- the internal waters of New Zealand
- all rivers and other inland waters of New Zealand
Minimum sea service Option 1 – 12 months
x on vessels of 100 kW or more (required)
x while holding a current MEC 6 or equivalent
Option 2 – 24 months
x on vessels of 100 kW or more (required)
x completion of approved training record book within the last 5 years
Option 3 – 12 months plus 200 hours
x on vessels 100 kW or more (required)
x completion of approved MEC 6 training record book within the last 5 years
Qualification NZ Certificate in Marine Engineering Class 6 (Level 4) with Marine Engineering
Class 5 Strand
Final
examination
Successful completion of any examination the Director may require assessed by
Ancillary and
other
x STCW or STCW-F basic safety training
Career progression If you hold a MEC 5, you can progress to MEC 4

1 Note that where an MEC 5 certificate is subsequently renewed as a national-level certificate only, the certificate
will state that it does not authorise the holder to exercise this privilege.

32 Marine Engineer Class 5 – Motor and Steam

(a) an engineer on passenger ships of less than 24 metres of any propulsion

(b) an engineer on passenger ships of up to 3000 kW propulsion power within
inshore limits, on ships that have fewer than four systems and engine
system maintenance carried out ashore or under warranty
(c) an engineer on non-passenger ships of less than 24 metres of any
propulsion power within offshore limits
(d) an engineer on non-passenger ships of any propulsion power within
restricted limits
(e) a second engineer on fishing ships of any propulsion power in any operating
area2
(f) an engineer on fishing ships of less than 3000 kW propulsion power within
inshore limits, on ships that have fewer than four systems and engine and
system maintenance carried out ashore or under warranty
(g) a second engineer on fishing ships of under 750 kW propulsion power in
any operating area
(h) a second engineer on fishing ships of any propulsion power within any of
the following operational areas:
i. inshore fishing limits
ii. the internal waters of New Zealand
iii. all rivers and other inland waters of New Zealand

(j) an engineer on passenger and non-passenger steam ships of less than 24

performing duties on diesel engines and 6 months performing duties on
steam propulsion engines or boilers

Class 5 Strand

33 Marine Engineer Class 4
Short name MEC 4
Regulation Aligned with STCW-F certificate Regulation II/5
Privileges To perform the functions and duties on a motor ship as chief engineer:
(a) on passenger and non-passenger ships of less than 24 metres of
any propulsion power, within coastal and offshore limits
(b) on passenger and non-passenger ships under 3000 GT of any vessel
length and propulsion power, within restricted limits
(c) on fishing ships under 3000 kW propulsion power operating in any
operating area3
(d) on fishing ships under 750 kW propulsion power in any operating area
(e) on fishing ships of any propulsion power within any of the following areas:
i. inshore fishing limits
ii. the internal waters of New Zealand
iii. all rivers and other inland waters of New Zealand.
Minimum sea service Option 1 – with MEC 5
x 12 months
x on vessels of 250 kW or more (required) operating beyond restricted limits
x while holding MEC 5 or equivalent

Option 2 – with shore-based engineering training
x 12 months
x on vessels of 250 kW or more (required) operating beyond restricted limits
x with evidence of successful completion of shore-based engineering training

Option 3 – with MEC 6
x 24 months
x on vessels of 250 kW or more operating beyond restricted limits
x while holding MEC 6 or equivalent
Qualification NZ Diploma in Marine Engineering (Level 6)
Ancillary
and other
x STCW-F basic safety training
x proficiency in survival craft and rescue boats (other than fast rescue boats)
x advanced fire fighting
Career progression MEC 4 is the highest engineering certificate for non-STCW ships

Note – MEC 4 and MEC 3 training courses are the same. If you want to get a MEC 3 certificate you need
to meet the sea service requirements listed under MEC 3.

3 Note that where an MEC 4 certificate is subsequently renewed as a national-level certificate only, the certificate
will state that it does not authorise the holder to exercise this privilege.
