Objective
The objective of the Mariti me Rules Amendments 2025 is to amend Maritime Rules Parts 19, 20, 31,
32, 34, 35, 40A, 40C, 40D, 40E, and 82  to make changes to S eacert and non- commercial use of
commercial ships, and to make corrections to the maritime rules.
Seacert
The objective of the Maritime Rules Amendments 2025 relating to Seacert is to update Maritime Rules
Parts 31 and 32 to:
- facilitate entry to the sector for maritime workers
- make a number of other changes to address regulatory and system issues.
These objectives will be met through introducing the new Skipper two nautical mile certificate and a
further option to fulfil sea service for the Skipper Restricted Limits endorsed to 500 GT.
Non-commercial use of commercial ships
The objective of the Maritime Rules Amendments 2025 relating to non-commercial use of commercial
ships is to update Maritime Rules Parts 19, 20, 31, 32, 40A, 40C, 40D, 40E, and 82 to set out
requirements for the non-commercial use of a commercial ship, ensure consistency, and clarify
provisions that do not apply during non-commercial use.
Minor and technical changes
The objective of the Maritime Rules Amendments 2025 is also to make minor corrections or other
amendments to clarify or update rules in Parts 19, 20, 31, 32, 34, 35, and 82.
The authority for making Maritime Rules Regulatory Amendment Package 2025 is found in sections 34
and 36 of the Maritime Transport Act 1994, including sections 34(1)(c) and (2), and sections 36(1)(a),
(j), (k), (n), (o), (p), (za), and (zb).
Maritime Rules (rules) are secondary legislation under the Legislation Act 2019. Under that Act, the
rules are required to be presented to the House of Representatives. The House may, by resolution,
disallow any rule. The Regulations Review Committee is the select committee responsible for examining
all secondary legislation and may consider any matter relating to secondary legislation and report on it
to the House. Anyone can make a complaint to the Regulations Review Committee about the operation
of secondary legislation.
Extent of consultation
Formal consultation on the Proposed Regulatory Amendments Package (RAP1) Maritime Rules
Amendments 2024 (from which this Maritime Rules Amendment s 2025 was developed) began on 4
October 2024 and concluded on 15 November 2024. The public were invited to comment on the draft
rules via notification on the Maritime New Zealand website. A notice was also published in the Gazette.
Copies of the draft rules were made publicly available during the consultation. A summary of consultation
is provided at the end of these rules.

3 New rule 19.4
After rule 19.3, insert the following rule 19.4:
“19.4 Non-commercial use of a commercial ship
(1) This rule 19.4 applies to the owner and the maritime transport operator of a ship that is
engaged in the non-commercial use of that ship.
(2) Where a ship in a maritime transport operation is engaged in non-commercial use, rules
19.62(1), 19.62(2)(b)(ii), and 19.62(3)(c) do not apply to that ship’s non-commercial use,
during the period of that use.
(3) The owner or the maritime transport operator, or both, as applicable, must ensure that the
non-commercial use of the ship is conducted in accordance with the applicable rules in
this Part, including the safe operating procedures applicable to the operation.
(4) The owner or the maritime transport operator, or both, as applicable, must ensure that,
during the non-commercial use of the ship, any powered equipment, lifting equipment,
and any other machinery on board the ship that could pose a danger to passengers or
other persons on board is secured or isolated from those passengers and persons.
(5) The owner or the maritime transport operator, or both, as applicable, must ensure that,
during the non-commercial use of the ship,—

(a) the ship’s wake does not cause unnecessary danger or risk of damage to other
ships or structures, or of harm to other persons; and
(b) in circumstances where tides, river flows, visibility, rough seas, adverse weather,
emergencies, or other situations cause danger or a risk to the safety of persons on
board, every person on board wears a properly secured personal flotation device of
an appropriate size for that person.
(6) The owner and the maritime transport operator must ensure that the safe operating
procedures referred to in clause 1.4 of the Appendix, relating to the non-commercial use
of a ship, includes details of provisioning for the following, where appropriate:
(a) up to date charts and nautical publications relevant to the areas covered by the
proposed voyage:
(b) how the ship is crewed and the crew’s skill and experience requirements to ensure
sufficient crew and sufficient qualified crew are carried on board to operate the ship
safely, taking into account the assessment criteria in rule 31.21 of Part 31:
(c) any circumstances where the scope of certification of the ship will be exceeded:
(d) lifesaving appliances, firefighting appliances, communications and distress
signalling equipment, and navigational equipment that are appropriate to the area
and circumstances of the intended operation:
(7) The owner or the maritime transport operator, or both, as applicable, must ensure the
non-commercial use of any ship is exclusively for the purpose of the owner’s or maritime
transport operator’s pleasure, and is conducted as a discrete voyage that is separate to
and independent of any other use of that ship.
(8) The owner or the maritime transport operator, or both, as applicable, must ensure that,
during the non-commercial use of any ship, the maximum cargo capacity, and maximum
number of people on board assigned to that ship in its scope of certification is not
exceeded.
(9) Other than for those elements of the scope of certification that are not permitted to be
exceeded under subrule (8), a ship may be operated outside its scope of certification,
during its non-commercial use, to the extent permitted in the relevant safe operating
procedures applicable to the operation and in accordance with this rule 19.4.”.

The summary of public consultation below does not form part of the rules, but provides details of the
consultation undertaken in making the rules.
Summary of public consultation
1. Maritime NZ published a notice of the Minister’s intention to make rules in the New Zealand
Gazette on 4 October 2024 (2024-au5026). Publication coincided with the consultation process
commencing. An Invitation to Comment (ITC) document was posted on the Maritime NZ website
for a six week period. Information was also posted on the Government consultation website.
The consultation closed on 15 November 2024.
2. Sixteen submissions were received from two individuals, one marine pilot, one local authority,
six operators, one training organisation, two industry associations and three government
agencies. Overall:
- Non-commercial use of commercial vessels – most submitters agreed with the
proposed rules changes to reduce regulation of non-commercial use subject to key
conditions. One submitter noted ambiguity in the current rules, but disagreed with the
proposals to address the issue and considered a legislative change was required. They
considered that the definition of a pleasure craft in the Maritime Transport Act 1994 (the
Act) should be amended so that non-commercial vessels are deemed to be pleasure craft
for the duration of the non-commercial journey. Additionally, the vessel need not comply
the Maritime Transport Operator Certificate (MTOC) or the Maritime Transport Operator
Plan (MTOP).
- SEACERT – most submitters supported the new limited pathway for skilled foreign
seafarers to enter the New Zealand domestic sector and the Skipper Two Nautical Mile
(S2NM) certificate. Submitters who commented on the amendments to the Skipper
Restricted Limits (SRL) endorsed to 500 GT considered that further or other work was
required on the endorsement.
Most feedback was on the non-rule proposal to amend the competency framework and
training record book (TRB) for the Qualified Deck Crew (QDC) certificate. Submitters
disagreed with the proposals. This issue will be dealt separately to the rules process.
Overall comments
3. Two submitters provided overall comments on the proposed changes. These included:
- One submitter noted that they had no concerns with the proposed changes.
- One submitter had reservations about the non-commercial use proposals for commercial
vessels.
Non-commercial use of commercial vessels
Rule changes to reduce regulation of non-commercial use subject to key conditions
4. Seven submitters commented on the proposal for rule changes to reduce regulation of non-
commercial use subject to key conditions.
- Three submitters agreed with the preferred option. One submitter sought clarification on
whether a vessel during a period of non-commercial use could permissibly be provided to
a third party of the owner or operator, such as a family member, friend, or other person
under the policy wording in 19.4(8) [now rule 19.4(7)] “exclusively for the purpose of the
owner’s or maritime transport operator’s pleasure”.
- One submission raised concerns about whether the new provisions proposed would
prohibit ‘mixed trips’ where commercial and non-commercial/recreation activities are
mixed in a single journey.
- One submitter asked about the application of Health and Safety at Work Act 2015
(HSWA) to a commercial vessel during non-commercial use.

- One submitter noted ambiguity in the current rules, but disagreed with the proposals to
address the issue and considered a legislative change would be better. They were
concerned with the proposals for change in rule 19.4. They considered that they were in
vessels standard operating procedures and were redundant.
Specifically, they considered that the definition of a pleasure craft in the Act should be
amended so that non-commercial vessels are deemed to be pleasure craft for the
duration of the non-commercial journey. Additionally, the vessel need not comply the
Maritime Transport Operator Certificate (MTOC) or the Maritime Transport Operator Plan
(MTOP).
New provisions cover risks when operating
5. One submitter raised concerns about the provisions on securing or isolating commercial
equipment on board that may pose a danger to passengers or other persons during any period
of non-commercial use. The submitter noted that this responsibility of the owner or operator
may be covered by existing obligations under HSWA.
Maritime NZ comment
6. We consider that an owner or operator providing their vessel to a third party for non-commercial
use is consistent with the policy intent and that any risks would be managed by that owner or
operator.
7. Our proposal does not prohibit trips that contain both commercial and non-commercial activities.
The proposal is that lesser regulation applies to trips that are non-commercial only. If a trip has
a commercial component, then all of the normal existing regulations apply.
8. During a period of non-commercial use, HSWA will generally not apply as no work activity is
taking place, however a person conducting a business or undertaking (PCBU) could still be
liable under some circumstances. For example, if in the course of normal work activity, regular
maintenance is neglected by the PCBU and an incident occurs, even during a period of non-
commercial use, that PCBU could be liable if it does not (through action or inaction, as in this
case) carry out its primary duty of care.
9. An amendment to the Act is out of scope of this consultation. Maritime NZ considers that the
changes to the proposals for non-commercial use will mean that its requirements are not overly
onerous.
10. The proposal for securing or isolating equipment aligns with Australia. Maritime NZ considers
that this further specification is necessary as during a period of non-commercial use a vessel
may have passengers or other persons on board that are not familiar with the reasonably
practicable measures under HSWA for safe operating.
Seafarer Certification
Proposal One: Amend the competency framework and the TRB for the QDC Certificate
11. Five submitters commented on the proposal to amend the competency framework and the TRB
for the QDC certificate.
Shortages of QDC certificate holders
12. Four submitters commented that they had seen no shortages in the supply of QDC certificate
holders. Two submitters provided further comment:
- One submitter noted serious culture and wage level issues in the maritime sector and that
it was hard to get training signed off on.
- One submitter noted that if new employees do not have a QDC certificate then they assist
them to gain one. They also noted that it was an accessible certificate that can be
delivered internally, the trainee can work on the job, and it introduces them to the
maritime system. They also noted its importance in in passenger vessel operations
because of requirements to it being in Minimum Safe Crewing Document (MSCD) tables.

Financial cost impacts of changing the QDC certificate’s competency framework and
TRB
13. Four submitters commented on cost impacts of changing the competency framework and TRB
for the QDC certificate. Submitter comments included:
- One submitter noted that training providers may lose income. They stated that training
providers get funding to deliver a course that only uses the TRB and this was not in the
spirit of the training framework.
- One submitter considered that this was not an issue.
- One submitter stated that operators would still train seafarers under the current settings
for the QDC certificate and therefore there would be no cost savings.
- One submitter stated it would contribute to a low wage and productivity economy and
would not encourage workforce development. They considered that it would lead to
employee turnover and an increase of inexperienced seafarers in the industry.
- One submitter considered that revising the QDC certificate's competency framework and
TRB would have no financial impact and that the primary financial implications for
seafarer’s are from New Zealand Qualification Authority qualification pathways and the
Tertiary Education Commission funding framework.
Feedback on the proposed changes to the competency framework and TRB for the QDC
14. Seven submitters commented on the proposed changes to the QDC certificate’s competency
framework and the TRB. Five submitters did not support the proposed changes. Their individual
comments included:
- One submitter was satisfied with current settings and that the competencies were all
relevant. They considered that deletions did not suit the QDC certificate’s purpose.
- One submitter commented that the proposal would worsen existing maritime sector
issues and would not favour employees. They commented that industry should provide
more training opportunities and work to retain skilled seafarers.
- One submitter consider that the changes were excessive and could impact how QDC-
qualified crew are utilized on-board.
- One submitter supported removing specific tasks such as splicing lines and gave
suggestions for alignment with training requirements. The submitter also suggested that:
o The medical requirements for QDCs should be simplified or eliminated.
o STCW training courses and adapting Australian training could be useful.
15. One submitter commented that the QDC should be an entry level qualification but there should
be two entry points. Either with sea service (allowing support roles to apply) or through study.
16. One submitter suggested changes to the proposed framework including that:
- “Buoyage” be amended to “Identifies lateral and cardinal buoys and explains how to pass
correctly”; and
- “Assisting in the wheelhouse” be amended to “Understands the principles of keeping a
safe lookout”.
Other impacts, costs, or benefits not noted in the consultation document
17. Three submitters noted other impacts, costs, or benefits. There was no shared themes from
their submissions. Comments included:
- One submitter stated that they considered the changes would reduce the requirements
for QDC certificates and further training would be required to ensure that they could work
at the same level as current holders.

- One submitter stated that some current certificate holders did not understand their role
properly.
- One submitter noted differences before and after COVID. Before COVID they had trained
QDC certificate holders with an industry training organisation and trained 60 people per
year. After COVID, it has been difficult to run the programme.
Further comments
18. Four submitters made further comments on the proposed changes including:
- One submitter noted significant negative workplace culture issues.
- Two submitters offered assistance on amending the QDC certificate’s TRB and
competency framework.
- One submitter suggested a settings change for MSCDs to say: “QDC or a person who
has completed and approved Part 35 QDC”. They considered that this would ha ve
significant advantages. This includes reducing application costs, processing time, and
administration time.
- One submitter suggested that the QDC should be aligned with the MCA Deck rating
yacht. If the two frameworks were consistent then this would support seafarers and
worker retention. They considered that the word “yacht” should be added into the
framework.
Maritime NZ comment
19. Maritime NZ is not proposing changes to the amendment rules. Maritime NZ does note the
submitters’ concerns about the proposed changes to the QDC framework and TRB and will
respond to them outside the rules’ process.

Maritime NZ comment
37. Maritime NZ notes submitters’ comments for the proposal and other queries. Maritime NZ
considers that the submitter comments were beyond the scope of possible changes that could
be made to the rules. As Maritime NZ was aware that prior to consultation the requirements for
the SRL endorsed to 500 GT was an existing issue, no changes were made to the proposed
rules.
Minor and technical amendments
38. One submitter commented on the minor and technical rules amendments in relation to the
proposed amendment to the privileges of the Chief Mate Yacht certificate to align it with the
Master Yacht certificate. They noted that superyachts are defined as starting at 24m or over in
length and that they include both sail and powered craft. They considered that this qualification
was unnecessary apart for some small sail school companies. They proposed a limit of 200 t
and having a Power/ sail option. They suggested consulting the superyacht sector to increase
the pool of available seafarers.
Maritime NZ comment
39. Maritime NZ notes that feedback on the usefulness of the certificates. Maritime NZ considers
that the proposed change is beyond the scope of a minor and technical amendment. Maritime
NZ is proposing no changes to rules.
