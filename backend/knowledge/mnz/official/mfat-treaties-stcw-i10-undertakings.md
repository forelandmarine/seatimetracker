# STCW Regulation I/10 undertakings in the MFAT treaties register

## Q: What is a Regulation I/10 undertaking and why does it matter to me?
Under STCW Regulation I/10, a country may only endorse (recognise) another country's STCW certificate of competency for masters, officers or radio operators if the two administrations have an undertaking. Maritime NZ's recognition guidance says that for masters, officers and GMDSS operators, only certificates from countries with an I/10 agreement with New Zealand can be recognised; ratings can be recognised from any STCW "White List" country. Undertakings run in one direction: some let New Zealand recognise the other country's certificates for service on New Zealand ships ("into NZ"), others let the other flag accept New Zealand certificates ("NZ out"). For a foreign seafarer wanting to work on a New Zealand ship, only the "into NZ" direction matters.

## Q: Does the register match Maritime NZ's own list of countries it recognises?
Mostly. Maritime NZ's July 2026 guidance lists 28 issuing countries for master and officer certificates (Australia, Belgium, Canada, Croatia, Denmark, Fiji, Finland, France, Germany, Hong Kong, Iceland, Ireland, Kiribati, Malta, Malaysia, Netherlands, Norway, Pakistan, Panama, Philippines, Poland, Republic of Korea, Romania, Singapore, Sri Lanka, Ukraine, United Kingdom, Uruguay). Differences found:
- In the register "into NZ" but not on Maritime NZ's list: Barbados (NRA 2001/34, 2001). Treat Barbados as not currently recognised unless Maritime NZ confirms.
- On Maritime NZ's list but not found in the archived register: Finland, Panama, Philippines, Poland, Romania, Uruguay (a Romanian undertaking appears in a search listing but its record was not archived). This is most likely an archiving gap, not proof there is no undertaking.
- Belgium and the Netherlands appear in the archive only as "NZ out" records, although Maritime NZ recognises both; the reverse records were probably not archived.
- No undertaking with the USA appears anywhere, and the USA is not on Maritime NZ's list, so USCG officer licences cannot be recognised as STCW certificates of competency (USCG ratings may still qualify through the White List route).

## Q: Is the UK (MCA) covered, including MCA yacht certificates?
Yes for the UK as an administration: NRA 2011/80 (in effect 29 November 2011) covers recognition of UK training and certification for service on New Zealand vessels, and the UK is on Maritime NZ's list. Neither the register nor Maritime NZ says anything specific about MCA yacht certificates (OOW Yacht, Chief Mate Yacht, Master Yacht). Maritime NZ's recognition guidance maps foreign yacht certificates to the NZ yacht certificates in Part 32 rules 32.108 to 32.113, and recognition of masters and officers needs an oral exam. Recognition is not the same as using MCA time towards a new NZ certificate: that is a sea service question (Part 32 rules 32.173 to 32.182) handled case by case, with one documented case in providers/dawson-associates-maritime-law.md.

## Q: Is anything changing?
Nothing in the register or anywhere else found on 25 October 2026 announces new or withdrawn I/10 undertakings. The most recent undertaking recorded is Germany (2012 to 2013). In 2024 submitters asked Maritime NZ to make Ukraine "a recognised administration" (maritime-rules-amendments-2025.md, submissions summary); Ukraine is already on both the register (2002) and Maritime NZ's list, so this probably concerns how recognition works in practice rather than the list itself.
