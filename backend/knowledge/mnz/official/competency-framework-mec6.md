Function: Operate Vessel Machinery and Systems

Function: Operate Vessel Machinery and Systems

Diesel engines.

The layout,
function and
care required.

1. Common
systems and
components.
Oral and written
examination, and
assessment by
observation
recorded in an
approved task
book. Task book
criteria are marked
with an asterisk (*).
Demonstrates knowledge of: 4 & 2 stroke cycle, principal components,
location and functions.
2. Fuel System

- Identifies key components and describes their purpose, including:  diesel
supply from fuel tank to injectors, filters, pumps and bleed points,
identifying faulty injectors, for both common rail and jerk type systems
- Describes the operation of the diesel injector
- Primes the diesel fuel system and removes air
- Describes bunkering and fuel transfer procedures
- Bunkers a vessel without spill and according to the bunkering plan
3. Lubrication
System

- Identifies key components and describes their purpose, including: layout
of a typical lubrication system, types of oil & checking oil, reasons for
abnormal oil pressure and the action to take to correct, including fuel and
water contamination.
- Discusses the dangers of fuel contamination and describes action to take
to correct
- Describes maintenance of a clean oil supply
- Conducts a oil lubrication oil change
4. Air System Identifies key components and describes their purpose, including: filters,
turbo charger, blowers, exhaust system and exhaust colour diagnosis.

5. Cooling System

Identifies key components and describes their purpose, including: typical raw
water cooling system, its components and functions, Impeller replacement,
system blockages and rectification, anodes, anti-siphoning valves, typical
fresh water cooling system, its components and functions. Impeller
replacement, heat exchangers, the importance of correct corrosion inhibitor,
thermostat checking and replacement and keel-cooling systems.
6. Electrical
System

- Describes safe electrical practices. Identifies key components and
describes their purpose, including: batteries, starting systems, glow plugs,
alternator, charging system, drive-belt adjustments, fuses and circuit
breakers.
- Sketches a typical DC and AC distribution system and describes and
explains the protection devices
- Describes safe electrical practices including the principles and use of
Isolating transformers, Residual Current Devices, and certification and
use of hand tools
7. Starting
Systems
- Describes the electrical and air start systems found on a vessel of this
class
- States the reasons for failure to start and state actions to take to make
emergency start arrangements
8. Propellers &
Stern Glands
Identifies key components and describes their purpose, including: shaft,
thrust arrangements, stern glands and flexible couplings.
9. Gearboxes - Identifies key components and describes their purpose
- Describes the care of the gearbox and emergency arrangements
- Describes the daily checks required

10. Engine and
thrust bearing
mountings
- Identifies key components and describes their purpose
11. Control
systems
- Identifies key components and describes their purpose
- Describes back-up control systems
- Describes safety features including Alarms system interlocks and limp
home modes
12. Steering
systems
- Describes simple mechanical and hydraulic steering systems.
- Describes the check made to these systems and the emergency steering
gear
13. Equipment  - Describes safe use of winches and windlasses
- Describes a basic fridge system and the safety precautions when running
and maintaining.
14. System Safety - Lists PPE to be used in the machinery space
- Demonstrates awareness of engine room hazards and describes hazard
identification and onboard procedures for elimination, isolation and
minimisation, including, heat, pressures, slipping, moving machinery,
guards, hand tools, common onboard chemicals for cleaning and
maintenance.
- Defines confined/enclosed spaces and states the hazards. Describes the
procedures for entering
Operational
procedures.
1. Engine Room
Watchkeeping
- Describes the records to be kept in the engine room log book and how
these can assist in the early diagnosis of engine faults
- Describes the purpose and management of the ‘Maintenance Plan’ as
required by the MOSS Maritime transport Operator Plan (MTOP)
- Describes the importance of keeping the Master informed of the running
condition of the vessel

2. Machinery
operating
procedures are
described.
Describes normal operational procedures for engine as described in the
MTOP
3. Safe working
practices are
demonstrated.
Operates vessel’s propelling machinery while maintaining safe working
practices and situational awareness.
4. Safe working
practices with
lifting gear is
described
- Describes safety precautions to be taken when operating equipment:
winches, windlass, lifting gear, ramps and davits.
- Describes precautions to be taken before using lifting gear, including
checking of lifting gear and load
- Describes procedures for safe use of lifting gear, including signals,
entrapment and identification of site specific hazards.
- Understands the requirements of Maritime Rule Part 49
Operate and
monitor a
vessel’s engines
and auxiliary
equipment.

1. Load/check
spares, fuel,
lubricants and
fresh water for
intended voyage.

- Prepare vessel in accordance with the vessel’s MTOP, including
knowledge of fuel and lubricant piping systems, valves, pumps and safety
arrangements.
- Loads fuel and lubricants are in accordance with the vessel’s safety
management plan
- Checks spare parts and ensures they are sufficient to cover emergencies
and are in accordance with the vessel operating practices
- Fills fresh water tanks in accordance with the vessel operating practices.
- Completes documentation in accordance with the vessel’s MTOP

2. Pre-start checks
of engines and
auxiliary
equipment

Completes pre-start checks in accordance with the vessel’s MTOP, including
where applicable:
- *Propulsion engine/s, including: fuel, oil and cooling water header tank
levels, valves, V-belts and hoses
- *Auxiliary equipment including: generators, batteries, bilge pumps,
strainers, bilge water levels and alarms, refrigeration, hydraulic system,
fire-fighting system, electrical system (fuses/circuit breakers and
switchboards)
3. Start and
monitor engines
and auxiliary
equipment
*Starts engines and auxiliary equipment and monitors gauges and instrument
readings during warm-up in accordance with the manufacturer’s operating
instructions
4. Test alarms and
safety
arrangements
*Tests alarms in accordance with the manufacturer’s operating instructions
5. Emergency
starting

Describes procedures in accordance with the vessel’s MTOP
6. Shut-down of
engines and
auxiliary
equipment
*Follows procedures in accordance with the vessel’s MTOP

Monitor the
operation of the
vessel’s
engines, drive
train, and
auxiliary
equipment.

1. Operate
vessel’s engines
and equipment
*Operates machinery to maintain ma ximum performance, observing safety
precautions, in accordance with the manufacturer’s operating instructions
2. Changes in
operational
performance
*Monitors and adjusts any changes in accordance with the vessel’s MTOP,
and identifies likely causes including: heavy weather, reduced visibility,
fishing, towing, breakdowns, low fuel, propeller and hull damage.
3. Environmental
protection
Describes bunkering and fuel transfer procedures. Bunker a vessel without
spill and according to the bunkering plan. Oil Spill Contingency Plan is
described. Penalties associated with accidental discharge of fuels, oils and
sewage from the vessel are described. Procedures for the treatment and
discharge of sewage, and black and grey water from the vessel are
described. Recording of fuel/oil/sewage movement on/off the vessel is
demonstrated.

Maintain
propulsion
machinery.

1. Demonstrate
knowledge of the

Oral and written

Checks and records in accordance with the vessel’s Maintenance Plan, the

2. Conduct
planned
maintenance
Carries out planned maintenance and inspection:
- Oil change
- Fuel filters
- Bleeding air from fuel
- Battery checks and top-up
- Inspects electrical switchboards, wiring, fuses and circuit breakers
- Replaces drive belts
Maintenance
and repairs on
a vessel's
mechanical
and electrical
systems.
Select and
prepare stores
and spare parts.
Selects stores and spares for scheduled maintenance to mechanical and
electrical systems in accordance with manufacturers’ instructions

Scheduled
maintenance.
1. Scheduled
maintenance
tasks are
performed.
Tasks to include where possible:
- *Electrical systems: alternators or generators, batteries; navigation
lights; switch boards, fuses and fuse links, circuit breakers; power
points, switches and lights.
- *Mechanical systems: propulsion system (main engine, gearbox,
shafting, sterntube and propeller), bilge system (pumps and motors,
strainers, valves, piping); refrigeration plant; hydraulic system (pumps
and motors, piping, control and other valves, filters, header tanks and
piping); deck machinery (trawl winches, anchor windlass and anchors,
deck cranes); steering systems (wheel, means of transmission from
wheel to rudder, steering motor, rudder, emergency steering); fire
pumps, valves and piping, hoses and nozzles.
- Test and maintain fishing equipment if fitted.
2. Systems are
tested.
*Test systems before return to service in accordance with manufacturers’
instructions.
3. Complete
documentation.
*Completes documentation, and files in accordance with the vessel’s
MTOP
4. Handle
materials safely.
*Handles, stores and secures maintenance materials and equipment in
accordance with vessel’s MTOP
Fault
diagnosis.
Diagnose faults
in mechanical
and electrical
systems.
*Diagnoses faults including: Mechanical systems – change in oil pressure,
overheating, lack of fuel, discolouration of exhaust, uneven running,
unusual noises, failure to operate, fault indicating light or alarm.
Electrical systems – failure to operate, fault indicating light or alarm.

Unscheduled
maintenance.
Perform
unscheduled
maintenance
tasks.
*Performs unscheduled maintenance and repair tasks where possible to
mechanical and electrical systems in accordance with manufacturer’s
instructions including:
- *Identifies, removes, replaces and tests mechanical and electrical
components requiring replacement
- *Repairs to mechanical systems allow the vessel to continue to operate
without causing further damage to the vessel and/or its engines and
equipment
- *Performs any improvised repairs to rectify component failures where
replacement or full repair is not possible, to ensure continued safety of
the vessel, its crew and passengers, in accordance with the vessel’s
MTOP.

room equipment
-Bilge system

- Describes the layout of a typical bilge system
- Describes the principles of suction and the reasons for failure of the
system
- Identifies the likely causes of a rise in bilge  level and states the
prevention measures to be taken
- States the reasons for bilge ventilation
- Describes the causes of back flooding and state the action to take if
occurs.
- Operates a vessel bilge system

- Describes the procedures for identifying fire and explosion hazards,
and the actions to take to prevent fire on the vessel.
- Describes the layout and maintenance required of a typical vessel fire
main system
- Describes the principle of operation, use, location and maintenance of
a CO2, Dry Powder and Foam extinguisher
- Describes the action that may be required as Engineer as part of the
vessel Fire Party
- Demonstrates basic fire fighting techniques

- Describes the operation, limitations and cautions to be undertaken
