# Maritime NZ library coverage check

Checked 2026-10-25 against the certificates the app covers. For each certificate family the table shows which official files explain (a) sea service, (b) training and courses, (c) assessments and oral exams, (d) medical, (e) application process and (f) renewal (revalidation). Ratings: GOOD = the question can be answered from official files with a current or near-current source; PARTIAL = answerable but relying on an old, orphaned or under-review document, or only part of the answer is there; MISSING = no official source in the library.

File names are in `official/`. "P32-n" means the Part 32 split file `part-32-0n-*.md`.

## Conflicts and gaps that remain

1. STCW-F renewal sea service: rule 32.21(3) allows 12 months in the last 5 years (or 30 months of accepted shore equivalents) for STCW-F and STCW-F-aligned certificates, with no "3 months in the last 6 months" option. The Renewing STCW-F page (April 2026) also offers "3 months in the previous 6 months", and so does the live online form's renewal checklist for MFV Limited, MFV Unlimited, SFV Limited and SFV Unlimited (checked 25 Oct 2026; its MEC 4 and MEC 5 STCW-F renewal text follows the rule). No clarification, news item or rule change was found. The assistant should state the rule, say Maritime NZ's page and form show an extra option, and suggest confirming with Maritime NZ before relying on it.
2. Online application form: the requirement checklists the form shows before you start (every certificate, pathway, endorsement and renewal) are now captured in online-application-form-requirements-2026.md. Still not captured: the detailed document upload list, which only appears after an application is started (starting one needs personal details, so it was not done). Three form entries conflict with Part 32 and are flagged in that file (SRL 24 m wording, SRL <500 GT missing the 18-month route, fishing renewal 3-in-6 option).
3. STCW and STCW-F Convention text: Part 32 sets many sea service requirements by reference to STCW regulations (II/1 to II/5, III/1 to III/7) and STCW-F (II/1, II/2, II/4). The convention text is IMO copyright and was not stored; the numbers come from the Maritime NZ guidelines and the SeaCert framework (January 2024).
4. Skipper two nautical miles: still no guideline, approved course, competency framework, exam, NZQA qualification, provider course or launch notice (checked 25 Oct 2026: Maritime NZ news feed, training and framework pages, RAP 3 page, seven providers, NZQA, fyi.org.nz, web and forum search). S2NM is also missing from the online form's certificate list, so it cannot yet be applied for online. No Maritime NZ statement explains why it is missing from the form. The policy intent and original timetable (rules from September 2025 once guidance, systems and a new S2NM exam were ready) are in official/rap-2024-invitation-to-comment-seacert.md; the 2026 rule tweaks were made because Maritime NZ found two problems while implementing it (rap3-invitation-to-comment-2026.md, maritime-rules-amendments-2026.md). Industry views: providers/nzmta-rap2024-submission.md.
5. Advisory circulars for Parts 31 and 32: none published (Maritime NZ says they are being amended).
6. Chief Mate Yacht training record book: not published by Maritime NZ.
7. Competency frameworks missing for Integrated Rating, Skipper Fishing Vessel Unlimited, Chief Mate <3000 GT, S2NM and the SRL endorsements: rechecked 25 Oct 2026, the Competency frameworks page (last updated 6 July 2026) still lists none, and no draft, consultation document or OIA reply containing one exists anywhere we could find. The online form gives the qualification and exam requirements, but not the competency content. Partial stand-ins (UNOFFICIAL): NZQA graduate profiles for SFV Unlimited (2893 Skipper strand) and for the AB Deck and AB Engine training behind Integrated Rating (2511), in providers/nzqa-maritime-qualifications-graduate-profiles.md; the January 2026 NZQA review of seven maritime qualifications created no new S2NM, Integrated Rating or Chief Mate <3000 GT qualification. Chief Mate <3000 GT is taught through the MIT Level 7 diploma, a provider-developed qualification (ref 3832 on careers.govt.nz); no published graduate profile for it was found, only provider course descriptions. Tanker, polar and IGF material was not stored.
8. SeaCert framework document (January 2024) is being updated; it predates S2NM and the 2025 and 2026 rule changes.
9. Recognition guidance (STCW July 2026 re-save, STCW-F February 2020) is officially under review.
10. Processing times: still no official turnaround for ordinary applications. Known figures: bank transfer payments take 1 to 2 business days before the application is submitted; drafts are kept 20 days; a request to the Regulatory Licensing Review Panel must be made within three months of the decision letter, acceptance is decided within 10 working days and an accepted review can take 6 weeks (all from the online form); the Ministry of Justice criminal record check can take up to 20 working days. No published list of common reasons applications are returned was found; the form only says every listed requirement must be current and evidenced.
11. Licensing: no CC BY 4.0 licence applies; see MANIFEST.md.
12. Coastguard Boating Education's website was offline on 2026-10-25; notes were taken from the Internet Archive copy of 11 July 2026 (providers/coastguard-boating-education.md). Fees and intakes may have changed.
13. Foreign recognition (MCA, AMSA and others): no new Maritime NZ position, press release, consultation, OIA reply, parliamentary question or forum report found (searched 25 Oct 2026: Maritime NZ site and consultations, fyi.org.nz, Maritime NZ OIA releases and ministerial briefing lists, NZ Parliament, industry bodies, web and forum search); the guidance remains "under review" and no source says why. The nearest explanation is background only: OIA-released Board minutes show the certification team's systems were outdated and manual and a certification transformation project was paused in 2022 (providers/oia-board-minutes-and-briefings-certification.md). Related policy that was announced: RAP 2024 proposed a limited pathway (Maritime Transport Act s41(2)) for skilled foreign seafarers with domestic, non-STCW qualifications to get NZ domestic certificates, for a few high-standard countries, expected November 2025 at the earliest; no launch, guidance or country list has been published (official/rap-2024-invitation-to-comment-seacert.md). The STCW I/10 undertakings are now captured from archived MFAT records (official/mfat-treaties-stcw-i10-undertakings.md); the live MFAT site was still unreachable. MCA yacht time towards new NZ certificates is still only covered by one law firm case (providers/dawson-associates-maritime-law.md) and the rule that the Director may accept any approved sea service (P32-6); MCA yacht CoCs are recognised through the UK I/10 undertaking with an oral exam, but no source explains how each MCA yacht CoC maps in practice beyond rules 32.108 to 32.113.
14. Oral exam experience: covered by approved examiners and providers (providers/oral-examiners-voyager-marine-okiwa.md), not by any Maritime NZ statement of exam length or pass rates.
