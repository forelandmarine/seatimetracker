# Oral exam experience for NZ certificates: paraphrased fact notes

Notes in our own words from examiner and provider pages read on 2026-10-25. The official rules for the exam are in official/final-examinations-guidelines-for-candidates.md, official/web-training-and-final-examinations.md and Part 35 Subpart C; those win where anything below differs. Examiner businesses are commercial; their own claims are not independently checked.

## Q: Who runs the Maritime NZ oral exam? Is it someone at Maritime NZ?
Usually not. The final exam is run by an examiner the Director of Maritime NZ has approved for that grade of certificate. Several are private businesses. Voyager Marine Solutions (Nelson) says it was approved in June 2025 for deck grades (examiner O'Shea Butler, Master Mariner) and added engineering grades in October 2026 (examiner Jack Paoli, Chief Engineer). Okiwa says its examiner Katherine Walker is approved for final oral exams for a wide range of deck, fishing and yacht grades and for STCW I/10 recognition exams.
Note: official guidance says the exam and examiner are normally arranged through your training provider; otherwise email seafarers@maritimenz.govt.nz.
Sources: https://www.voyager-marine.com/post/voyager-marine-accredited-to-conduct-oral-examinations ; https://www.voyager-marine.com/post/voyager-marine-expands-into-engineering-oral-examinations ; https://www.okiwa.co.nz/what-we-do.html

## Q: Which certificates can these examiners test?
- Voyager Marine (deck, from June 2025): SRL (nautical), SRL <500 GT endorsement, Skipper Coastal/Offshore, QDC, Deck Watch Rating, AB Deck, Watchkeeper Deck <500 GT Near Coastal, Master <500 GT Near Coastal, Master <500 GT, Watchkeeper Deck, Chief Mate <3000 GT, Master <3000 GT, Chief Mate, Master Yacht <24 m, Chief Mate Yacht, Master Yacht <500 GT, Master Yacht. (Engineering, from October 2026): EWR, AB Engine, MEC 3, MEC 4, MEC 5, MEC 6.
- Okiwa: SRL (nautical and engineering), SRL <500 GT, SCO, DWR, AB Deck, Watchkeeper Deck <500 GT NC, Master <500 GT NC and unlimited, Watchkeeper Deck, Master <3000 GT, Chief Mate, Master, QDC, all four fishing officer grades, ADH-F, Master Yacht <24 m, Master Yacht, a "Mate Yacht" grade, foreign fishing vessel certificates and STCW I/10 recognition.
Sources: https://www.voyager-marine.com/post/voyager-marine-accredited-to-conduct-oral-examinations ; https://www.okiwa.co.nz/what-we-do.html

## Q: Can I sit my oral exam online, or from overseas?
Yes with these examiners. Voyager Marine says most of its exams are done online (by video link), so you can sit them from home, on board or while travelling, without waiting until you are back in New Zealand, and dates fit around your roster. Okiwa says all its Maritime NZ exams can be done by video conference, or face to face at your workplace, on board or at an agreed place.
Sources: https://www.voyager-marine.com/maritime-exams ; https://www.okiwa.co.nz/what-we-do.html

## Q: How long is the exam and what does it cost?
Voyager Marine lists its deck oral exam at 3 hours, from NZD 165 depending on the grade (an earlier 2025 post showed 180); if a training provider books it, Voyager invoices the provider. Cancel or rebook at least 24 hours ahead. Okiwa does not publish fees or length. SIS Training includes the Maritime NZ exam fee in its SRL course price (for example NZD 1,874 + GST for the Picton intake starting 23 November 2026) and holds the oral exam at the end of its 4-week block course.
Note: the official guidance says you pay the examiner directly and should agree the amount beforehand. There is no fixed national exam fee; the NZD 368 Maritime NZ fee is for the certificate application, not the exam.
Sources: https://www.voyager-marine.com/service-page-1/maritime-oral-exam ; https://sistraining.co.nz/maritime/srl-course/

## Q: Can the same company coach me and then examine me?
Voyager Marine says no: if you used its practice orals or mentoring, it will not examine you, but it will help you find another examiner. It sells practice (mock) orals separately (NZD 115 deck, NZD 150 engineering), also usable for AMSA or MCA orals.
Source: https://www.voyager-marine.com/maritime-exams

## Q: What is the exam like and how should I prepare?
Voyager Marine describes it as a one-to-one professional conversation in which the examiner tests whether you understand why procedures exist and can apply them, not whether you can recite them. Its tips: study little and often and test yourself rather than re-reading, practise answering out loud, explain topics to someone else, pause and plan before answering, give a short direct answer first and then expand, ask the examiner to clarify a question if unsure, and dress and behave professionally.
Note: the official guidance adds that you must send your TRB, assignments and training results to the examiner at least 10 days before, bring photo ID (passport or NZ driver licence), the exam is in English, you are told the result at the end, and if you fail you must wait at least three weeks before a resit (you may choose a different examiner). If you are well below standard the examiner can require up to six months' extra sea service or training first.
Source: https://www.voyager-marine.com/post/mastering-a-maritime-oral-exam-techniques-for-success

## Q: Can someone handle my Maritime NZ application for me while I am at sea?
Voyager Marine offers paid help with sea time calculation, preparing forms and documents, foreign certificate recognition, and acting as your agent with Maritime NZ.
Note: to use an agent you must give them an Authority to Act (paper form MSF122 or the digital authority in the online form); see official/online-application-form-requirements-2026.md.
Source: https://www.voyager-marine.com/seafarer-licencing
