# New Zealand Maritime School (MIT): paraphrased fact notes

These notes are written in our own words from MIT programme pages read on 2026-10-25. They are not MIT's text. Maritime NZ lists the New Zealand Maritime School (MIT and Unitec) as an approved training provider. The binding requirements are in Maritime Rules Part 32 and the Maritime NZ guidelines (see official/). Fees are MIT's approximate figures and change yearly.

## Q: Does finishing an MIT course give me the Maritime NZ certificate?
No. Every MIT maritime page says the course is only one part. To get the certificate you still need the sea service, a Maritime NZ medical and eyesight test (including colour vision for deck), a fit and proper person check, any ancillary certificates and, for most certificates, a final oral exam with a Maritime NZ examiner. Entry to most courses is open, and MIT stresses that finishing the course does not guarantee a licence.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-domestic-maritime-operations-restricted-limits-level-4/

## Q: What is the yacht route to Chief Mate Yacht and Master Yacht in New Zealand?
MIT runs the only listed course for these certificates: the New Zealand Diploma in Yacht Operations (Level 6). The Chief Mate Yacht part is 15 courses (120 credits), a 28-week block with about 23 weeks in class, starting in March. The optional Master Yacht strand adds five courses (80 credits), about 15 weeks with 9 in class, with starts in February, May and August. MIT quotes about NZD 10,600 domestic for 2026 and about 9,500 for 2027. After the diploma you apply to Maritime NZ for Chief Mate Yacht; after the Master strand you apply for Master Yacht or Master Yacht <500 GT. MIT aims it at people already working on superyachts over 24 m and up to 3000 GT.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-diploma-in-yacht-operations-level-6-with-optional-strand-in-master-yacht/

## Q: Do I need all my yacht sea time before starting the yacht diploma?
MIT says the diploma is meant for people who have done their sea service as set out in Maritime NZ guidance and completed the Chief Mate Yacht Training Record Book, but it accepts that many yacht crew study over two or three years around their rotations, so not all sea service has to be finished before the first courses. Some courses can be done at a distance, but not the whole qualification.
Note: Maritime NZ's training record book page (June 2026) does not list a Maritime NZ Chief Mate Yacht TRB; the Maritime NZ Chief Mate Yacht guideline says the TRB is available from your training provider.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-diploma-in-yacht-operations-level-6-with-optional-strand-in-master-yacht/

## Q: I hold a UK (MCA) Chief Mate Yacht ticket. Can I go straight to the Master Yacht strand?
MIT says holders of the UK Chief Mate Yacht can move into the Master Yacht strand, and that their Chief Mate Yacht certificate is counted as prior learning when they apply. You still need to meet Maritime NZ's sea service and other requirements for the Master Yacht certificate.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-diploma-in-yacht-operations-level-6-with-optional-strand-in-master-yacht/

## Q: What does the yacht diploma cover?
Chief Mate Yacht courses include the STCW basic safety courses, security awareness, survival craft (PISC), medical first aid, advanced firefighting, GMDSS, and yacht-specific papers in navigation and passage planning, celestial navigation, electronic navigation aids, stability, simulator-based watchkeeping and a general knowledge and orals preparation paper. The Master strand adds business and law, meteorology and seamanship and other command papers.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-diploma-in-yacht-operations-level-6-with-optional-strand-in-master-yacht/

## Q: How do I start on superyachts from scratch in New Zealand?
MIT runs a 12-week, full-time Level 3 Superyacht Crewing certificate with one week at sea. It covers STCW basic safety and security awareness, RYA Powerboat Level 2 and PWC, IAMI GUEST interior and service certificates, and basic deck, engine and watchkeeping skills. It does not lead to a Maritime NZ certificate by itself, and MIT notes yachts usually want crew aged 18 or over. To use it internationally you also need a Part 34 medical.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-maritime-crewing-level-3-in-superyacht-crewing/

## Q: How do I get Qualified Deck Crew (QDC)?
MIT's Level 3 Domestic Maritime Crewing certificate (20 weeks part-time, free for eligible domestic students) prepares you for QDC while you work on a vessel and complete the Maritime NZ Training Record Book. MIT lists the ancillaries as crowd control and STCW elementary first aid, and the course includes the marine radio (MRROC) exam.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: Part 32 Table 2 lists first aid and RROC as the QDC ancillaries; crowd control is not in the QDC table, though it may be needed for a passenger endorsement. MIT also lists a "final exam" for QDC, which matches the Maritime NZ QDC guideline.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-domestic-maritime-crewing-level-3/

## Q: How do I get Skipper Restricted Limits (SRL) through MIT?
The Level 4 Domestic Maritime Operations (Restricted Limits) certificate runs 26 teaching weeks (22 online, 4 on site), with starts in March, May, September and December, about NZD 1,900 domestic in 2026. You need access to a suitable vessel to complete the Maritime NZ SRL Training Record Book, and it suits deck crew or experienced recreational skippers.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-domestic-maritime-operations-restricted-limits-level-4/

## Q: How do I go from SRL to Skipper Coastal/Offshore (SCO) at MIT?
MIT lists a Level 5 Skipper Coastal and Offshore certificate (17-week block, March start, about NZD 6,200).
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL / PAGE ERROR: on 2026-10-25 the "leads to" section of MIT's SCO page described Chief Mate and Master (foreign-going) certificates, copied from another programme. The official SCO requirements (Part 32 Table 4) are: hold SRL endorsed to 24 m, then 12 months sea service on ships of 12 m or more while holding that certificate, plus training, exam and ancillaries (first aid, firefighting, survival craft, GROC). Maritime NZ's approved training page also lists the SCO course as Level 4, not Level 5.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-domestic-maritime-operations-coastal-and-offshore-level-5/

## Q: Which MIT course leads to Watchkeeper Deck <500 GT Near Coastal or Mate Fishing Vessel Limited?
The Level 4 Maritime Operations certificate (28 weeks, 18 in class, March start, about NZD 8,000 in 2026) has a watchkeeping strand (Watchkeeper Deck <500 GT NC) and a fishing strand (Mate Fishing Vessel Limited, described as mate on 24 to 45 m vessels in coastal and offshore limits). You can do both strands.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-maritime-operations-with-strands-in-fishing-and-watchkeeping-level-4/

## Q: And for Master <500 GT Near Coastal or Skipper Fishing Vessel Limited?
The Level 5 Maritime Operations certificate (17 weeks, 15 in class, March and May starts, about NZD 4,300). MIT recommends you already hold Watchkeeper Deck <500 GT NC, Mate Fishing Vessel Limited, SCO or equivalent.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-maritime-operations-level-5/

## Q: How long does the deck rating route to AB Deck take?
MIT's Level 4 Maritime Crewing (Deck Crew strand) is 17 weeks full time, January start. MIT says you then need two months of sea time and the Training Record Book to apply for Deck Watch Rating, and a further 12 months as a DWR for Able Seafarer Deck. A steering certificate is also listed.
Note: Part 32 sets these by reference to STCW regulations II/4 and II/5; since 31 March 2026 a longer sea-service-only route without the course is also allowed (Maritime Rules Amendments 2025 (No 3)).
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-maritime-crewing-level-4-with-a-strand-in-deck-crew/

## Q: And for engine ratings?
The Level 4 Maritime Crewing (Engineering strand), 17 weeks from January, leads to Engine Room Watch Rating after two months' sea time and the TRB, then Able Seafarer Engine after a further six months as a certified EWR.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-certificate-in-maritime-crewing-level-4-with-a-strand-in-engineering/

## Q: How do I become a deck officer (Watchkeeper Deck, the "second mate's ticket")?
The New Zealand Diploma in Nautical Science (Level 6) runs about 2.5 years including 12 months (360 days) at sea, June start. MIT describes two sea time routes for the Watchkeeper Deck application: 12 months of sea time with the approved Training Record Book, or, for people already in the industry, at least 36 months on a qualifying ship including at least 6 months in the navigation team. New trainees first do the 17-week Level 4 Deck Crew course.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-diploma-in-nautical-science-level-6/

## Q: What sea time do I need after Watchkeeper Deck for Chief Mate and Master?
MIT says Chief Mate needs the Level 7 Diploma in Foreign-going Master or Chief plus at least 12 months' sea time as a Watchkeeper Deck, and Master needs at least 36 months as a Watchkeeper Deck.
Note: Part 32 refers to STCW regulation II/2 for both; STCW also allows Master with 24 months if at least 12 months were served as chief mate, which MIT's page does not mention. Check the Maritime NZ Master guideline.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/diploma-in-foreign-going-master-or-chief-level-7-also-known-as-chief-mate-or-master/

## Q: What is the engineering route at MIT?
- MEC 6: Level 4 certificate, 12 weeks (2 in class) plus the Training Record Book, March start, about NZD 3,400. Applicants for the MEC 6 certificate must be 18.
- MEC 5: Level 5 certificate, 14 weeks (5 in class), about NZD 3,900.
- MEC 4 and MEC 3: New Zealand Diploma in Marine Engineering (Level 6), about 30 months full time including 9 months at sea. MIT gives two sea time routes: 12 months with the approved TRB, or at least 36 months as an Engine (Room) Watch Rating for people already in industry.
- MEC 2 and MEC 1: Level 7 Diploma in Foreign-going Master or Chief (Chief Engineering strand); MEC 1 is a further 12-week strand.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-diploma-in-marine-engineering-level-6/

## Q: Can I get credit for previous study or experience?
MIT offers recognition of prior learning against its courses. For sea time recognised through MIT's RPKS process on the nautical and engineering diplomas, MIT charges a fee per credit (stated as NZD 36 per credit). Whether sea service counts for the Maritime NZ certificate is decided by Maritime NZ, not the school.
Source: https://www.manukau.ac.nz/programme/new-zealand-diploma-in-nautical-science-level-6/
