# Official information on certification capacity: notes

Reliability: these are genuine Maritime NZ records, but they are background about workload and plans, not statements of certification rules. They help explain why guidance is old, "under review" or missing. They do not say why the recognition guidance specifically is under review.

## Q: Why is so much Maritime NZ certification guidance out of date or "under review"?
No record states the reason for any specific document. The Board minutes released under the OIA show the certification team was under sustained pressure from 2022. At a Board meeting in about May 2022, management said the "certification transformation project" (meant to look at all certification processes because "a lot of the systems are out-of-date and manual") had been paused because of staff turnover; part of it moved into the Te Korowai programme, the timing of the next phase was unknown, and the priority was to stabilise the Certification Team. Staff were seconded from elsewhere in Maritime NZ to work through the backlog and some quick process changes were made. At a late-2022 meeting the Regulatory Licensing team (formerly the Certification team) presented to the Board, described as a team "under pressure with a heavy workload ultimately impacting on delivery".
Source: https://fyi.org.nz/request/24688/response/97563/attach/2/Decision%20letter%20to%20John%20Luke%20F33866with%20documents.pdf

## Q: What did Maritime NZ tell the incoming Minister in 2023 about seafarer certification?
The 2023 Briefing to the Incoming Minister said its funding review proposals included investment to lock in improvements to seafarer certification systems, that it was developing rule changes to simplify seafarer certification processes, and that it was working with cross-sector groups and training organisations on rule and system changes to make it easier to enter the industry and give seafarers more flexibility in their careers. It also flagged maritime workforce shortages and immigration settings. Our reading (not stated in the briefing) is that these plans became the 2024 to 2026 rule packages (RAP 2024, RAP 2.0 and RAP 3) and the April 2026 online application form.
Source: https://www.maritimenz.govt.nz/media/rxxjkoyk/briefing-for-the-incoming-minister-2023.pdf

## Q: Has Maritime NZ briefed the Minister about foreign certificate recognition, S2NM or competency frameworks?
Not by any published title. The ministerial briefings lists for June 2024 to June 2026 include "Maritime Rules Amendments 2025 (No 3)" (December 2025), "Maritime Legislation Review: first package of policy decisions" (31 March 2026), a joint briefing for the select committee Inquiry into Ports and the Maritime Sector (July 2025), and meetings with the NZ Marine Transport Association, but nothing titled on seafarer certification, recognition, S2NM or frameworks. Some titles are withheld. Copies can be requested under the OIA from ministerialservices@maritimenz.govt.nz.
Source: https://www.maritimenz.govt.nz/about-us/our-strategy-and-performance/briefings-list/

No. All 39 requests to Maritime NZ listed on fyi.org.nz (checked 25 October 2026) are about other topics (incidents, levies, staffing, Board minutes, emissions, COVID-era seafarer travel). Maritime NZ's own OIA responses page (updated 17 August 2026) lists only incident, response-capability and RMS Niagara releases.
