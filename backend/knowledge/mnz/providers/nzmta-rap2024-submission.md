# NZMTA submission on RAP 2024: paraphrased notes

Notes in our own words. The NZMTA represents domestic commercial vessel operators and consulted its members before submitting. Reliability: a reliable record of what operators asked for in late 2024, but it is an industry opinion, not guidance. The Maritime NZ proposals it responds to are summarised in official/rap-2024-invitation-to-comment-seacert.md.

## Q: Does industry support a pathway for foreign seafarers into NZ domestic certificates?
NZMTA reported general support for a limited pathway focused on seafarers from countries with well regarded maritime administrations, naming the UK MCA as an example. Members suggested a bridging course on New Zealand law, rules and incident reporting, plus a Maritime NZ written or oral exam to find gaps. Some wanted the training record book (TRB) dropped for highly experienced foreign seafarers, as in Australia where a TRB is only needed by people with little commercial sea time. Examiners had seen candidates with little command experience try for the SRL 500 GT endorsement, so members stressed that foreign applicants must be competent in practical command.
Source: https://www.marinetransport.co.nz/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/NZMTA-Omnibus-Rule-Submission-Final.pdf

## Q: Did operators think S2NM was a good idea, and at what size?
Yes for vessels up to 6 m: an accessible standard qualification that would cut the number of one-off Part 35 training approvals. Members warned that 12 m would overlap SRL and suggested 7.5 m or 9 m if it was ever extended. They asked that anyone moving up from S2NM to SRL still complete the SRL task book rather than rely on recognition of prior learning, and said S2NM's implementation needed to be clearly defined as part of a wider SeaCert review or it would be misunderstood like QDC.
Source: https://www.marinetransport.co.nz/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/NZMTA-Omnibus-Rule-Submission-Final.pdf

## Q: What did operators say about QDC?
Members thought Maritime NZ's proposed cuts to the QDC task book went too far, supported dropping some tasks (rope types, splicing, cleaning), wanted buoyage, compass use, gangway rigging and lookout duties kept, and asked for simpler QDC medicals. They also said they had not seen a shortage of QDC holders.
Source: https://www.marinetransport.co.nz/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/NZMTA-Omnibus-Rule-Submission-Final.pdf

## Q: Are there skill shortages?
NZMTA said there are shortages at all levels, especially MEC 2 and MEC 3 engineers, and hoped for easier movement of seafarers between New Zealand and Australia.
