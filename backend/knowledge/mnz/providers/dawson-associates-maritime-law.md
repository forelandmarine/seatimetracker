# Dawson & Associates: paraphrased fact notes

Notes in our own words from an article dated 23 September 2021 by a New Zealand maritime law firm describing one client's Master Yacht application. It is a single case study, not a Maritime NZ policy statement, and it predates the 2024 guidelines and the 2025 to 2026 rule changes.

## Q: Does sea time on a private (non-commercial) superyacht count for NZ yacht certificates?
In the firm's case, Maritime NZ wanted proof that the private superyacht was run to a commercial standard. The yacht's flag registry issued a Statement of Compliance showing it met the relevant yacht code, and Maritime NZ then accepted the time as equivalent to commercial service. This matches the current Maritime NZ Chief Mate Yacht and Master Yacht guidelines, which accept private yacht time if you show the yacht runs to commercial standards (for example a commercial registration certificate, ISM certificate, safety management certification, a management company's document of compliance with a statement that an ISM-type system is used, or a yacht engaged in trade certificate).
Source: https://www.maritimelaw.co.nz/insight/what-happens-when-your-seatime-does-not-fit-a-conventional-path

## Q: I hold UK (MCA) yacht certificates. Which one does Maritime NZ treat as equal to NZ Chief Mate Yacht when counting sea time?
In this case Maritime NZ accepted the UK MCA Chief Mate (Yacht) <3000 GT certificate as equivalent to NZ Chief Mate Yacht, so time served while holding the MCA OOW (Yacht) <3000 GT, plus the ancillaries, was argued and accepted as equivalent. The firm notes the MCA route from OOW to Chief Mate needs no extra sea time, only the RYA Yachtmaster Ocean and ancillaries.
Note: Part 32 Tables 26 and 27 count Master Yacht sea service "while holding a current certificate as Chief mate yacht, or equivalent". The MCA rules have since changed (MSN 1858 Amendment 2, May 2026, see the MCA library), so check current MCA terminology.
Source: https://www.maritimelaw.co.nz/insight/what-happens-when-your-seatime-does-not-fit-a-conventional-path

## Q: What is the general lesson if my sea time doesn't fit the rule exactly?
The firm's view is that it may still be possible to show your experience is equivalent, and that a well organised application that maps your evidence to each criterion in the rules makes approval easier. This is consistent with Part 32, which lets the Director accept "equivalent" certificates and weigh sea service against the criteria in rules 32.173, 32.174 and 32.181.
Source: https://www.maritimelaw.co.nz/insight/what-happens-when-your-seatime-does-not-fit-a-conventional-path

## Q: What does the NZ Master Yacht certificate cover?
The article describes it as STCW-compliant and for superyachts under 3000 GT, carrying no cargo and no more than 12 passengers, but says it applies to vessels "under 24 metres length".
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: Part 32 rule 32.113 gives Master Yacht the duties of master on a superyacht in any operating area. Superyachts are yachts of 24 m or more; yachts under 24 m are covered by the separate national Master Yacht <24 m certificate (rule 32.47). The "under 24 metres" phrase in the article appears to be an error.
