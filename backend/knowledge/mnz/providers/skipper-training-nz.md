# Skipper Training NZ: paraphrased fact notes

Notes in our own words from Skipper Training NZ pages read on 2026-10-25. Skipper Training NZ is a Maritime NZ approved training provider (SRL, MEC 5, MEC 6, MFV Limited and Unlimited, SFV-U, DWR, Watchkeeper Deck <500 GT NC).

## Q: Do I have to do a course to get Qualified Deck Crew (QDC)?
No. Skipper Training NZ states plainly that no course is needed for QDC. You need the completed QDC Training Record Book, the ancillary certificates, the Maritime NZ final (oral) exam, fit and proper person clearance, and a medical and eyesight test, and you must be 16 or older. It offers optional help (about 50 hours of training while you complete the TRB, including sea survival, extinguisher use and MRROC) mainly for employers. It also says QDC is recognised by AMSA in Australia as General Purpose Hand.
Note: Maritime NZ's own conversion table recognises Australian GPH as QDC; we did not find an AMSA source confirming the reverse.
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/qualified-deck-crew/

## Q: My skipper won't have time to sign off my SRL training record book. What can I do?
Skipper Training NZ says the TRB sign-off is a common hurdle and its SRL course is built so you complete and get the TRB signed off with a tutor during the course, including on its own training vessel. It runs a 5-week Nelson block course with a guided distance phase, and a "Hybrid" option with home study and about one practical week (8 days if you do first aid locally). Both are funded and student-loan eligible.
Note: you still need the 200 hours of sea service required by Part 32 Table 3; the course does not replace it.
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/skippers-restricted-limits-srl/

## Q: What does SRL let me do?
Skipper Training NZ describes SRL as the entry-level command certificate with engineering components, for commercial vessels up to 12 m, or up to 24 m or 500 GT with endorsements, in restricted limits.
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/skippers-restricted-limits-srl/

## Q: How do I move from SRL to Skipper Coastal/Offshore (SCO)?
You need SRL endorsed to 24 m (or equivalent), proof of the minimum sea service, current ancillaries, and to meet fit and proper person and medical standards. Skipper Training NZ says the SCO course takes 4 to 5 weeks with recognition of prior learning, or about six months without, in Nelson or remotely, and prepares you for the Maritime NZ SCO exam.
Note: the official sea service is 12 months on ships of 12 m or more while holding SRL endorsed to 24 m (Part 32 Table 4).
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/skipper-coastal-offshore/

## Q: How do I get the SRL 500 GT endorsement?
Skipper Training NZ says if you have at least 12 months' sea service on commercial vessels over 12 m while holding the SRL 24 m endorsement, and complete the TRB, you may be eligible. It runs a 1.5-day workshop (not NZQA accredited) to finish the TRB with tutors, followed by the Maritime NZ oral exam, and says the vessel manoeuvring tasks must be done on a vessel over 24 m before you attend.
Note: Part 32 rule 32.62 (as amended to 21 Sept 2026) allows either 12 months within the last 5 years on ships of 12 m or more while holding SRL 24 m, or 18 months in command of commercial ships of 12 m or more, in both cases with the approved TRB, plus any exam.
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/500-ton-endorsement-workshop/

## Q: I have an overseas skipper ticket that NZ won't recognise. Is there a faster route?
Skipper Training NZ offers recognition of prior learning (RPL) against the Maritime NZ SRL competency framework, and a 4-week "Gap" course to complete the TRB on its vessels and prepare for the SRL oral. It warns RPL is not a shortcut for people new to the industry, and is dearer because it is not government funded. You still need to meet Maritime NZ's other requirements, including medical, eyesight and fit and proper person.
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/recognition-of-prior-learning/

## Q: What does MEC 6 need and allow?
Skipper Training NZ lists 200 hours of relevant sea service, age 18, first aid, medical and fit and proper person. It summarises MEC 6 privileges as engineer on fishing, passenger and non-passenger ships up to 3000 kW in enclosed waters, passenger ships up to 750 kW inshore, non-passenger ships under 24 m coastal, fishing vessels under 24 m offshore, and second engineer on fishing ships under 750 kW unlimited, all with restrictions, and tells readers to check Maritime NZ for the full detail.
Note: Part 32 Table 6 also requires the approved MEC 6 Training Record Book completed on board within the 5 years before applying. Check rule 32.49 for the exact privileges.
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/marine-engineer-class-6-mec-6/

## Q: Can I study for Watchkeeper Deck <500 GT or Mate Fishing Vessel without leaving my job?
Skipper Training NZ runs the Level 4 Maritime Operations (fishing and watchkeeping strands) and the Level 6 Diploma in Fishing Vessel Operations (MFV Unlimited) as modular, self-paced programmes with tutor support and scheduled classroom time between trips.
Source: https://www.skippertraining.ac.nz/watchkeeper-deck-and-mate-fishing-vessel-limited/
