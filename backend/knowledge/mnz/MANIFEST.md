# Maritime NZ knowledge library manifest

Built 2026-10-25 for the SeaTime Tracker 'Explain training pathways' assistant. Every official file has YAML frontmatter (title, authority, doc_ref, url, attachment_urls, version_date, licence, licence_note, retrieved, currency_note).

- `official/`: New Zealand Government material from maritimenz.govt.nz (and two Internet Archive copies of pages removed from it). Web pages were captured as text; PDFs were downloaded and converted with pypdf. Page headers, footers and page numbers were removed and tables are flattened to text. Scanned PDFs (competency framework Master <500 GT NC, GP medical guidance, Amendments 2025 (No 3), GE-12-20) were transcribed from the page images.
- `providers/`: paraphrased fact notes from New Zealand training providers and industry sources (third-party copyright, no verbatim copying). Where they disagree with official text they are flagged `CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL` and the rule wins.

## Currency and supersession summary

- SeaCert: Part 32 (made 27 January 2014, in force 1 April 2014) is the SeaCert framework and is still the law. The current consolidation is dated 21 September 2026 (Amendment 20). Part 31 consolidation is also 21 September 2026. Parts 34 and 35 consolidations are dated 21 April 2025 (Part 34's only later change was to its objective).
- Old (pre-SeaCert) certificates: the transition and ring-fencing windows have closed. Old certificates stopped being valid on 2 September 2017 unless the holder had chosen by 1 September 2017 to ring-fence or transition (late notice possible until 1 April 2018 for STCW transition and 31 December 2019 otherwise; certificates in Part 32 Table 46 ended 31 December 2016). Maritime NZ's Ring fencing page (15 April 2026) says ring-fencing is closed; the old Transitioning page said the transition deadline had passed (archived January 2026). Ring-fenced certificates last for life; NZOM, NZOW and NZCM holders must meet currency requirements every five years. GE-12-20 (sea service and training relief for legacy holders moving to national or STCW-F certificates) expired 16 July 2025 by its own terms, although the exemptions page still lists it.
- Parts 31A, 31B and 31C were revoked on 1 April 2014 (history stub only).
- New certificate: Skipper two nautical miles (S2NM), Part 32 rules 32.41A and 32.41B, in force 1 September 2025 and amended 21 September 2026.
- Other recent rule changes: SRL <500 GT endorsement (rule 32.62) changed 1 December 2025 and 21 September 2026; rating pathways (DWR, AB Deck, EWR, AB Engine, ETR, Integrated Rating) and the MEC 3 TRB rule changed 31 March 2026 (Amendments 2025 (No 3)), replacing expired general exemptions GE-05-20, GE-06-20 and GE-08-20.
- Process change: Maritime NZ launched an online seafarer application form and a refreshed website in April 2026. Most certificates are now applied for and renewed online; recognition of foreign certificates and TTMRA recognition still use paper forms. "Revalidation" is now called "renewal". The checklists the form shows for each certificate were captured on 25 October 2026 (official/online-application-form-requirements-2026.md); Skipper two nautical miles is not yet in the form.
- Orphaned guidance: the per-certificate "Guidance for certificate of competency/proficiency" PDFs (mostly August 2024) are still hosted but are no longer linked from the new site; the old pages that linked them were captured from the Internet Archive (January 2026). They are the most detailed official explanation of sea service evidence and application steps, but their paper-form steps predate the online form.
- Under review per Maritime NZ: recognition of non-NZ STCW and STCW-F guidelines (oral exam content may be out of date), STCW renewal guidance (January 2025), the SeaCert framework document (January 2024), and the Part 31 and Part 32 advisory circulars (none currently published).
- RAP 2024 (October 2024) is the policy origin of S2NM (planned with a new S2NM exam, start September 2025) and of a Maritime NZ "limited pathway for skilled foreign seafarers" into domestic certificates under Maritime Transport Act s41(2), expected November 2025 at the earliest; no launch of that pathway was found by 25 October 2026 (official/rap-2024-invitation-to-comment-seacert.md).
- Proposals not yet law: RAP 3 (April to May 2026) SeaCert ideas: new national certificates for coastal and offshore limits and counting STCW-F fishing sea service towards some STCW certificates.
- Seafarer medical forms were updated March 2026; the main medical guideline and GP guidance are dated May 2019.

## Not stored (and why)

- Maritime Rules Part 20 (operating limits) full text: summarised by the Operating limits page; not a certification rule.
- Tanker, polar and IGF competency frameworks and guidelines: endorsements outside the app's certificate list.
- Pilot licence and PEC material, jet boat licences, MLC pages, technical clarifications ("Having the conduct of the ship", "Status of guidance"): out of scope.
- STCW-F Amendments 2017, Maritime Rules Amendments 2025 (No 2) (scanned; its Part 32 and 34 changes are in the consolidations) and RAP 3 proposed rule text: superseded by consolidations or not law.
- Individual exemptions published monthly in the NZ Gazette (for example 2026-au3608 includes a named person's exemption from rule 32.42): personal, not general.
- The STCW and STCW-F Convention texts that Part 32 incorporates by reference (for example STCW II/1, II/2, III/1): IMO copyright; the certificate guidelines and SeaCert framework give the resulting numbers.
