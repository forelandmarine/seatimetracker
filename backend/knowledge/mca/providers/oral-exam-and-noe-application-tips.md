# Oral exams and NoE applications: paraphrased provider notes

In our own words. The MCA rules are in MIN 690 Amendment 6 (official/min-690-oral-exam-booking-noe.md) and in the application form for your CoC (MSF 4343 yacht deck, MSF 4274 merchant deck, MSF 4275 engineer, MSF 4900 small vessel engineer, MSF 4257 workboat). The MCA has not published a list of refusal reasons; these are what trainers see most often. See also providers/chieftain-sea-service-verification.md, providers/pya.md and providers/jpma-hss-oral-preparation.md.

## Q: What are the most common reasons an NoE application is delayed or sent back?
Trainers list the same few problems again and again:
- Sea service testimonials that are unsigned, unstamped, missing the vessel details, self-signed, or not verified (for yachts, by the PYA or Nautilus). MSF 4343 warns that unverified yacht testimonials can add about 160 days.
- Missing or out-of-date ancillary certificates (for example Advanced Fire Fighting, HELM, GMDSS, or STCW updating training older than five years).
- Sea time counted wrongly: watchkeeping days miscounted, service logged in the wrong capacity, yard time or standby days counted as seagoing, or not enough seagoing service in the last five years.
- Incomplete forms, or the wrong form for the route.
- Applying too late, so the NoE is not ready when you want to book.
Sadler suggests starting the NoE application 3 to 6 months before you want to sit the oral, keeping scans of everything, and checking the requirements for your exact CoC.
Source: https://www.captainbarrysadler.com/understanding-noes-your-guide-to-the-notice-of-eligibility-process/

## Q: Which form do I use for my NoE?
Sadler lists the route forms: MSF 4274 (merchant deck), MSF 4275 (engineer), MSF 4343 (yacht deck), MSF 4259 (ETO), MSF 4260 (tugs) and MSF 4345 (fishing). Small vessel engineers use MSF 4900 and Master (Workboat) candidates MSF 4257.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: in its process steps the same Sadler article says to submit form MSF 4201 for the NoE. MSF 4201 is the CoC revalidation form (official/msf-4201-apply-coc-revalidation.md), not an NoE application. Use the form for your route.
Source: https://www.captainbarrysadler.com/understanding-noes-your-guide-to-the-notice-of-eligibility-process/

## Q: How long are the NoE, the oral pass and the written exam passes valid?
Providers agree: the NoE is valid for 5 years from issue; once you pass the oral you have 3 years to get the CoC issued; and deck written (IAMI) and engineering (Qualifications Scotland) exam passes last 3 years from the exam date and must still be valid when the CoC is applied for. Bluewater adds that if a deck exam pass expires you only need to resit the exam, not the course. JPMA notes the Master (Workboat) Workboat Operations module lasts five years. For OOW Unlimited and Master (Code Vessels) <200 GT, Part B must be passed within 12 months of passing Part A (MIN 690).
Source: https://www.bluewateryachting.com/faq ; https://www.captainbarrysadler.com/understanding-noes-your-guide-to-the-notice-of-eligibility-process/

## Q: Does my ENG1 need to be in date when I apply?
Sadler says you must include an ENG1 (or accepted equivalent) and that it can be expired if it shows you were medically fit to sail. The MCA forms are stricter about issue: MSF 4343 says the medical fitness certificate must be valid when the CoC is issued. So an older ENG1 may support an NoE application, but plan to have a valid ENG1 by the time the CoC is issued, and check your form's checklist.
Source: https://www.captainbarrysadler.com/understanding-noes-your-guide-to-the-notice-of-eligibility-process/

## Q: How long is the wait to book an oral exam in 2026?
Fred's Oral Prep reported on 6 February 2026 that MCA booking replies were taking up to a month because a new booking system was being introduced (it had been expected by the end of February 2026 and was running late). It also said the MCA had gone back to a more manual booking process because frequent date changes had caused backlogs, and that you may now be offered a date up to 6 months ahead rather than about 3. Its tips: give clear date ranges when you are unavailable, keep screenshots of your booking form because no confirmation is sent at that stage, and use the same email address you will sit the exam on (changing it cancels the booking).
Note: MIN 690 says the MCA aims to process NoE applications and that bookings are made through the link in the NoE email; it does not give booking lead times. Treat the 2026 timings as one provider's experience.
Source: https://www.fredsoralprep.com/blog/how-to-book-your-online-exam

## Q: What if I cannot make my booked oral?
Fred's guide repeats the MCA rules: cancelling or moving an exam is only allowed in exceptional circumstances with written evidence (for example a doctor's letter, or an employer's letter about a schedule change) sent to onlineoralexams@mcga.gov.uk; unauthorised cancellations lose the fee, and not attending without a valid reason counts as a fail. If you have connection problems you must email the same address within 5 minutes of the start time; Fred suggests taking screenshots as evidence.
Source: https://www.fredsoralprep.com/blog/how-to-book-your-online-exam

## Q: What do examiners expect in the oral itself?
From a long-time oral prep tutor: read the NoE email and every attachment (code of conduct, Teams guidance, plotting sheet) before the day; log in at least 10 minutes early; dress formally; answer the question asked and stop, rather than reciting everything you know; and if you did not hear or understand a question, ask for it to be repeated. The first question is often about your vessel, so describe the vessel as a surveyor would want (gross tonnage, length, flag, classification society, private or commercial), not your own role or cruising grounds. For the online exam use the Teams app (not the browser), a laptop, desktop or tablet with a movable camera to show the room, a fast connection, a clear desk with only permitted stationery, and a quiet private room (not a public place).
Source: https://www.fredsoralprep.com/blog/dont-piss-off-the-examiner

## Q: How should I prepare for a Part A and Part B exam (OOW Unlimited, Master 200)?
JPMA recommends doing its 8-day preparation course before Part A, because Part A covers the whole syllabus, and sitting Part A straight after the course. Part B usually follows a few weeks later, and JPMA suggests one or two refresher days just before it. It describes Part A as 15 questions, each with four test points, in 40 minutes, and says candidates with a dyslexia diagnosis can get extra time if they send evidence to exams@mcga.gov.uk before sitting Part A.
Source: https://www.hss.ac.uk/course-details/oral-preparation/deck-oral-prep-for-yacht-workboat-mca-oral-exams/

## Q: What if I fail?
Providers advise reapplying while the material is fresh. The MCA's rules (MIN 690) set fixed waiting periods between attempts that cannot be negotiated, resit fees (£159, and £84 for third and later Part A resits), and a check that you still have 6 months' seagoing service in the last five years when you reapply. The examiner gives the result and feedback at the end of the exam and an email follows.
Source: https://www.captainbarrysadler.com/understanding-noes-your-guide-to-the-notice-of-eligibility-process/ ; https://www.fredsoralprep.com/blog/how-to-book-your-online-exam
