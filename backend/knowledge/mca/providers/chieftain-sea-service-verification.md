# Chieftain Training: application problems and oral preparation, paraphrased notes

Chieftain Training is a Southampton training centre for yacht and workboat deck officers (see also chieftain-training.md for its OOW 3000 notes). These notes cover its advice on the most common cause of NoE delay for yacht candidates, and how it runs oral preparation. Paraphrased only.

## Q: What is the most common reason a yacht deck NoE application is delayed?
Sending sea service testimonials that have not been verified. Chieftain says unverified testimonials delay an NoE by around 160 days, and advises getting them verified by Nautilus or the PYA, the two bodies the MCA authorises. You do not have to be a member, but non-members pay a fee.
POSSIBLY OUT OF DATE: the 160-day figure and the verification arrangement come from MIN 543, which expired by its own text on 28 March 2022. The current MCA form MSF 4343 still warns of delays of around 160 days for unverified yacht testimonials, so the advice is still in line with the MCA's own form.
Source: https://chieftain.training/sea-service-verification/

## Q: How long does verification take, and how do I avoid it being sent back?
Allow a couple of weeks and up to a month, so do not leave it until just before you need the NoE. Before sending testimonials to the verifier, check that: the service has been counted using the MCA definitions; the form is complete and readable; and the testimonial is not signed by you. If you were the Master, the testimonial must be signed by the management company or the owner.
Source: https://chieftain.training/sea-service-verification/

## Q: Do Master 200 (Code Vessel) candidates need sea service testimonials?
Chieftain says no testimonials are needed for Master (Code Vessels) <200 GT. This matches the MCA form MSF 4343, which adds one exception: if you are converting from a merchant OOW (Unlimited) CoC you must show 3 months' seagoing service on vessels of 15 to 24 metres or under 200 GT. Note that MSN 1858 Amendment 2 section 3.1 still requires 6 months' seagoing service while holding a Yachtmaster Offshore (or IYT Master of Yachts Limited), so keep your own record of it.
Source: https://chieftain.training/sea-service-verification/

## Q: What sea service categories does Chieftain say the MCA uses for yachts?
- Your total claimed service can never be more than the days you were signed on.
- Onboard (yacht) service: all time signed on a yacht, whatever the yacht is doing.
- Seagoing (actual sea) service: time at sea with the main engines in use for at least 4 hours in a 24-hour period. It can include time at anchor or river and canal transits that are part of a passage (for example waiting for a berth, a lock or a canal, or a departure delayed by bad weather), but a period at anchor or on a mooring can never be longer than the voyage before it. Anchoring at the end of a passage instead of going alongside counts as stand-by or onboard service, not sea service.
- Stand-by service: time straight after a voyage while preparing for the next one; at most 14 days in a row, and never more than the voyage before it.
- Shipyard service: standing by a build, refit or major repair; at most 90 days per application for OOW 3000 (all testimonials together). Routine maintenance counts as onboard service, not yard service.
- Watchkeeping service (OOW 3000 holders and above only): sea service in full charge of a navigational watch for at least 4 hours in every 24 while on a voyage.
These match the definitions in MSN 1858 Amendment 2 section 5.2.
Source: https://chieftain.training/sea-service-verification/

## Q: How does Chieftain run deck oral preparation?
One-to-one, either online by Zoom or at its Southampton centre, for Master 200 and OOW 500 (limited and unlimited), Master 500 Workboat, OOW 3000, Master 500 and 3000 Yachts, the Master Yacht celestial exam, and OOW and Master Unlimited. Most candidates start with 12 or 16 hours and some top up; three or four sessions of about 4 hours each is the pattern it recommends. Candidates are expected to have their prerequisites and sea time in place first.
Source: https://chieftain.training/mca-deck-officer-exam-preparation-oow-master-3000-500-workboat-yacht/

## Q: Which routes lead to Master 500 (Workboat)?
Chieftain lists four starting points for Master (Workboat) <500 GT: Master (Code Vessels) <200 GT, OOW (Yachts) <3000 GT, OOW (Unlimited), or a UK Class 1 fishing CoC, all with a minimum age of 18 and an ENG1. The MCA rules for this certificate are in MGN 496 and the application form is MSF 4257.
Source: https://chieftain.training/mca-deck-officer-exam-preparation-oow-master-3000-500-workboat-yacht/
