# PYA: paraphrased fact notes

These are notes written in our own words from PYA web pages. They are not PYA's text. Check the official MCA notices (MSN 1858 Amendment 2, MIN 543, MSN 1904, MIN 642) for the binding rules.

## Q: Who can verify my yacht sea service for an MCA application?
The PYA says it is authorised by both the UK MCA and Transport Malta to verify yacht sea service for crew anywhere in the world. Verified testimonials are used for Notice of Eligibility (NoE) applications and for CoC revalidation, and can also be shown to employers. (Nautilus International offers the same verification service; see MIN 543.)
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/sea-service-verification

## Q: How does the PYA testimonial process work now?
It is fully online. You fill in the testimonial on your PYA profile and send it digitally to the person who will sign it. Once they sign, it goes to the PYA Sea Service Department, which checks it against the MCA sea service definitions and may ask you for more information or evidence. After approval, SRB officers do a final check and the testimonial is marked as verified. The whole process can take up to about 25 working days, so build that into any NoE timeline.
Source: https://www.pya.org/news/top-tips-on-getting-a-valid-sea-service-testimonial

## Q: What does verification cost?
Full members get unlimited testimonial and certificate verification plus a PYA Service Record that holds all verified service in one place. Non-members pay per testimonial (stated as 50 euros each, with a minimum of two on the first submission) and receive digitally signed testimonials rather than a Service Record. Prices are as listed on the PYA page in October 2026 and may change.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/sea-service-verification

## Q: What do I need on my profile before I can start?
A passport-style photo, personal and contact details, a billing address and a certified PDF copy of your passport. Certified copies of certificates may also be asked for.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/sea-service-verification

## Q: Who can sign my testimonial?
The PYA lists the Captain, Chief Officer, Chief Engineer, Owner or Manager as acceptable signatories, and says a Master cannot sign their own testimonial: service as Master must be confirmed by the Owner or Manager.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: for deck officer CoCs, MSN 1858 Amendment 2 (section 5.4) says testimonials or certificates of service must be signed by the Master of the vessel, and for service as Master by the owner or a responsible person from the management company. It does not list the Chief Officer as a signatory. For engineers, MSN 1904 says testimonials are signed by the Chief Engineer or, if applicable, the Master. Crew should aim to have the Master sign deck testimonials.
Source: https://www.pya.org/news/top-tips-on-getting-a-valid-sea-service-testimonial

## Q: What are the common mistakes that get a testimonial sent back?
The PYA's checklist points to: wrong start and end dates, dates that overlap service already recorded on another testimonial, claiming more sea service days than the total time employed on board, and not following the MCA definitions for your department. Answer any query from the Sea Service Department quickly, as an unanswered query stalls the verification.
Source: https://www.pya.org/news/top-tips-on-getting-a-valid-sea-service-testimonial

## Q: When should I ask for a testimonial?
Before you leave the vessel, especially on seasonal or short contracts. Chasing a signature from a former captain later is much harder.
Source: https://www.pya.org/news/top-tips-on-getting-a-valid-sea-service-testimonial

## Q: Can I fill in a discharge book after I leave a yacht?
No. The PYA notes that a seaman's discharge book records joining and leaving dates and ports and cannot be filled in retrospectively, so get one when employment starts. It is issued by a maritime administration, usually the flag state or your own country.
Source: https://www.pya.org/faq

## Q: What do I need for a Yacht Rating Certificate (YRC)?
The PYA issues the YRC on behalf of the MCA. Requirements listed: age 16 or over; 6 months onboard service including 2 months at sea, all on vessels of 15 m or more load line length; the four basic STCW courses (PST or RYA Basic Sea Survival, Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting, Elementary First Aid, PSSR); a valid medical such as ENG1; and a completed Yacht Rating Training Record Book. With certain sailing qualifications (for example Day Skipper plus Competent Crew, Watch Leader plus Competent Crew, or a Yacht Rating course completion certificate) the TRB is not needed and the service drops to 2 months at sea on 15 m plus vessels. This matches the MCA MSF 4340 guidance.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/yacht-rating-certificate

## Q: How do I apply for the YRC through the PYA and what does it cost?
Send the MSF 4340 form (signed and countersigned), a certified passport copy (certified by a training school, or by your Captain or Chief Officer with their CoC number), passport photos countersigned on the back, proof of service (PYA service record, or testimonials plus a discharge book or discharge certificate, i.e. two forms of proof), STCW certificates and your medical. The PYA says not to pay through the MCA website. Fees: included for new full members in their first year; otherwise the page shows different figures for members and non-members (around 40 to 80 euros), so check the current price.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/yacht-rating-certificate

## Q: Where do I get the OOW 3000 deck Training Record Book and how long should it take?
Either download the MCA version free from gov.uk or get a bound PYA copy (free for full members, otherwise a small fee). The PYA suggests allowing about a year to complete all tasks, with the captain signing each task as you go. The completed TRB goes in with the NoE application and is shown to the examiner at the oral. Note that MSN 1858 lets you skip the TRB if you can show 36 months' actual seagoing service on yachts of 24 m or more load line length.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/oow-3000-deck-training-record-book

## Q: What do I need to start working on yachts at all?
The PYA FAQ lists STCW Basic Training, Security Awareness and a valid seafarer medical (ENG1 or an accepted equivalent) as the entry-level minimum, with role-specific extras such as Powerboat Level 2 for tender driving or AEC 1 for engineering. Keep your passport, medical, training certificates, verified testimonials and employment papers together.
Source: https://www.pya.org/faq

## Q: Should I have a written employment contract?
Yes. Crew on commercial seagoing yachts covered by the Maritime Labour Convention should get an individual Seafarer Employment Agreement and keep a signed copy. Private yacht crew should still ask for a clear written contract.
Source: https://www.pya.org/faq
