# Glasgow Maritime Academy: paraphrased notes

A private provider in Glasgow running MCA oral preparation online and in the classroom. These notes cover what it says about the nature of the oral and how candidates should prepare. Paraphrased only.

## Q: What makes the OOW oral hard?
The provider's view is that the subject knowledge is not the hardest part: the difficulty is applying it quickly, in the right order, out loud, while the examiner presses you with follow-up questions. The oral is often the first time a candidate has faced an examiner.
Source: https://www.glasgowmaritimeacademy.com/oow-orals/

## Q: Can I just learn a list of likely questions?
No. The examiner can ask about any part of the published MCA oral syllabus for your certificate, so preparation has to cover the whole syllabus, not a short list of sample questions. This matches MIN 690 Amendment 6, which says questions in both Part A and Part B can come from any part of the MIN 653 Amendment 1 syllabus.
Source: https://www.glasgowmaritimeacademy.com/oow-orals/

## Q: What topics come up for OOW (Unlimited) and OOW (Yachts) <3000 GT?
For OOW Unlimited the provider groups the syllabus into: passage planning and position fixing (including celestial); compasses, buoyage and meteorology; watchkeeping, Colregs (including rule-of-the-road cards and close-quarters scenarios), routeing, VTS and bridge procedures; radar, ARPA and ECDIS; cargo work and seamanship (anchoring, mooring, towing, pilot transfer, heavy weather); and safety, stability, emergencies and law. For OOW Yachts it adds emphasis on ECDIS and RCDS, sextant work, tides, manoeuvring and berthing, steering failures, IAMSAR and man overboard, GMDSS distress procedures, guest safety, MARPOL and SOPEP, M Notices, the Code of Safe Working Practices, yacht codes, ISM, ISPS, Port State Control, safe manning and hours of rest.
Source: https://www.glasgowmaritimeacademy.com/oow-orals/

## Q: How should I get ready before starting an oral prep course?
Have your sea service sorted, check your NoE position with the MCA, and have already started learning the Collision Regulations, so the course can sharpen rather than start from nothing. The provider's method is: teach each subject, then practise it the way an examiner asks (a scenario, your reasoning out loud, then follow-ups to test the answer), then a full mock oral with feedback on both content and delivery, and finally work on remaining weak areas.
Source: https://www.glasgowmaritimeacademy.com/oow-orals/

## Q: What do these courses cost and how long are they?
Listed prices (October 2026): OOW Unlimited £850 and OOW Yacht 3000 £1,400, each four weeks, live on Zoom or in a Glasgow classroom, with monthly intakes; Master 200/OOW 500 four weeks at £1,675. Course fees do not include the MCA's exam fees, which are paid separately to the MCA.
Source: https://www.glasgowmaritimeacademy.com/oow-orals/ and https://www.glasgowmaritimeacademy.com/other-orals/

## Q: Do Master 200 / OOW 500 candidates have to pass a Part A test first?
Yes, the provider says Part A must be passed before the Part B oral can be booked, which matches MIN 690 Amendment 6 (for NoEs issued from 2 April 2024; a Part A pass is valid for one year).
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: the provider describes Part A as a multiple-choice test on UK maritime legal and administrative procedures. In MIN 690, Part A assesses fundamental topics from the oral syllabus (MIN 653 Amendment 1) in a 40-minute proctored online test; the test on UK legal and administrative procedures is UKLAP 1, taken by holders of non-UK CoCs applying for a UK Flag State Endorsement (FSE), not Part A.
Source: https://www.glasgowmaritimeacademy.com/other-orals/
