# Careers at Sea (MNTB): paraphrased notes

Careers at Sea is the Merchant Navy Training Board's careers site for the UK shipping industry. It explains officer cadetships, ratings routes and sponsorship. Paraphrased notes only.

## Q: How do I become a Merchant Navy officer in the UK?
The usual route is a sponsored officer cadetship of about three years. A shipping company, training management company or charity pays the college fees and travel and gives a monthly bursary. You alternate college and sea phases (college, sea, college, sea, college) and finish with an academic award plus an MCA Certificate of Competency.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: How much sea time is in a cadetship?
At least 12 months at sea for deck cadets and roughly 8 to 9 months for engineering cadets, usually in trips of 2 to 3 months. For deck, this lines up with MSN 1856, which requires 12 months' approved seagoing service in an approved cadet programme (including 6 months of bridge watchkeeping) documented in a Training Record Book.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: What qualifications do I need to start a cadetship?
For HNC/HND routes, four GCSEs at grade 4 or above (or Scottish equivalents) including maths, English and a science. Foundation Degree or Scottish Professional Diploma routes add 48 UCAS points including maths or physics; honours degree routes need about 104 UCAS points. One-year pre-cadetship courses exist for people without these grades.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: Do I apply to the college or to a company?
To a sponsor (shipping company, training management company or charity such as Trinity House or Maritime London), not to the college. There are two intakes a year (September and January) and sponsors often recruit up to a year ahead, so you can apply before getting exam results.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: Can non-UK nationals get a sponsored cadetship?
Generally only UK citizens, and EU citizens who have lived in the UK for more than a year, are eligible for sponsorship. Others are advised to approach shipping companies directly, as many recruit globally.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: Is there an upper age limit?
The site gives no upper age limit; you need to be fit enough to pass the ENG1 seafarer medical, which you must hold before going to sea.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: I have a degree or Royal Navy experience. Can I shorten training?
A general degree is unlikely to count, though a mechanical engineering degree may help for the engineering route. Royal Navy and engineering backgrounds still need a sponsor. Ask the MCA for a Letter of Initial Assessment (form MSF 4352) to find out what counts.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: How do I start as a rating, and how long does it take?
Ratings need no minimum academic grades (some firms ask for about three GCSEs) but must pass ENG1 and complete STCW basic training. Paid apprenticeships (Seafarer deck rating, Maritime mechanical and electrical mechanic, Small Commercial Vessel Crewmember) usually last 18 to 24 months, mixing sea time and college. Apply directly to employers such as ferry, tug and RFA operators.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: Are there apprenticeship routes to officer level without a full cadetship?
The site lists apprenticeship routes to OOW 500gt (Near Coastal) and Small Vessel Chief Engineer 3000gt/9000kW. Ratings can also move up through pre-cadetship courses. Funding such as the John William Slater Memorial Fund (via the Marine Society) may pay for STCW courses or a first CoC for ratings or people with yacht, workboat or tug experience.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/

## Q: Which colleges run UK cadet training?
City of Glasgow College, Fleetwood Nautical Campus, South Shields Marine School, Warsash Maritime School (Solent University), the Scottish Maritime Academy (NESCol) and UHI Shetland.
Source: https://www.careersatsea.org/careers/
