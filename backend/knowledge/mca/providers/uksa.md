# UKSA: paraphrased fact notes

Our own summary of UKSA's Superyacht Cadetship page (Cowes, Isle of Wight). Check MSN 1858 Amendment 2 for the binding OOW (Yachts) requirements.

## Q: What does the UKSA Superyacht Cadetship lead to?
A structured route from no experience to the MCA Officer of the Watch (Yachts <3000 GT) CoC, mixing college phases at UKSA with paid work on yachts. UKSA describes the full journey as taking roughly five years.
Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship

## Q: How is the cadetship structured?
Five phases: about 22 weeks of shore-based study; 12 to 18 months of paid work at sea; a return to UKSA for OOW-related modules (including Efficient Deck Hand); another 12 to 18 months of paid work at sea; and a final 8 weeks of study to finish. The two sea phases follow the MCA Yacht Training Record Book, with the second focused on watchkeeping.
Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship

## Q: Which qualifications are gained in each phase?
First phase: RYA/MCA Yachtmaster Offshore (sail), Coastal Skipper/Yachtmaster Offshore shore-based theory, STCW basic safety, PDSD, Marine SRC radio, powerboat and PWC tickets, radar and the MCA Approved Engine Course. Middle phase: EDH, HELM (Operational) and, for some, Yachtmaster Ocean theory. Final phase: OOW (Yachts <3000 GT) CoC, plus restricted survival craft, ECDIS, GMDSS GOC, Navigation and Radar (OOW Yachts), General Ship Knowledge (OOW Yachts) and oral exam preparation.
Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship

## Q: Who can apply?
Applicants must be at least 18 at the start. UKSA looks for good English and maths (ideally GCSE grades A to C or equivalent), real commitment to a yachting career, ideally a year of work experience, an ENG1 before training starts, and success at a two-day selection (presentation plus interview). An optional Foundation Degree in Operational Yacht Science needs UCAS points, with flexibility for older applicants.
Note: MSN 1858 sets a minimum age of 19 for the OOW (Yachts <3000 GT) CoC itself, which a five-year programme naturally satisfies.
Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship

## Q: What does it cost and is there funding?
From 22,500 GBP as listed in October 2026. UKSA mentions 0% finance (subject to status) and a bursary of up to 50% of selected course fees for UK passport holders aged 18 to 25. Accommodation and meals are included while training at UKSA.
Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship

## Q: Are the sea placements paid and is a job guaranteed?
Placements are paid jobs on yachts, but UKSA says it is not a recruitment agency and cannot guarantee a placement, although it works with employers and offers careers support. The page does not state pay rates or a total sea-day figure.
Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship

Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship

## Q: Does a cadetship shortcut the MCA sea time rules?
No. A cadet still has to meet the MSN 1858 service requirements (36 months' yacht service including 365 days' seagoing service on yachts of 15 m or more, with at least 250 days of actual sea service) and complete the TRB. The programme simply organises the training and placements around those rules.
Source: https://uksa.org/professional-training/deck/superyacht-cadetship
