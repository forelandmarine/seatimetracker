# Warsash (Small Vessel engineering): paraphrased fact notes

Our own summary of Warsash material aimed at yacht and small vessel engineers. Binding rules: MSN 1904 and MIN 642. The Warsash chart is dated November 2022 (PDF metadata).

## Q: What happened to the old Y4, Y3, Y2, Y1 yacht engineer tickets?
They were replaced by the Small Vessel (SV) engineer CoCs. MSN 1904 says the old yacht engineer route (MSN 1859) closed on 31 December 2022, with no new NoEs after that. Existing yacht engineer CoCs can still be revalidated, and holders can finish training or convert to SV. Warsash notes the one-week Y4 skills test stopped being accepted from September 2022, so the two-week SV Workshop Skills Training is now needed.
Source: https://maritime.solent.ac.uk/courses/engineering-and-electro-technical/small-vessel-engineering-workshop-skills

## Q: What do I need before applying to the Experienced Seafarer route?
Per the Warsash flow chart, in order: basic STCW courses (PST, PSSR, fire prevention and fire fighting, elementary first aid, plus security awareness), AEC 1 and 2, then 24 months' onboard service including 6 months' seagoing service on vessels over 200 kW in an engineering role, with all yacht service verified by PYA or Nautilus (MIN 543). Then apply to Warsash for registration with evidence of courses and verified service.
Source: https://maritime.solent.ac.uk/documents/career-progression-charts/small-vessel-engineer-experienced-seafarer-route.pdf

## Q: Does engine room cleaning count as engineering sea service?
No. Warsash says cleaning-only time is not accepted; you need experience as an assistant engineer or engine rating or above. Dual roles such as deck/engine or ETO/engine are counted at half. MSN 1904 says the same (cleaning-only service not accepted).
Source: https://maritime.solent.ac.uk/documents/career-progression-charts/small-vessel-engineer-experienced-seafarer-route.pdf

## Q: How do I record sea time on non-yacht small vessels?
Yacht time must be verified by PYA or Nautilus; other vessel types use the methods in MIN 642 (discharge book or certificates of discharge supported by testimonials signed by the Chief Engineer or, where applicable, the Master).
Source: https://maritime.solent.ac.uk/documents/career-progression-charts/small-vessel-engineer-experienced-seafarer-route.pdf

## Q: Can I book SV Workshop Skills Training before I am registered?
Warsash says you must be registered with an approved provider and hold an LIA/TRB and registration letter to gain the SV CoC. MSN 1904 notes workshop training may be done before enrolment, but it only counts towards the seagoing service requirement after registration. The Warsash course runs two weeks of hands-on fitting, assembly and electrical maintenance.
Source: https://maritime.solent.ac.uk/courses/engineering-and-electro-technical/small-vessel-engineering-workshop-skills

## Q: Is an SV engineer CoC only useful on yachts?
No (unless it carries the yachts-only limitation). Warsash describes the SV CoC as usable on superyachts, tugs, workboats, standby, survey, research, patrol and fishing vessels, within the under 9,000 kW and under 3,000 GT limits.
Source: https://maritime.solent.ac.uk/courses/engineering-and-electro-technical/small-vessel-engineering-workshop-skills

## Q: Who issues Action Plans for engineers with degrees or HNDs?
Another provider (Hoylake Sailing School) reports that Warsash is currently the only provider issuing Alternative Route Action Plans under MSN 1904 section 4.7. Contact Warsash admissions for current details.
Source: https://www.hss.ac.uk/2025/08/06/how-can-engineers-with-previous-qualifications-apply-for-exemption-from-sv-courses-service-requirements/
