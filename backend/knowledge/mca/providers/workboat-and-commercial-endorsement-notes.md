# Workboats and commercial endorsements: paraphrased notes

These notes are in our own words. The MCA rules are in the Workboat Code Edition 3 (official/workboat-code-edition-3-crew-excerpt.md), the Sport or Pleasure Vessel Code (official/sport-or-pleasure-vessel-code-2025-manning-excerpt.md), MGN 496 (official/mgn-496-master-workboat-coc.md), MSF 4257 (official/msf-4257-apply-master-workboat-coc.md) and the ML5 form (official/msf-4112-ml5-medical-report-and-certificate.md). The RYA runs the commercial endorsement scheme itself.

## Q: Is there a single "workboat licence"?
No. Chieftain stresses that the skipper qualification depends on the vessel, its area category, the job and your role on board. For workboats under 24 m load line length the Workboat Code Edition 3 (WB3) sets the manning table; at 24 m and over the vessel normally moves into the wider Merchant Shipping and STCW system, where Certificates of Competency such as OOW or Master (Workboat) <500 GT apply. Chieftain notes WB3 came into force on 13 December 2023 for new vessels and existing coded workboats were expected to move to it by December 2026.
Source: https://chieftain.training/workboat-qualifications-wb3-a-guide-for-skippers-and-crew/

## Q: Which RYA certificates can skipper a small workboat, and how far offshore?
Chieftain's summary, when the certificate is commercially endorsed and the vessel and Code allow it: Powerboat Level 2 for very sheltered work in Category 6 only; Advanced Powerboat Certificate of Competence, or Yachtmaster Coastal, for Categories 3 to 6 (up to 20 miles from a safe haven); Yachtmaster Offshore for vessels up to 200 GT in Categories 1 to 6 (up to 150 miles); Yachtmaster Ocean worldwide on vessels up to 200 GT. Chieftain adds that the Advanced Powerboat qualification that counts is the Certificate of Competence (passed by independent exam), not just the course completion certificate. Many employers in renewables and aquaculture now ask skippers for the MCA Master (Code Vessels) <200 GT CoC instead.
Note: check the exact table for your vessel. The Workboat Code Edition 3 manning table is in official/workboat-code-edition-3-crew-excerpt.md; for sport or pleasure vessels the equivalent is Table A5.1 in official/sport-or-pleasure-vessel-code-2025-manning-excerpt.md (for example Powerboat Level 2 there also needs 12 months relevant experience and is limited to open boats and RIBs under 10 m in Category 6).
Source: https://chieftain.training/workboat-qualifications-wb3-a-guide-for-skippers-and-crew/

## Q: What do I need to get an RYA commercial endorsement?
From the RYA's page and application notes: the certificate to be endorsed (Yachtmaster Coastal, Offshore or Ocean, Advanced Powerboat, or the Powerboat Level 2 or Day Skipper course completion routes); a sea survival certificate (STCW Personal Survival Techniques or RYA Basic Sea Survival); an RYA SRC or other acceptable GMDSS radio certificate; the online Professional Practices and Responsibilities (PPR) course; a medical (ML5 or ENG1); and a passport photo. The RYA says to allow about 21 working days, and the first-time fee on its 2026 form is £49 (a paid five-day fast track is available unless the ML5 needs MCA referral). Holders of an MCA deck CoC under MSN 1856 or MSN 1858 do not need the PPR course. You must also keep the right first aid certificate for the vessel (the Codes require first aid to be refreshed every five years).
Source: https://www.rya.org.uk/training/careers/commercial-endorsements/ ; https://www.rya.org.uk/media/holh1btj/bbf_rya_application_for_commercial_endorsement.pdf

## Q: Which medical do I need for a commercial endorsement?
The RYA accepts the ML5 (MSF 4112) or an ENG1. It only accepts the latest ML5 revision (Rev 01/26); the old Rev 02/23 can be used for an endorsement only until 31 December 2026, although it stays valid until its own expiry date. Skippers working in Category 1 or 0 waters need an ENG1. If the doctor ticks any 'Yes' box on the ML5, the RYA sends it to the MCA for review and you must complete the medical review section, and the MCA will not accept an ML5 more than a year old for a referral. A restricted medical gives a restricted endorsement, and the RYA warns that ENG1 restrictions can stop you gaining STCW qualifications for vessels over 24 m. This matches the MCA's own ML5 notes.
Source: https://www.rya.org.uk/training/careers/commercial-endorsements/ ; https://www.rya.org.uk/media/holh1btj/bbf_rya_application_for_commercial_endorsement.pdf

## Q: How long does a commercial endorsement last and how do I revalidate it?
Five years from processing, as long as your ML5 or ENG1 stays in date. To revalidate, the RYA asks for proof of at least 150 days' actual sea service on appropriate vessels in the previous five years plus a valid medical; if you cannot show that, you may still be considered if you spent at least half of the five years in work closely linked to managing and operating those vessels. Holders who gained the endorsement before April 2012 must do the PPR course before their next revalidation. Bluewater adds that the RYA wants evidence of updated first aid training within the last five years at revalidation.
Source: https://www.rya.org.uk/training/careers/commercial-endorsements/ ; https://www.bluewateryachting.com/faq

## Q: Can I add an STCW endorsement to my Yachtmaster?
Yes, for Yachtmaster Offshore and Ocean holders working under non-UK administrations. You send the four STCW basic training certificates (PST, Fire Prevention and Fire Fighting, Elementary First Aid, PSSR). The RYA charges nothing extra if you apply at the same time as the commercial endorsement, otherwise £15. Chieftain reminds holders that PST and fire fighting must be refreshed every five years under STCW.
Source: https://www.rya.org.uk/media/holh1btj/bbf_rya_application_for_commercial_endorsement.pdf ; https://chieftain.training/commercial-endorsements/

## Q: What else does a WB3 skipper or crew need besides the skipper ticket?
Chieftain lists the usual extras under WB3: sea survival for crew; first aid (at least one person on many Category 2 to 6 vessels, more further offshore); fire fighting training (one person on vessels under 15 m, all crew on 15 m and over under WB3); radar and electronic chart training where that equipment is carried (the MCA Small Ships Navigation and Radar framework); an MCA-approved one-day stability course for the Master where the vessel must carry a Stability Information Booklet; a radio certificate (SRC, or the GOC further offshore); and an engineering qualification such as the Approved Engine Course where required.
Note: Chieftain's fire training rule matches Table A5.3 of the Workboat Code Edition 3 (one crew member under 15 m, all crew at 15 m and over). Do not mix this up with the 2025 Sport or Pleasure Vessel Code, which for vessels of 15 m and over asks only for the Master and at least one crew member (section 28.10.3).
Source: https://chieftain.training/workboat-qualifications-wb3-a-guide-for-skippers-and-crew/

## Q: What are the entry routes to Master (Workboat) <500 GT?
JPMA / HSS lists routes for holders of Master (Code Vessels) <200 GT, OOW Unlimited, OOW (Yachts) <3000 GT, Master (Yachts) <3000 GT and UK Class 1 Fishing CoCs. In every route you need 6 months' sea service on a workboat of 15 m or more load line length, the approved Workboat Training Record Book, the 'Workboat Operations (Master Workboats)' module and exam, and the Master Workboat oral. For example, from OOW (Yachts) <3000 GT you need 12 months' sea service since gaining it including 120 days' watchkeeping on vessels of 15 m or more (the 6 months on a workboat can sit inside that), plus the Master (Yachts) modules, HELM (Management), Advanced Fire Fighting, Medical First Aid, Medical Care and Yachtmaster Ocean. Holding only Yachtmaster Offshore instead of Ocean leads to a Master Workboat <500 GT Near Coastal certificate for UK near coastal waters. A Master (Yachts) <3000 GT holder mainly needs the 6 months' workboat service, the TRB, the Workboat Operations module and the oral. Candidates without an approved ECDIS or NARAS/NAEST course get an ECDIS limitation on the CoC.
Note: this follows MGN 496 (2013), which is still the current MCA notice (the 2025 Sport or Pleasure Vessel Code still cites MGN 496). Some cross-references in MGN 496 point to notices since replaced.
Source: https://www.hss.ac.uk/course-details/workboats-wind-farm-transfer-vessels/workboat-master-less-than-500gt/

## Q: How long do the Master (Workboat) module passes last?
JPMA / HSS says the training module passes are valid for three years, except the Workboat Operations module, which lasts five years, and the passes must still be in date when the CoC is issued. Because there are many modules, HSS suggests some candidates first take the OOW (Yachts) <3000 GT CoC so those modules no longer need to be presented within the three-year window. Bluewater gives the same three-year rule for IAMI deck exam passes.
Source: https://www.hss.ac.uk/course-details/workboats-wind-farm-transfer-vessels/workboat-master-less-than-500gt/ ; https://www.bluewateryachting.com/faq

## Q: What is the Master (Workboat) oral like and how should I prepare?
There is no separate MCA oral syllabus document for Master (Workboat) on gov.uk; MGN 496 describes the oral, and the written Workboat Operations syllabus is in official/syllabus-master-workboat-written-exam.md. JPMA / HSS runs its Master (Workboat) <500 GT oral preparation over 8 days (Monday to Thursday, a self-study Friday, then Saturday to Tuesday), the same length as its Master (Code Vessels) <200 GT course, and strongly advises finishing all written modules before the oral (see providers/jpma-hss-oral-preparation.md). JPMA recommends completing the Stability and Seamanship modules before the Workboat Operations module because that module assumes their content.
Source: https://www.hss.ac.uk/course-details/workboats-wind-farm-transfer-vessels/workboat-master-less-than-500gt/ ; https://www.hss.ac.uk/course-details/oral-preparation/deck-oral-prep-for-yacht-workboat-mca-oral-exams/
