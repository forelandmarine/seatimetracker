# Flying Fish: paraphrased fact notes

Our own summary of Flying Fish pages. Flying Fish is an MCA approved training provider and RYA centre based in Cowes. These notes cover the entry level; the officer pathway rules are in MSN 1858 Amendment 2.

## Q: What do I need before my first deckhand job?
An ENG1 seafarer medical (Flying Fish suggests getting it before booking training, in case a condition rules you out) and STCW Basic Safety Training. A Yachtmaster is not required to start. Useful extras on a CV include watersports, diving, boat handling, hospitality, engineering or trade skills.
Source: https://www.flyingfishonline.com/career-paths/begin-a-career-on-a-superyacht/become-a-superyacht-deckhand/

## Q: What is in STCW Basic Safety Training and how long is it?
About a week, made up of fire prevention and fire fighting, personal survival techniques, personal safety and social responsibilities, elementary first aid, and proficiency in security awareness. It must be taken at an approved centre (in the UK, MCA approved).
Source: https://www.flyingfishonline.com/news/what-is-stcw/

## Q: Which STCW certificates need refreshing?
Fire fighting and personal survival need updating every five years (the Manila amendment rule applied from 2017). Flying Fish says elementary first aid, PSSR and security awareness do not expire. This agrees with the MSN 1858 course table, which marks PST, fire fighting, PSCRB and advanced fire fighting as needing updates.
Source: https://www.flyingfishonline.com/news/what-is-stcw/

## Q: What is the typical progression on deck?
Flying Fish describes deckhand, then lead deckhand, then officer, then captain. Its page does not give sea time for each step; the officer stages follow MSN 1858 (OOW Yachts <3000 GT, Chief Mate, Master <500 GT and <3000 GT, and since May 2026 the Yacht Unlimited CoCs).
Source: https://www.flyingfishonline.com/career-paths/begin-a-career-on-a-superyacht/become-a-superyacht-deckhand/

## Q: How much does a pre-sea deckhand course cost?
As listed in October 2026: the Superyacht Deckhand Course is 15 days for about 3,340 GBP including STCW; a combined deck and stew course is 22 days for about 4,750 GBP.
Source: https://www.flyingfishonline.com/career-paths/begin-a-career-on-a-superyacht/become-a-superyacht-deckhand/

## Q: What do deckhands earn and how do charter tips work?
Flying Fish quotes roughly 2,000 to 3,500 euros a month (average around 2,500), usually paid in euros, depending on the yacht's crew budget. On charter yachts guests customarily tip around 10% of the charter fee, shared among crew. Living costs are low because crew live aboard.
Source: https://www.flyingfishonline.com/career-paths/begin-a-career-on-a-superyacht/become-a-superyacht-deckhand/

## Q: Where and when is the best place to look for a first job?
Antibes is the main Mediterranean hub; the Med season runs roughly March to October. Hiring peaks around February to May, with further peaks in July and August and in September to November ahead of the Caribbean season. Stay in a crew house, walk the docks, register with crew agencies and job boards, and ask working crew for referrals. Flying Fish says its graduates typically find work within one to four weeks, sometimes up to six.
Source: https://www.flyingfishonline.com/career-paths/begin-a-career-on-a-superyacht/become-a-superyacht-deckhand/

## Q: Does time as a deckhand count towards my officer ticket?
Yes, if it is deck department service on yachts and properly evidenced. For OOW (Yachts) you need 36 months' yacht service including 365 days' seagoing service on yachts of 15 m or more (MSN 1858), so start getting testimonials signed and verified by PYA or Nautilus from your first boat. (This point is drawn from MSN 1858, not the Flying Fish page.)
