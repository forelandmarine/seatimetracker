# A new route to unlimited yacht command (MCA, 24 September 2026)

Full slide text below, in slide order. Slide numbers printed on the slides are omitted.

## Slide 1
A NEW ROUTE TO UNLIMITED YACHT COMMAND
The UK Yacht Unlimited Certificate of Competency
Javed Munir
Assistant Director, Seafarers Training, Safety and Health
24 September 2026
DIGITISING SEAFARERS TRAINING AND CERTIFICATION

## Slide 2
Why change was needed
The former route created a structural break in an otherwise specialist yacht career.

THE OLD JOURNEY
1. Master (Yachts) <3,000 GT
2. OOW Unlimited Merchant CoC
3. Further training and sea service
4. Chief Mate progression
5. Further sea service to Master Unlimited
Often many years and unfeasible for experienced yacht professionals

THE NEW PRINCIPLE
Recognise yacht service as operationally appropriate.
Keep career continuity in the yacht sector while requiring the relevant education, assessment, safety training and MCA oral examination.

## Slide 3
What has been introduced
Two STCW Regulation II/2 certificates, limited to service on yachts.
- CHIEF MATE, Yachts Unlimited, Regulation II/2
- MASTER, Yachts Unlimited, Regulation II/2
ANY SIZE YACHT • WORLDWIDE
The limitation matters: These certificates do not grant Chief Mate or Master Unlimited status on cargo ships or other vessel types.

## Slide 4
The route at a glance
A yacht-relevant pathway with management-level safeguards.
1. ENTRY: Existing yacht or OOW Unlimited qualification
2. SERVICE: Verified qualifying service on yachts
3. EDUCATION: Approved management-level modules
4. ASSESSMENT: MCA written assessments and oral exam
5. ISSUE: Yacht Unlimited CoC
Core assurance: STCW Article IX alternative arrangements • Chapter VII alternative certification • Regulation II/2 management-level competence

## Slide 5
Chief Mate (Yachts) Unlimited
The principal entry route and competence package.
- ENTRY: Master (Yachts) <3,000 GT, or OOW Unlimited with specified yacht service
- EDUCATION: Nine management-level modules, unless covered by an eligible MCA-approved management qualification
- ASSESSMENT: Chief Mate Navigation and Stability assessments, valid ENG1, ancillary certificates and MCA oral exam

## Slide 6
Master (Yachts) Unlimited
Three service pathways, one management-level outcome.
- A. Chief Mate route: 12 months onboard as Chief Mate, including 6 months seagoing, all on yachts ≥500 GT
- B. Existing yacht Master: 6 months onboard as Master, including 3 months seagoing, all on vessels ≥500 GT
- C. OOW Unlimited route: 36 months onboard as OOW, including 15 months seagoing, service conditions in MSN 1858
All routes also require the applicable ancillary certificates, valid ENG1 and the Master Unlimited GT Yacht oral examination.

## Slide 7
What this means for officers and operators
A clearer talent pipeline without compromising certification integrity.
- CAREER CONTINUITY: Progress within the yacht sector without a forced cargo-vessel detour.
- RELEVANT EXPERIENCE: Yacht seagoing service is recognised as operationally appropriate.
- COMPETENCE ASSURANCE: Approved education, safety courses, written assessment and MCA oral examination remain.
- WORKFORCE PIPELINE: Operators gain a visible route to develop senior officers for yachts above 3,000 GT.

## Slide 8
Regulatory Assurance
Established STCW mechanisms, management-level competence and international transparency.
1. STCW FRAMEWORK: Article IX and Chapter VII enable equivalent training and alternative certification within a controlled framework.
2. COMPETENCE SAFEGUARDS: Regulation II/2 and section A-II/2 remain the benchmark, supported by approved training, service and MCA assessment.
3. RECOGNITION & TRANSPARENCY: The yacht-limited route was notified to IMO and circulated to STCW Parties as STCW.2/Circ.148.
Sources: STCW Article IX, Chapter VII, Regulation II/2 and Code A-II/2; MSN 1858 Amendment 2; STCW.2/Circ.148.

## Slide 9
CTM Engineering review: Emerging consensus
A sector review considered suitability for yachts of unlimited power and tonnage.
1. WORKING GROUP: Fourteen organisations joined the group established in January 2026; five provided detailed module feedback and others contributed broader views.
2. 88% RETAINED: Feedback supported keeping 88% of learning outcomes unchanged, with a broader consensus not to amend the modules.
3. MAIN ISSUE: The principal concern was Applied Thermodynamics, Module 2, outcome 3 on steam plant and turbines, equipment rarely carried on yachts.

## Slide 10
CTM Engineering review: proposed way forward
Retain the modules and protect transferability through an approved academic top-up.
1. NO MODULE CHANGE: The 28 August 2026 recommendation explained the wider drawbacks of changing a single learning outcome and proposed retaining the current modules.
2. RESPONSE DATE: Working-group members have until 30 September 2026 to raise objections or recommendations.
3. ACADEMIC TOP-UP: If no objection is received, candidates will top up to an MCA-approved marine engineering academic qualification at an MCA-approved college.

## Slide 11
THREE TAKEAWAYS
1. A genuine unlimited yacht route: Chief Mate and Master on any size yacht, worldwide.
2. No dilution of standards: STCW management-level competence, approved training and MCA assessment.
3. A partnership with industry: A route built with colleges, seafarer representatives and operators.
My ask of PYA and industry
- Promote the route accurately
- Help officers understand the service evidence required
- Continue sharing implementation feedback with MCA
