# Engineering Knowledge Written Examination Syllabuses

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/engineering-knowledge-written-examination-syllabuses

> The written examination syllabuses for Engineering Knowledge (EK).

## Change history (gov.uk)

- 2017-11-03: First published.

This guidance provides information and outlines the written exam syllabuses for Merchant Navy Engineering Officers, leading to the issue of a UK Certificate of Competency.

This is guidance to all Owners, Masters, Officers, Ratings and those concerned with maritime training. This guidance should be read with with [MSN 1857 - Amendment 1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/msn-1857-mf-amendment-1-uk-requirements-for-engineer-officers-and-engineer-operators) and replaces the syllabuses published on [UK seafarer careers: Training provision, information and examination syllabuses](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/uk-seafarer-careers-training-provision-and-information). This replacement has been made for formatting and accessibility purposes only, no content has been amended during this move.

Please send any feedback to [exams@mcga.gov.uk](mailto:exams@mcga.gov.uk).

## Attachment: Engineer Officer of the Watch (EOOW) (III/1) Engineering Knowledge (EK) Examination Syllabus

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/engineering-knowledge-written-examination-syllabuses/engineer-officer-of-the-watch-eoow-iii1-engineering-knowledge-ek-examination-syllabus

## 1\. Auxiliary equipment syllabus

#### The candidate will know the:

  1. Watchkeeping requirements for safe and efficient operation of engine room and auxiliary equipment spaces.
  2. Operation and procedures for ballast, bilge and fuel transfer systems.
  3. Operation of sewage, fresh water production, compressed air, refrigeration systems. 4. Operation of pumps and heat exchangers.
  4. Importance of the thrust bearing.
  5. Dangers of and procedures for entering enclosed spaces.
  6. Control of level, temperature, pressure.
  7. Operation of and need for centrifugal purifiers.
  8. Starting procedures for lifeboat engines and control of fall of lifeboats.
  9. Operation of centrifugal and positive displacement pumps.
  10. Pollution prevention procedures and equipment.
  11. Fire detection and fire fighting equipment and procedures.
  12. Purpose of legislation and the legislative bodies that enforce the legislation.
  13. Describe watchkeeping activities required for the safe and efficient operation of the vessel. State the procedures for accepting and handing over a watch and requirements for UMS operations. State the documents used explaining their use and importance.
  14. Describe fuel transfer procedures with reference to stability considerations.
  15. Sketch and describe sewage systems, fresh water generators including potable water treatment.
  16. Sketch and describe the operation of oil water separators, explaining their operating principles. State the MARPOL requirements, describe the associated documentation required.
  17. Sketch and describe bilge, bilge injection, ballast systems. Identify problems that may occur and state their causes.
  18. Define an enclosed space, stating the enclosed space entry procedure, explaining the dangers that may be encountered.
  19. Describe the construction of heat exchangers, stating their advantages.
  20. State the importance of the main propulsion thrust bearing, describing its operation and the importance of the oil temperature.
  21. Describe the operation of control air compressors, stating the starting and stopping sequence and the purpose of the safety devices fitted.
  22. Describe the control of systems using two step control, proportional control describing the operation of the transducer.
  23. Describe the basic refrigeration system, referring to the condition of the refrigerant at cardinal points. Diagnose faults from given symptoms, describe the charging and recovery of refrigerant.
  24. Describe the starting and maintenance of lifeboat engines. Explain how the fall of a lifeboat is controlled.
  25. Explain the principle of operation of centrifugal purifiers, including the importance of the correct gravity disc, speed, flow rate, temperature. Differentiate between purifier and clarifier. Explain the consequences of water contamination of fuel and lubricating oil.
  26. Sketch and describe a centrifugal pump, explaining its operating principle, stating possible faults and their causes. Describe priming methods.
  27. Sketch and describe positive displacement pumps, explaining why they need relief valves.
  28. State the checks to be made on the steering gear prior to leaving port.
  29. Describe the procedure for starting and stopping auxiliary engines including first start arrangement.
  30. Describe the pollution prevention procedures, spillage procedures, bunkering procedure.
  31. Describe the construction and operation of portable fire extinguishers. State their identification and placement.
  32. State the classes of Fire and the actions to be taken on discovering a fire. Describe the organisation of fire parties and emergency parties. Describe the testing of CABA.
  33. State the preparations required before operating fixed fire fighting installations. Describe the construction, operation and testing of fire detector heads.
  34. Explain the purpose and use of legislation and legislative organisations; MARPOL, M-Notice types, H&S at work, IMO, SOLAS, ISM, STCW and COSWP.



## 2\. Ship construction and electrical section syllabus

#### The candidate will know the:

  1. Maintenance of and safety requirements for working on electrical equipment.
  2. Protection of generators and distribution systems.
  3. Operation of generators in parallel.
  4. Operation and maintenance of storage batteries.
  5. Conventions and interpretation of electrical circuit diagrams.
  6. The structural components of the vessel and the open deck drainage arrangements. Effects on stability of transferring fluids within the vessel.
  7. Load line survey.
  8. State the requirements for safe working on electrical equipment, stating the procedure for treatment of electric shock. State the procedure for working near live equipment.
  9. Describe the maintenance required for electrical motors and electrical systems. Explain the cause, detection and prevention of earth faults. Differentiate between earthed and insulated neutral systems.
  10. State the requirements and procedure for paralleling of generators. Explain the consequences of incorrect paralleling and the protection devices fitted.
  11. Describe the switchboard protection devices for protection of generators and distribution system, explaining the need for discrimination.
  12. Differentiate between different types of storage batteries, stating their uses.
  13. Describe the maintenance required and the precautions to be taken.
  14. Identify items within a circuit diagram and explain possible effects of faults.
  15. Describe, with sketches, major structural parts of a ship explaining their purpose.
  16. Sketch mid-ship cross sections of standard vessels.
  17. Describe freeing ports and scuppers, explaining their purpose.
  18. Describe the testing for water tightness of doors, hatches and bulkheads.
  19. Explain the load line survey, identifying items included in the survey.



## 3\. Motor section syllabus

#### The candidate will know the:

  1. Watchkeeping requirements on main propulsion and auxiliary engines.
  2. Safe preparation and shutdown of main propulsion and auxiliary engines.
  3. Starting, stopping and control of main propulsion and auxiliary engines.
  4. Procedures for emergency situations.
  5. Operating principles of two and four stroke engines including turbochargers.
  6. Identification of faults and understand the remedial action required on main propulsion and auxiliary engines.
  7. Operation of ancillary systems for main propulsion and auxiliary machinery.
  8. Operation, control, protection and maintenance of starting air compressors and storage.
  9. Operation, control, protection and maintenance of an auxiliary boiler and feed system.
  10. Describe watchkeeping activities required to maintain operation of main propulsion and auxiliary machinery and associated systems (fuel, lubrication, cooling, air, starting). State the procedures for accepting and handing over a watch and requirements for UMS operations.
  11. Explain the importance of maintaining correct parameters, levels, clearances, lubricating oil condition.
  12. Describe the preparation of propulsion and auxiliary machinery from cold to ready for manoeuvring and shutdown to normal port conditions, explain the reasons for correct warming and cooling down.
  13. Explain starting and reversing of diesel engines and describe the requirements to operate from local, engine side control.
  14. Describe the control of temperatures, pressures, viscosity associated with main propulsion and auxiliary machinery.
  15. Explain the causes and dangers arising from scavenge fires, crankcase oil mist, economiser fires, describing their prevention. State how they are detected and the procedure to be taken should they occur.
  16. Identify faults and explain the actions required when given observations and symptoms of main propulsion plant, auxiliary machinery and associated systems. (Fuel, lubrication, cooling, air, starting).
  17. Describe the operating principles of two and four stroke diesel engines including timing diagrams and indicator cards.
  18. Describe the operating principles and explain the purpose of turbochargers.
  19. Identify and explain the function of the constructional components and running gear of diesel engines and turbochargers, describing the routine maintenance required.
  20. Sketch ancillary systems required for operation of main propulsion and auxiliary machinery; cooling water, lubricating oil, high pressure fuel, starting air, fuel oil storage and preparation, identifying the major components and describing the maintenance required.
  21. Describe the manual and automatic operation of starting air compressors. State, and explain the need for, the protection devices fitted.
  22. Identify the fittings of auxiliary boilers and explain their purpose. Describe the testing of level gauges, alarms and safety valves.
  23. Describe and explain the warming procedure for an auxiliary boiler.
  24. State the operating sequence of an automatic burner unit of an auxiliary boiler, explain the need for purging and state the checks and precautions required.
  25. Describe the feed system for an auxiliary boiler; explain the need for, and describe, boiler water tests. Explain the consequences of boiler water contamination.



## 4\. Steam section syllabus

#### The candidate will know the:

  1. Watchkeeping requirements on boilers, turbines, condensate systems and auxiliary engines.
  2. Safe preparation and shutdown of boilers and turbines and condensate system.
  3. Starting, stopping and control of main propulsion and auxiliary turbines.
  4. Procedures for emergency situations.
  5. Operation and construction of boilers, turbines and gearing.
  6. Identification of faults and understand the remedial action required on boilers, turbines and condensate systems.
  7. Operation and maintenance of the condensate and feed system.
  8. Operation of ancillary systems for boilers, main propulsion and auxiliary machinery.
  9. Operation, control, protection and maintenance of auxiliary engines.
  10. Describe watchkeeping activities required to maintain operation of main propulsion and boilers, auxiliary machinery and associated systems (fuel, lubrication, condensate, cooling, heating). State the procedures for accepting and handing over a watch and requirements for UMS operations.
  11. Explain the importance of maintaining correct parameters, levels, clearances, lubricating oil condition.
  12. Describe the preparation of propulsion and auxiliary machinery from cold to ready for manoeuvring and shutdown to normal port conditions, explain the reasons for correct warming and cooling down.
  13. Explain starting and reversing of turbines and describe the requirements to operate from local control including testing and operation of emergency trips.
  14. Describe the control of temperatures, pressures, levels associated with main propulsion, boilers and auxiliary machinery.
  15. Identify faults and explain the actions required when given observations and symptoms of main propulsion plant, auxiliary machinery and associated systems. (Fuel, lubrication, cooling, condensate, heating).
  16. Sketch ancillary systems required for operation of main propulsion and auxiliary machinery; cooling water, lubricating oil, condensate, fuel oil storage and preparation, heating, identifying the major components and describing the maintenance required.
  17. Describe the construction of boilers, main turbines and condensers.
  18. Describe gearing arrangements, explaining their purpose and faults that may occur.
  19. Describe and explain the warming procedure for a main boiler, stating the requirements for coupling boilers.
  20. Describe the maintenance of main boilers, testing of alarms and safety devices.
  21. Explain the need for, and describe, boiler water tests. Explain the consequences of boiler water contamination.
  22. State the operating sequence of an automatic burner unit of a main boiler, explain the need for purging and state the checks and precautions required.
  23. Describe the component parts of a condensate and feed system for a main boiler and turbine plant.
  24. Describe the operation and maintenance of auxiliary engines, identify faults and explain the need for safety devices.



## 5\. Control engineering syllabus

#### The candidate will know:

  1. Instruments: 
     1. Operation and application of a range of transducers suitable for measuring the following variables: temperature, flow, displacement, velocity, pressure, strain, position, level and light.
     2. Properties of transducers.
     3. Identification of suitable transducers for various control systems.
  2. Regulators: 
     1. Operation of pneumatic and electrical actuators.
     2. Positioners.
     3. Fail safe and fail set.
     4. Identification of suitable actuators for given control systems.
  3. Control Systems: 
     1. On-off control systems.
     2. Block diagram representation of open and closed loop systems.
     3. Continuous control systems.
     4. Logic gates and processes.
  4. System Diagrams: 
     1. Conventions and interpretation of systems drawn to BS1553 and BS1646.



#### The candidate will be able to:

  1. Instruments: 
     1. Explain the operation and application of transducers suitable for measuring variables from the following list: temperature, flow, displacement, velocity, pressure.
     2. Explain the following properties of a transducer: range, accuracy, repeatability, sensitivity, resolution, linearity, hysteresis.
     3. Identify a suitable transducer for a given control system.
  2. Regulators: 
     1. Describe the operation of pneumatic and electrical actuators.
     2. Explain the use and operation of positioners.
     3. Explain failure modes, giving examples for different systems.
     4. Identify a suitable transducer for a given system.
  3. Control Systems: 
     1. Describe the behaviour of an on-off type of control system and give an example of an application.
     2. Draw a block diagram of a specified closed loop control system consisting of a controller, external input (set point), error detector, error signal, actuator, regulator, manipulated variable, process, controlled variable, feedback loop, transducer and any appropriate signal conditioning devices.
     3. Draw an output/time graph showing the transient and steady state response of a closed loop system in response to a unit step input. The graph should be labelled with steady state error, time to peak, time to settle, overshoot and final value.
     4. Draw labelled graphs showing over damped, under damped, and critically damped behaviour in a closed loop system.
     5. Explain Gain/Proportional band, integral action time, and derivative action time.
     6. Choose the appropriate action and/or actions for given systems.
     7. Draw graphs to show the effects of change of P, I and D variables on system response referencing; set point/load changes, offset/steady state errors, overshoot, initial rate of change, setting time.
     8. Identify and explain the use of basic, two input logic gates, truth tables, determine the output of combination of gates.
  4. System Diagrams:

     1. Identify and explain the purpose of symbols highlighted on a typical Pipe and Instrument Diagram.

## Attachment: Management Level (III/2) Engineering Knowledge (EK) Examination Syllabus

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/engineering-knowledge-written-examination-syllabuses/management-level-iii2-engineering-knowledge-ek-examination-syllabus

## 1\. Requirements for all certificates

Candidates should have knowledge of current and emerging technologies, practises and operation techniques required for the operation and maintenance of machinery, equipment and ship structure usually in charge of the Chief Engineering officer.

Candidates are required to understand the legal and management responsibilities of the rank of Chief Engineer officer.

Candidates should be well acquainted with machinery and boiler causalities which may occur at sea and be able to state how these can be prevented or remedied.

The candidate to have knowledge of the following:

  1. Materials used in marine engineering and ship construction.
  2. Control, automation and alarm systems associated with marine equipment.
  3. Operation, testing and maintenance of auxiliary equipment and systems.
  4. Operation, testing and maintenance of electrical equipment.
  5. Operation, testing and maintenance of marine propulsion plant using steam as power.
  6. Operation, testing and maintenance of marine propulsion plant using internal combustion engines as power.
  7. Safety and security of the crew and vessel.
  8. Legal powers, responsibilities and administrative duties of a Chief Engineer.
  9. Vessel construction and maintaining seaworthiness.



The candidate will be able to:

  1. Describe the use of metals, materials, liquids, gases and vapours used in marine engineering and ship construction with reference to their physical properties.
  2. Identify cause of failure and describe failure mechanisms and testing of common materials used in marine engineering and ship construction.
  3. Describe processes to which component parts of machinery and equipment are subjected which are relevant to their operation, explaining why these processes are required.
  4. Describe the principles of operation of monitoring and measuring devices associated with marine equipment, giving examples of their use based on an analysis of their characteristics.
  5. Describe the operation, testing and maintenance of automatic control systems for engineering plant.
  6. Describe the operation, testing and maintenance of auxiliary systems, analysing possible faults and explaining their remedy, including: 
     1. propulsion transmission systems, including thrust and shaft bearings, stern tubes and propellers;
     2. pumping systems including pollution prevention equipment and systems;
     3. steering and stabilising systems, including bow thrusters and rudders;
     4. refrigeration and air conditioning systems;
     5. fresh water production, conditioning and storage systems;
     6. deck machinery and cargo handling systems;
     7. air compressors, receivers and associated equipment;
     8. auxiliary diesel engines and associated equipment.
  7. Explain the principles of the operation, testing and maintenance of electrical equipment, including: 
     1. alternators and generators, including excitation, voltage control, load sharing and protection;
     2. motors, including starting;
     3. distribution systems, their arrangement and protection of supplies;
     4. transformers;
     5. batteries.
  8. Describe methods of fault finding, analyse symptoms and suggest methods of rectification of faults in electrical systems.



## 2\. Requirements for steam certificate only

  1. Describe the configuration of marine plant using steam for propulsion.
  2. Describe the operation, testing and maintenance of marine plant using steam for propulsion, analysing possible faults and explaining their remedy, including: 
     1. steam boilers, their mountings, feed water and fuel, including preparation, systems;
     2. methods of boiler water testing and conditioning and action to be taken to maintain safe conditions;
     3. steam turbines, gearing and lubrication systems;
     4. steam distribution systems and associated equipment;
     5. control and alarm systems associated with automatic operation of marine steam plant;
     6. assessment of power, output and efficiency and action to be taken to maintain safe and efficient operation;
     7. internal combustion engines used for auxiliary and emergency sources of power.



## 3\. Requirements for motor certificate only

  1. Describe the configuration of marine plant using internal combustion engines as power plant.
  2. Describe the operation, testing and maintenance of marine plant using internal combustion engines as power plant, analysing possible faults and explaining their remedy, including: 
     1. marine internal combustion engines (medium speed trunk and crosshead types), gearing systems and clutches;
     2. starting and reversing systems;
     3. cooling, lubrication, fuel and fuel preparation systems;
     4. methods of testing fuel oil, lubricating oil and cooling water and action to be taken to maintain safe conditions;
     5. control and alarm systems associated with automatic operation of marine internal combustion plant;
     6. methods of assessment of power output and internal combustion plant efficiency, and action to be taken to maintain safe and efficient operation of plant;
     7. auxiliary steam boilers and associated equipment.
  3. Ensure the safety and security of the crew and vessel, including: 
     1. describe information issued by relevant bodies, including IMO, UK MCGA, MAIB, with respect to safety at sea;
     2. explain precautions against fires or explosions, explosive mixtures and sources of ignition;
     3. explain principles and methods of fire prevention and extinction in all areas of a ship;
     4. explain the principles of the operation, and describe the testing and maintenance of, fire detection and extinguishing systems including pumps and associated pumping systems;
     5. describe the control and organisation of fire and damage control parties;
     6. explain the principles involved with, and the operation, testing and maintenance of, plant and equipment specifically for use with dangerous substances as cargo;
     7. explain the organisation and control procedures necessary for the safe and efficient operation in the UMS mode;
     8. describe the constructional details of ships and explain the stresses that the hull is subjected to and the methods of resisting these stresses, fire protection and flood protection;
     9. the source of and problems associated with vibration and noise;
     10. describe the requirements of the International Convention on Load Lines and maintaining conditions of assignment.
  4. Explain the legal and administrative responsibilities of a Chief Engineer, including: 
     1. organisation and training of staff for normal and emergency duties;
     2. organisation of temporary and permanent repairs and surveys;
     3. ensuring ship is in seaworthy condition prior to sailing taking into account nature of voyage;
     4. compliance with international legislation, class and port state requirements, including consequences of non-compliance;
     5. ensuring sufficient consumables are available prior to sailing taking into account necessary reserves;
     6. preparing for, undertaking and securing Bunkering operations taking into account present legislation, statutory duties and protest procedures;
     7. preparing for, undertaking and leaving dry dock.
