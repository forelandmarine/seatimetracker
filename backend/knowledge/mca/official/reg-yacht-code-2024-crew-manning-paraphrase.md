# REG Yacht Code (July 2024 Edition): crew and manning provisions, paraphrased

Source: https://www.redensigngroup.org/publications/

> Library note: these are paraphrased notes, not the code text. They cover only what the code says about crew, qualifications, manning, medical fitness and hours of rest. Construction, stability, fire and equipment chapters are not covered. For the detailed certificate requirements (sea service, courses, orals) the code points back to the flag Administration; for UK yachts that means MSN 1858 Amendment 2 (deck), MSN 1904 (engineers), MSN 1862 (Yacht Rating) and MSN 1868 (safe manning).

## What Part A and Part B cover (Preamble and Part B 1.2)

- The REG Yacht Code has two parts plus a set of Common Annexes that apply to both.
- Part A: yachts of 24 metres and over in load line length, in commercial use for sport or pleasure, carrying no cargo and no more than 12 passengers. It replaced the Large Yacht Code (LY3).
- Part B: passenger yachts of any size, in private use or engaged in trade, carrying more than 12 but not more than 36 passengers (and no more than 200 persons in total), with no cargo. It replaced the Passenger Yacht Code.
- Part B is not a code for small commercial yachts under 24 metres. Those are covered in the UK by the small commercial vessel codes (see guide-operational-standards-small-vessels.md and the Workboat Code files).
- The code is an equivalent arrangement under STCW Article 9, among other conventions. This is why yacht-specific certificates can be accepted in place of full STCW merchant certificates on yachts under 3000 GT.

## Part A Chapter 24: Manning, certification and hours of work

Chapter 24 contains only one rule: Part A yachts must meet Common Annex G and any extra requirements of their flag Administration. All the detail sits in Annex G (below).

## Common Annex G: Manning, certification and hours of work (applies to Part A and Part B)

### G1 and G2: scope and purpose
Annex G applies to every yacht under the code and to the certification, training and qualifications of the master, officers and crew, and their hours of work and rest. Its aims are that the yacht is always adequately manned, that every crew member is trained, certificated and qualified for their post, that people are rested before duty, and that all seafarers are medically fit.

### G3: Safe manning
- (1) Besides the master, a yacht must carry enough qualified deck and engineer officers and enough suitably qualified ratings to operate safely at all times, covering safety, the marine environment and security.
- (2) Manning levels are assessed using IMO Resolution A.1047(27) on the principles of safe manning, the applicable conventions (including STCW and the MLC), and national rules on safe manning, hours of work and rest.
- (3) The owner, master and operator are responsible for keeping the yacht safely manned and in line with any Safe Manning Document in force.
- (4) Factors to take into account include: the size, type and complexity of the yacht and its equipment; the propulsion and auxiliary machinery; the area and type of operation (hazards, port calls, traffic density); the day-to-day demands on crew beyond safety duties; the need for navigational and engine watches at sea, at anchor and in port; peak workloads; statutory rest; on-board maintenance; mooring and unmooring; emergencies including mustering and evacuating passengers; the Safety Management System; and security.
- (5) SOLAS requires a Safe Manning Document on vessels of 500 GT and above, except pleasure vessels not engaged in trade. Yachts under 500 GT, and yachts of 500 GT or more used only for pleasure, may also choose to hold one, which allows a "range and risk" approach to manning.
- (6) and (7) If equipment, construction or use changes in a way that could affect manning, the owner or operator must apply for a new Safe Manning Document. The document can be withdrawn if a new proposal is not submitted after such changes, or if the yacht persistently fails to meet hours of rest requirements.
- (8) Every yacht under the code must hold a Safe Manning Document or similar certificate showing that the Administration has approved its minimum manning.
- (9) and (10) The owner, operator or an authorised person applies to the Administration with a clear rationale: how the manning was worked out, confirmation that the Annex G guidance was considered, how hours of rest will be met, and the yacht's size, layout, equipment and machinery (with plans including a General Arrangement Plan). More than one manning proposal can be submitted for different operating patterns or passenger numbers.

### G3 (11) to (17): Qualifications and training
- (11) As a general rule the master and officers must hold certificates under STCW. However, for Part A yachts under 3000 GT and Part B private passenger yachts under 3000 GT, the Administration may accept appropriate yacht qualifications instead. (For UK yachts these are the yacht CoCs in MSN 1858 Amendment 2 and the Small Vessel engineer CoCs in MSN 1904.)
- (12) Anyone working on board who is not a seafarer counted in the manning must be treated as a passenger, unless they have completed familiarisation training to STCW A-VI/1 paragraph 1 and hold documents proving it.
- (13) Every seafarer who is part of the complement with designated safety or pollution prevention duties must, before being given any duties on board, complete STCW basic safety training (A-VI/1 paragraph 2) and hold documents proving it, in addition to familiarisation.
- (14) There must always be enough trained people to assist the total number of passengers on board.
- (15) and (16) Part B yachts only: masters and officers need additional passenger ship training (STCW Regulation V/2, as the Administration requires), and so do other crew named on the muster list to help passengers in an emergency.
- (17) For the accommodation chapter of Part B, "officers" means the master, chief engineer, chief officer or chief mate, deck officers of the watch, engineer officers of the watch, other officers named as such in the crew agreement, the ETO, and the chief steward or purser (head of the interior department).

### G4: Medical fitness
Every seafarer on a yacht under the code must hold a valid medical fitness certificate issued or recognised by the Administration. (For UK yachts this is normally the ENG1 or an accepted equivalent; see MSN 1887 Amendment 1 and MSN 1815 Amendment 7.)

### G5: Schedule of duties
- The master must make sure a schedule of duties is drawn up showing each crew member's hours of work and rest, at sea and in port, and the MLC minimum hours of rest.
- In preparing it, operators consider the nature of the operation, the type, size, construction and equipment of the yacht, manning and crew changes, sickness, maximum continuous watchkeeping, minimum rest, total workload, the ISPS Code where it applies, and the fatigue risk of irregular hours.
- The schedule should only be changed for substantially different work patterns or other significant reasons. A new schedule is not needed for every voyage if the existing one fits and the crew has not changed. A copy must be available to all crew.

### G6: Hours of work and rest
- Everyone on board, including the master, must get the minimum rest and not exceed maximum duty periods set by STCW, the MLC and national law, except in emergencies. Manning must be high enough to allow this.
- Minimum rest: at least 10 hours in any 24-hour period and at least 77 hours in any 7-day period. Rest may be split into no more than two periods, one of at least 6 hours, and the gap between rest periods must not exceed 14 hours.
- Drills should be arranged to disturb rest as little as possible, with compensatory rest for anyone whose rest is disturbed, including by call-outs.
- A record of each seafarer's actual hours must be kept on board, signed by the master (or an authorised person) and the seafarer, with a copy for the seafarer. Deviations in emergencies must be recorded. Records must be kept for at least 12 months and can be inspected by the Administration's surveyors.
- In emergencies that threaten the yacht or lives, the limits may be exceeded, but compensatory rest must follow.

### G7: Personal watercraft
Operators must follow the local law of the state whose waters they are in when using personal watercraft (jet skis and similar).

## Common Annex E: Passengers and occasional workers
- A passenger is anyone on board other than seafarers, occasional workers, people carried because of a rescue or circumstances outside the master's control, and children under one.
- Occasional workers are people who normally work ashore and are on board briefly for the owner or charterer (examples given include a nanny, masseuse or bodyguard). To be treated this way they must: have no emergency duties; get on-board familiarisation so they can look after themselves in an emergency; have crew-standard accommodation; be at least 16; be medically fit to be self-sufficient in an emergency (a seafarer medical certificate is not needed); have a contract giving protection equivalent to the MLC; not pay for food, medical care or travel home; and be trained for their own health and safety.
- If any of these cannot be shown, the person counts as a passenger. If the same people are used this way persistently or often, they must be treated as seafarers with full MLC rights. Occasional workers must be on the crew list, named as such.
- Library inference, not stated in the code: time on board as an occasional worker is not seafarer service, so it would not normally count as qualifying sea service for a CoC.

## Part B 10.3: Crew qualifications on passenger yachts carrying more than 120 persons
- Chapter 10 applies to Part B yachts certified for more than 120 and up to 200 persons. Yachts under 80 metres cannot be certified under it.
- Every seafarer on such a yacht must hold the STCW basic safety elements: personal survival techniques (A-VI/1-1), fire prevention and firefighting (A-VI/1-2), elementary first aid (A-VI/1-3), personal safety and social responsibilities (A-VI/1-4), and security awareness (A-VI/6 paragraph 5).
- Anyone else who is neither a passenger nor a seafarer must meet the occasional worker rules in Annex E, and occasional workers may not be more than 10% of all non-passengers on board. The code's example: 36 passengers, 148 seafarers and 16 occasional workers, 200 in total.

## Part B 11.16: Manning on sailing passenger yachts
- Chapter 11 applies to Part B sailing vessels (any vessel designed to carry sail, as the main or a supplementary means of propulsion).
- When setting manning, the Administration considers the sail handling systems (such as captive winches), the sail area and number of sails and masts, the type of rig (for example a traditional square rigger or a modern sloop), the skilled crew needed for manoeuvres under the yacht's Sailing Operations Manual, and views from the bridge blocked by masts and sails.
- All deck officers on these yachts must hold an RYA/MCA Yachtmaster Ocean Certificate of Competence for sailing vessels.
- The Administration may require one or more Sail Coordinators, depending on the factors above. The Sail Coordinator on watch has the single job of deciding which sails are set for the actual and forecast conditions, keeping within the rig's operating limits and heel-angle curves and referring to the Sailing Operations Manual and stability book. The master keeps overall responsibility. Sail Coordinators must hold at least an RYA/MCA Yachtmaster Ocean Certificate of Competence for sailing vessels.
- Part B 11.13 also requires dedicated lookouts on sailing passenger yachts where sails and masts block the view from the steering position; the lookout must have no other duty while under way.
