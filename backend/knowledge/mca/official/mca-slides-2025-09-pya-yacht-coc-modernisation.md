# UK Yacht CoCs: Modernisation (MCA, 25 September 2025)

Full slide text below, in slide order. Speaker notes in the file were template leftovers ("Marine Surveyor Development Scheme") and are not reproduced, except one relevant note on slide 3 of the fuels section.

How to read this: these slides are the MCA's first public outline of the Yacht Unlimited idea. The deck route they describe was published, with changes, as MSN 1858 Amendment 2 section 4 on 18 May 2026 (see official/msn-1858-amendment-2-yacht-deck-officers.md). The engineer route they describe had still not been published on gov.uk on 25 October 2026. For the MCA's later status update, see official/mca-slides-2026-09-pya-yacht-unlimited-route.md.

## Slide 2
Implementation of CT&M programme – September 2025
- Switch from Paper Charts to ECDIS
- Adoption of Simulator training in lieu of actual sea-service 1:3 up to maximum of 1 month
- Introduction of data skills training, cybersecurity training
- Workshops now incorporate modern automation and technology,
- Simulator-based learning is being integrated with core subjects like mechanics and thermodynamics to emphasise the application of knowledge.
- Common Delivery of some modules.

## Slide 3
Purpose of Yacht Unlimited CoC's
- Supporting Career Advancement: Ensures seafarers in the yacht sector can advance professionally without being disadvantaged.
- Structured Progression Pathway: The Yacht Unlimited CoC provides a clear career progression tailored for seafarers in the large yacht sector.
- Sector-Specific Flexibility: Acknowledges unique operational demands of large yachts, excluding cargo competencies but covering navigation and safety.
- Compliance with International Standards: The CoC aligns with STCW Convention standards ensuring global recognition and competence for yacht officers.

## Slide 5
Small Vessel Engineering Modernisation
- Modernised principles will be integrated into Small Vessel training pathway.
- The syllabuses for the Small Vessel written exam modules will be reviewed with the aims to:
  - remove outdated machinery and procedures
  - include current technologies and industry best practices relevant to small vessels
- The Small Vessel written exams will transition to the MCA's new digital assessment platform.

## Slide 6
Yacht restricted Unlimited kW CoC
- Designed to meet the academic standards of the Unlimited CoC pathway
- Introduction of updated syllabuses tailored for Oral and Engineering Knowledge exams for large yachts, excluding topics irrelevant to onboard environments, such as 2-stroke diesel engines
- Yacht restricted written exams will be administered via the MCA's new digital assessment platform
- Will offer increased flexibility regarding sea service requirements for Yacht Engineers
- Ensures continued transferability across all MCA Engineering Pathways
- Pathway for existing 3000GT/9000kW Chief Engineer CoC holders to achieve Yacht restricted Unlimited kW CoC.

## Slide 7
Approved Electrical Propulsion Course 1 (AEPC1)
- The MCA, in partnership with training providers, industry stakeholders, and shipowners, has established course standards focused on battery safety and electrical propulsion.
- These courses equip learners with the essential knowledge, comprehension, and skills required to safely and effectively operate vessels already employing electric drives with battery technology.
- The training spans 35 hours and aligns with the MCA's Small Vessel Approved course framework, running parallel with the Approved Engine Course 1.
- The course is divided into two parts:
  - Part 1 addresses battery safety and is relevant to all crew members.
  - Part 2 focuses on the operational and technical aspects of electric drives, targeting crew responsible for managing these systems onboard.

## Slide 8
Alternate Fuels
- The MCA has collaborated with industry partners, shipowners, and flag states to create training programs on alternative fuels.
- Each fuel type has distinct risks, necessitating specialized training for safe use.
- Bunkering staff must be trained in fuel-specific safety, including cryogenic protocols, toxicology, and handling of gaseous fuels as needed.
- Hydrogen Pilot course delivered September 2024:
  - Part 1 (1 day) – Basic Hydrogen awareness - aimed at those seafarers completing IGF basic training
  - Part 2 (1 day) – Advanced Hydrogen awareness - aimed at those seafarers completing IGF basic & advanced training including bunkering.
  - Practical bunkering exercise on actual vessel (additional to the 1-day part 2 training)
- Courses in development for: Ammonia; Methanol; IGF 30-day equivalency course

Speaker note: Alternate fuels at the moment are more suitable for these types of vessels due to size and trading patterns therefore more likely to appear as test cases before being fitted to larger ships.

## Notes for the assistant
- Slide 6 is the only MCA text found anywhere that describes the planned engineer route: a "yacht restricted Unlimited kW" CoC with yacht-tailored oral and Engineering Knowledge syllabuses (no 2-stroke diesel), digital written exams, more flexible sea service, and a pathway for existing 3000 GT / 9000 kW Chief Engineer holders. By September 2026 the MCA's engineering review had moved to a different proposal (keep the existing modules, add an academic top-up); see the 2026 slides.
- Slide 14 says the plan was to amend MSN 1858 and MSN 1859. MSN 1859 has been closed to new yacht engineer applicants since January 2023 (replaced by MSN 1904), so any engineer notice may instead amend MSN 1904 or be a new MSN. Nothing has been published.
- Slide 14 also signals that Part A (the online first part of the oral) would be extended to all yacht CoCs. Check MIN 690 for what is actually in force.
