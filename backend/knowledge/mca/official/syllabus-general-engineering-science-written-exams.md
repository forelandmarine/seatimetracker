# General Engineering Science Written Examination Syllabuses

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-engineering-science-written-examination-syllabuses

> The written examination syllabuses for General Engineering Science (GES).

## Change history (gov.uk)

- 2021-06-30: First published.

This guidance provides information and outlines the core engineering and mathematics knowledge required for restricted, management level Certificates of Competency (STCW III/2 and III/3). The MCA General Engineering Science examinations are currently delegated to the International Association of Maritime Institutions (IAMI).

This is guidance to all Owners, Masters, Officers, Ratings and those concerned with maritime training. This guidance should be read with with [MSN 1857 - Amendment 1](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/msn-1857-mf-amendment-1-uk-requirements-for-engineer-officers-and-engineer-operators), [MSN 1904](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/msn-1904-mf-uk-requirements-for-engineer-officer-small-vessel-certificate-of-competency) and replaces the syllabus published on [UK seafarer careers: Training provision, information and examination syllabuses](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/uk-seafarer-careers-training-provision-and-information). This replacement has been made for formatting and accessibility purposes only, no content has been amended during this move.

Please send any feedback to [exams@mcga.gov.uk](mailto:exams@mcga.gov.uk).

## Attachment: General Engineering Science I Written Examination Syllabuses

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-engineering-science-written-examination-syllabuses/general-engineering-science-i-written-examination-syllabuses

#### The candidate will be able to:

## 1\. Mathematics

### 1.1 Fractions and ratios

  1. Convert vulgar to decimal fractions to a specified number of places.
  2. Add, subtract, multiply and divide vulgar and decimal fractions.
  3. Convert vulgar and decimal fractions to percentages.
  4. Solve problems on ratio, proportion and percentage.



### 1.2 The rules of algebra

  1. Solve problems related to addition, subtraction and product of positive and negative numbers.
  2. Solve problems related to addition, subtraction and product of indices, including fractional indices.
  3. Solve problems involving quadratic function divided by linear factor.



### 1.3 Graphs

  1. Form linear equations consistent with data provided.
  2. Plot ordered pairs on graph paper, either given or calculated and join the points with an appropriate curve.
  3. Interpret the equation for a straight-line graph, identifying the intercept and gradient.



### 1.4 Trigonometry

  1. Convert degrees to radians and vice-versa.
  2. Solve problems relating to right angle triangles given two facts about the triangle.
  3. Solve problems on similar triangles.



### 1.5 Geometry and mensuration

  1. Solve problems on the area of an annulus of a circle.
  2. Determine the area of a figure using the mid-ordinate rule.
  3. Solve problems relating to the surface area and volume of cylinder, pyramid, cone and sphere.
  4. Determine the mass of an object from its volume and density.



## 2\. Mechanics

### 2.1 Kinematics

  1. Plot straight line distance time graphs from given data.
  2. Calculate and interpret the gradient of distance time graph as speed.
  3. Plot straight line velocity time graphs from given data.
  4. Calculate and interpret the gradient of velocity time graph as acceleration.
  5. Convert linear to angular velocity and vice versa.
  6. Solve problems relating to distance, time, linear velocity, uniform linear acceleration, angle turned using appropriate diagrams or calculations.



### 2.2 Dynamics

  1. Solve problems involving momentum, force, mass and acceleration.
  2. Convert units of pressure between N/m2, Pascals and bar, gauge and absolute and vice versa.
  3. Solve problems involving force, area and pressure.
  4. Draw graphs of force/distance and torque/angle from given data and interpret the area under the graph to Work Done.
  5. Solve problems involving work, energy and power.



### 2.3 Statics

  1. Define equilibrium and the principle of moments Determine the centroid of a symmetrical lamina.
  2. Solve problems related to levers, shafts. Cantilevers and simply supported beams involving concentrated or uniformly distributed loads.
  3. Differentiate between scalar and vector quantities.
  4. Resolve forces in horizontal and vertical components restricted to the first quadrant.
  5. Solve problems involving two forces meeting at a point.
  6. Solve problems involving the triangle of forces with the aid of Bow’s notation.



### 2.4 Friction

  1. Distinguish between static and dynamic friction.
  2. Determine the co-efficient of friction and explain the effect of lubrication.
  3. Solve problems relating to friction in a horizontal plane.



### 2.5 Machines

  1. Describe, with the aid of a sketch, the construction of: 
     1. Differential wheel and axle;
     2. Weston differential pulley block;
     3. Screwjack;
     4. Crabwinch;
     5. Worm and worm wheel;
     6. Rope-pulley block system.
  2. Determines the velocity ratio of the above machines.
  3. Solve problems relating to efficiency, mechanical advantage and velocity ratio.
  4. Derives the linear law associated with a machine E=aW + c.



### 2.6 Strength of materials

  1. Solve problems relating to direct stress.
  2. Solve problems relating to direct strain.
  3. Sketch and explain a complete load/extension diagram for low carbon steel.
  4. Compare stress/strain graphs for ductile and brittle materials in terms of ductility, brittleness, hardness, limit of proportionality, elastic limit, yield stress.
  5. Define Factor of Safety and relate it to Ultimate Tensile Stress, Ultimate Compression Stress and Working Stress.
  6. Solve problems relating to Factor of Safety, stress and strain.
  7. Solve problems relating to shear.

## Attachment: General Engineering Science II Written Examination Syllabuses

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-engineering-science-written-examination-syllabuses/general-engineering-science-ii-written-examination-syllabuses

#### The candidate will be able to:

## 1\. Heat engines

### 1.1 Heat energy

  1. Defines with reference to enthalpy, sensible heat, latent heat, specific heat capacity.
  2. Solve problems involving solids, liquids, mixtures of solids and liquids, liquids and liquids, liquids and vapours, relating to mass, specific heat capacity and temperature change.
  3. Define, and give an example relating to marine engines of, conduction, convection and radiation.
  4. Determine the co-efficients of linear and cubical expansion.
  5. Solve problems relating to change of temperature and change of dimensions of solids and liquids.
  6. Give examples, relating to marine engines, of the practical considerations relating to change of dimension due to temperature.



### 1.2 Gas laws

  1. State Charles’ Law and Boyle’s Law and derive the combination PV/T = C.
  2. Convert between Kelvin and Celsius scales of temperature, Standard Temperature and Pressure and Normal Temperature and Pressure.
  3. Solve problems relating to pressure, volume, temperature and mass.



### 1.3 Combustion

  1. States the chemical symbols of the elements and compounds associated with combustion, including the suffix and prefix numbers.
  2. Explains higher and lower calorific values of a fuel.
  3. Solves problems relating to the combustion of fuels by mass for stoichiometric air conditions and explains the need for excess air in relation to the effects on operation for internal combustion engines and boilers.
  4. Defines the following: 
     1. Indicated power;
     2. Brake power
     3. Friction power;
     4. Cooling water power;
     5. Exhaust power.
  5. Determine Mean Effective Pressure from indicator card data.
  6. Solves problems relative to two stroke and for stroke internal combustion engines involving indicated power, brake power, mechanical efficiency, thermal efficiency, specific fuel consumption.



### 1.4 Refrigeration

  1. State the properties required of a refrigerant.
  2. Describe a basic circuit for a compression type domestic refrigeration plant explaining the refrigerant condition at cardinal points of the circuit.



## 2\. Electro-Technology

### 2.1 Nature of electricity

  1. Describe the nature of current with regard to electrons.
  2. Explain that a current flow requires a potential difference.
  3. Define conductor and insulator, giving three examples of each in the marine relative to ships.
  4. Identify sources of electricity on board ships.
  5. Identify and use preferred symbols for representation of components of electrical circuits.
  6. Identify that an electric current has three main effects, magnetic, heating and chemical giving examples of each relative to marine vessels.



### 2.2 Electric circuits

  1. Define ampere, potential difference, electro-motive force, watt and resistance.
  2. Solve problems using Ohm’s Law for a circuit of up to three resistors in series.
  3. Solve problems using Ohm’s Law for a circuit of up to three resistors in parallel.
  4. Solve problems relating to the power dissipated in circuits.



### 2.3 Resistance

  1. States, with reference to temperature co-efficient of resistance, the effect of change of temperature on metals, carbon, electrolytes and insulation.
  2. Solves problems relating to temperature co-efficient of resistance.
  3. Solving problems relating to resistivity.
  4. Explain the effect of load current on source terminal voltage.
  5. Solve problems relating to internal resistance of a source.



### 2.4 Secondary cells

  1. Explain the difference between primary and secondary cells.
  2. Describe the chemical changes during the charging and discharging of a lead acid cell, explaining how the emf is measured in single, series and parallel configurations, state how the capacity is measured.
  3. Given a diagram, identify the main parts of secondary cells.



### 2.5 Electromagnetic induction

  1. Define flux, flux density, mmf, and magnetising force, stating the effects of ferromagnetic materials on flux density.
  2. Explain the motor principle in terms of the interaction between magnetic fields.
  3. Explain the generator principle in terms of Faraday’s Law and Lenz’s Law.
  4. Describe the production of an induced emf due to a change in magnetic field.



### 2.6 Measuring instruments

  1. Given a diagram, explain the principles of operation of moving iron and moving coil instruments.
  2. Explain the need for shunts and multipliers to extend the range of a basic electrical indicating instrument.



## 3\. Naval architecture

### 3.1 Fluids at rest

  1. Solve problems relating to thrust exerted due to pressure on horizontally and vertically immersed surfaces with the pressure on one side only.
  2. Apply the Principle of Archimedes to the equilibrium of floating bodies of simple geometric form.



### 3.2 Transverse stability (box shape only)

  1. Define Centre of Gravity, Centre of Buoyancy and Metacentre.
  2. Solve problems relating to the addition of mass to a vessel.
  3. Solve problems involving transverse movement of masses across the deck using the equation: GM = (m x d)/(∆⊝)

## 1\. Mechanics

#### The candidate will know:

  1. The relationship of linear and angular velocity, distance and acceleration.
  2. The effect of forces and torques on motion and lifting machines.
  3. Forces in equilibrium.
  4. The Conservation of Energy theory and momentum.
  5. The relationship between mass, volume and density.
  6. Static and dynamic friction.
  7. The effects of tensile, compressive and shear forces.
  8. The relationship between stress and strain.
  9. The mechanical properties of materials.
  10. Factor of safety.



#### The candidate will be able to:

  1. Solve problems relating to linear and angular distance, velocity and acceleration.
  2. Solve problems relating to work, power, energy and efficiency.
  3. Solve problems relating to lifting machines using mechanical advantage and velocity ratio.
  4. Solve problems relating to force, inertia and momentum.
  5. Solve problems relating to forces in equilibrium by graphical or analytical means.
  6. Solve problems relating to simply supported beams involving vertical forces and uniformly distributed loads.
  7. State and apply the conservation of energy theory.
  8. Solve problems relating to density of solids.
  9. Describe the relationship between density and specific gravity.
  10. Solve problems relating to static and dynamic friction.
  11. Describe the effect of friction on and between materials.
  12. Describe the effects of forces acting on a material.
  13. Explain tensile, compressive and shear forces.
  14. Explain the tensile test for an elastic material.
  15. Explain ductility, brittleness, toughness, hardness, elasticity, plasticity, malleability.
  16. Explain working stress and factor of safety.
  17. Solve problems relating to stress and strain, safe working load and factor of safety.



## 2\. Naval Architecture

#### The candidate will know:

  1. The relationship between centre of gravity and centre of buoyancy and the significance of the relative position of these points.
  2. The changes to the vessel’s condition when weights are added, removed and moved.



#### The candidate will be able to:

  1. Explain the relationship between centre of gravity and centre of buoyancy and the significance of the relative position of these points.

## Attachment: MCA General Engineering Science B Written Examination Syllabuses

Source: https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/general-engineering-science-written-examination-syllabuses/mca-general-engineering-science-b-written-examination-syllabuses

## 1\. Thermodynamics

#### The candidate will know:

  1. The heat required to change the temperature of solids, liquids and gases.
  2. The transfer of heat through solids, liquids and gases.
  3. The dimensional effects on liquids and solids of changes in temperature.
  4. The change of state of water as it is heated and the relationship between temperature and heat.
  5. The use of steam tables to determine the state of water and steam.
  6. The application of the gas laws.
  7. The application of the steady flow energy equation.
  8. The relevance of the calorific values of fuels.
  9. The combustion process and the effects of excess and insufficient air supply.
  10. The use of indicator diagrams to determine the power of an engine.
  11. The heat balance of an internal combustion engine.
  12. The basic refrigeration cycle and the components within a refrigeration plant.



#### The candidate will be able to:

  1. Solve problems on the transfer of heat and Specific Heat Capacity.
  2. Differentiate between heat and temperature.
  3. Describe heat transfer between different media and methods of improving and/or reducing heat transfer.
  4. Describe the affect to dimensions of change of temperature on different media.
  5. Solve problems on thermal expansion and contraction.
  6. Describe the change of state of water as heat is added or removed.
  7. Solve problems on the state of water and steam using steam tables.
  8. Solve problems involving the gas laws.
  9. Solve problems involving the steady flow energy equation.
  10. Describe the combustion of hydrocarbon fuels.
  11. Solve problems on combustion of fuels containing hydrogen, carbon and sulphur.
  12. Explain higher and lower calorific values.
  13. Describe the effects on combustion of excess air and inadequate air
  14. Solve problems involving indicator diagrams to determine the power of an engine.
  15. Solve problems to determine the energy balance of an engine involving: Indicated Power; Brake Power; Friction Power; Brake Thermal Efficiency; Friction losses; Cooling Water losses; Exhaust losses.
  16. Describe the basic refrigeration cycle, indicating the condition of the refrigerant at key points.
  17. Describe the components of the refrigeration system, explaining the change of state that occurs within the components.



## 2\. Hydrostatics

#### The candidate will know:

  1. The thrust on horizontal and vertical immersed surfaces.
  2. Determine that pressure increases with depth and the pressure acting at a specified depth.
  3. The relationship between the centre of pressure and the centroid of an immersed surface.
  4. The reaction at surface supports of immersed and partially immersed surfaces.
  5. The principles of Archimedes.
  6. The application of the Principles of Archimedes to floating rectangular shaped vessels.



#### The candidate will be able to:

  1. Solve problems to determine thrust on horizontal and vertical immersed surfaces.
  2. Solve problems involving the use of formula ρgh to establish pressure at a specified depth.
  3. Describe centre of pressure and the centroid of an immersed surface, explaining how they differ.
  4. Solve problems involving the use of centre of pressure and centroid of immersed and partially immersed surfaces and the reaction at flat surface supports.
  5. Explain the principles of Archimedes.
  6. Solve problems applying the Principles of Archimedes to floating rectangular shaped vessels.



## 3\. Electricity

#### The candidate will know:

  1. DC circuits with resistances in parallel and series.
  2. Power and energy in DC circuits.
  3. AC circuits comprising resistance capacitance and inductance, voltage and current magnification factor.
  4. True power, apparent power and power factor.



#### The candidate will be able to:

  1. Solve problems involving DC circuits with resistances in parallel and series.
  2. Solve problems involving power and energy in DC circuits.
  3. Solve problems involving AC circuit comprising resistance capacitance and inductance, voltage and current magnification factor.
  4. Solve problems involving true power, apparent power and power factor.
