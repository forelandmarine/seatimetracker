# MITAGS: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of what MITAGS publishes about its programs, written for quick reference. The binding rules are 46 CFR and current National Maritime Center (NMC) policy, and MITAGS itself points mariners to the Coast Guard as the final authority.

## Q: What does MITAGS offer for an AB who wants to become a Third Mate?
MITAGS runs an "AB to Mate" program for Able Seafarers with at least 1080 days of sea service. It bundles the STCW courses needed for an Officer in Charge of a Navigational Watch (OICNW) endorsement and a USCG mate licence, including Radar Observer Unlimited, ARPA, ECDIS, Terrestrial and Coastal Navigation, Celestial Navigation, Watchkeeping, Basic Shiphandling, GMDSS, Medical Care Provider, Meteorology, Search and Rescue, Advanced Firefighting, Visual Communications and a licence prep course. In October 2026 the published 2026 cohorts ran April to September and June to November in Baltimore, and July to December in Seattle, so the full sequence takes roughly five to six months.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/able-seaman-to-mate/

## Q: Do the AB to Mate courses have to be taken in a set order?
Yes, MITAGS sets prerequisites. Radar Observer Unlimited comes before ARPA and Watchkeeping, and Terrestrial and Coastal Navigation comes before ECDIS, Watchkeeping and Celestial Navigation. Basic Stability is recommended before Cargo Handling and Stowage, and the licence preparation course is the last (optional) step.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/able-seaman-to-mate/

## Q: Can a MITAGS course replace part of the Coast Guard exam?
MITAGS says its Celestial Navigation (Operational Level) course, if finished within one year of the application date, can satisfy the oceans navigation exam modules (Navigation General: Oceans and Navigation Problems: Oceans) when increasing scope from near coastal to oceans. Students who complete it also do not need to submit Task Control Sheets for those assessments. Check the course approval letter for the exact scope before relying on it.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/able-seaman-to-mate/

## Q: Does the MITAGS Watchkeeping course give sea time credit?
MITAGS states the Watchkeeping (Operational Level) course gives 30 days of sea service credit toward certain Third or Second Mate raises and some domestic or inland licences. It also says this credit cannot be used for STCW service requirements or for recency. Treat it as a small top-up, not a substitute for real sea days.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/able-seaman-to-mate/

## Q: What do I need before taking the MITAGS licence preparation course for original Third Mate?
MITAGS lists a submitted Coast Guard application or a Coast Guard approval letter to test as the prerequisite, and says the test week depends on the Regional Exam Center (REC) schedule, so it recommends booking the exam straight after the course.
OUT OF DATE: the MITAGS page refers to a "CG-718B" application. Since 14 Sep 2026 the application (CG-719B) is submitted through Navita, with fees on Pay.gov. Exams are still sat at an REC once the NMC approves you to test.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/able-seaman-to-mate/

## Q: Is there a sponsored route to Unlimited Third Mate Oceans at MITAGS?
MITAGS lists an apprenticeship-style Unlimited Tonnage Third Mate Oceans program over two years at its Maryland and Seattle campuses, run with a partner company. Entry requires age 19, a high school diploma or equivalent, US citizenship (passport eligible), a TWIC, an MMC and a medical certificate, plus a basic maths entrance test. In October 2026 the cost, partner company and start date were still shown as TBD.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/maritime-apprenticeship-programs/unlimited-tonnage-third-mate-oceans

## Q: Which courses does MITAGS require for Chief Mate or Master unlimited (management level)?
The Mate to Master program is about 13 weeks (65 training days). Mandatory courses listed are Advanced Shiphandling (10 days), Advanced Stability, Advanced Meteorology, Leadership and Managerial Skills, ECDIS (each 5 days), plus either Medical Person in Charge (10 days) or Ship and Shipboard Management (5 days) to cover medical care management. Optional courses (Advanced Cargo Operations, Advanced Watchkeeping, Ship Management, Marine Propulsion Plants, Voyage Planning and Electronic Navigation, and a 25-day licence prep) cover the remaining assessment tasks. Both the Maryland and Seattle campuses now run the full program.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/mate-to-master-program/

## Q: Can management level assessments be signed off on board instead of in a course?
Yes. MITAGS says its optional courses cover 81 assessment tasks, but these can instead be completed on board with a management level deck officer who is a Qualified Assessor. Officers who have good shipboard support can therefore skip some optional courses.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/mate-to-master-program/

## Q: What sea service does MITAGS quote for Chief Mate unlimited?
MITAGS lists an OICNW endorsement plus one year (365 days) of OICNW service on self-propelled vessels on oceans, near coastal waters or the Great Lakes, with at least half on vessels of 1600 GRT or more, on vessels of 100 GRT or more. Up to half the total may be on inland waters, bays and sounds, and 30 days of engine department time may count. Applicants must be 21, a US citizen, and hold a valid MMC and medical certificate. MITAGS does not give a separate checklist for 1600 ton Master on this page, so use the NMC checklist for that.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/mate-to-master-program/

## Q: Do I need to retake Basic Training or Advanced Firefighting for the management upgrade?
MITAGS says no, if you completed Basic Training or Advanced Firefighting within the last five years. Older certificates need revalidation or a refresher before you apply.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/mate-to-master-program/

## Q: What is the difference between Basic Training Revalidation and Refresher at MITAGS?
MITAGS describes Revalidation as a 2-day (16 hour) course for mariners who already hold the certificate and have at least one year of sea time in the last five years. The Refresher is 3 days (23 hours) and is for those without that sea time, refreshing survival techniques and other topics. Both are needed at MMC renewal, roughly every five years.
Note: from 1 Oct 2029 (CG-MMC Policy Letter 01-26), original and renewal STCW Basic Training will also need an approved violence and harassment prevention course; MITAGS pages did not mention this in October 2026.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/guide-to-stcw-certification/

## Q: How long and how much is the original STCW Basic Training at MITAGS?
It is a 5-day course. In October 2026 the price was 2,155 USD in Baltimore and 1,545 USD in Seattle. Candidates must be 18 or older, in good health and able to tread water for one minute, as the course includes pool work, firefighting and CPR.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/course/basic-training/

## Q: Does MITAGS run a TOAR course for towing officers?
No dedicated TOAR course is listed. MITAGS explains that the Towing Officer Assessment Record is signed off by a designated examiner, and that a Western Rivers endorsement on towing vessels of 200 GRT or more needs 90 days of observation and training plus an extra TOAR. Its tug courses are Z-drive (ASD) handling and a towing vessel renewal program.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/tug-and-barge/

## Q: Is there a MITAGS course for renewing a Master or Mate (Pilot) of Towing Vessels endorsement?
Yes, a 1-day Towing Vessel Renewal Program with full mission simulation. MITAGS says the Coast Guard approved it to meet the practical demonstration option for renewing a Master or Mate (Pilot) of Towing Vessels endorsement under 46 CFR 10.227. No sessions were scheduled in October 2026.
OUT OF DATE: the course page ties its maneuvering tasks to NVIC 04-01. Current towing officer guidance, including the TOAR, is NVIC 03-16 Change 7 (Sep 2024).
Source: https://www.mitags.org/course/towing-vessel-renewal-program/

## Q: What sea service is needed for Designated Duty Engineer (DDE) according to MITAGS?
MITAGS lists DDE 1000 HP at 360 days total with 180 as QMED, DDE 4000 HP at 720 days with 360 as QMED, and DDE unlimited HP at 1080 days with 540 as QMED, with equivalent positions accepted. Its DDE Unlimited course is a QMED subject prep course (General, Oiler, Fireman/Watertender) and the page does not claim it replaces the DDE exam. No sessions were available in October 2026.
Source: https://www.mitags.org/course/designated-duty-engineer-unlimited-horsepower/
