# WorkBoat credentialing columns: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of WorkBoat trade magazine pieces, most written by the head of a licensing software company (MM-SEAS), read in October 2026. The official sources are 46 CFR 10.107 and 15.705, the NMC time-and-a-half FAQ (nmc-faq-time-half-credit.md), the NMC sea service guide, NMC announcements and the US Code. Where anything here differs, the official text wins.

## Q: Who can get time-and-a-half sea service credit for a 12-hour day?
WorkBoat says only mariners on vessels authorized by the Coast Guard to run a two-watch system (six-on, six-off or equivalent) under 46 U.S.C. 8104 and 46 CFR 15.705, and who actually stand that watch. A normal day is 8 hours of watch or day work, overtime excluded, so a 12-hour day on any other vessel still counts as one day. Vessel types that may qualify include towing vessels, offshore supply vessels within the regulated tonnage and route limits, some fish processing vessels, and cadets on Maritime Administration training ships.
Note: this matches the NMC time-and-a-half FAQ. The NMC also says 12-hour days on recreational vessels under 100 GRT never earn time-and-a-half. Yachts are not on the two-watch list, so yacht crew normally get one day per day.
Source: https://www.workboat.com/how-to-receive-time-and-a-half-credit-for-your-mmc

## Q: How should a sea service letter show time-and-a-half?
WorkBoat's advice: the letter, on company letterhead and signed by an authorized person, should list whole days actually worked, never a fraction such as 0.5, and never be built by dividing total hours by 8 or 12. Where the vessel is authorized for two watches, it should include a statement that the service was on a two-watch system for a 12-hour working day authorized under 46 CFR 15.705. If the vessel type is not one listed in law but its Certificate of Inspection shows a two-watch system, the COI is not required with the application but can help prove eligibility. It warns that the NMC is checking for inflated sea service, which can lead to delay, denial or revocation.
Source: https://www.workboat.com/how-to-receive-time-and-a-half-credit-for-your-mmc

## Q: What makes a sea service letter go into "awaiting information"?
WorkBoat calls employer sea service letters the biggest single cause of delay once an application is in the queue, adding an average of 20 to 30 days per AI letter. Its checks: give each vessel's name and official or IMO number; give your position using the title on the vessel's COI or safe manning document rather than a company job title; use only the Coast Guard route terms (Inland, Great Lakes, Western Rivers, Near Coastal, Oceans); count a day correctly (a 12-hour shift is still one day unless two-watch credit applies); and include the exact renewal statements the NMC expects, for example the drills wording for Basic Training and towing renewals and the radar use wording for radar renewal.
Note: the official wording for renewal statements is in the NMC renewal checklists and sea service guide stored in this library.
Source: https://www.workboat.com/navigating-the-nmc-backlog-three-tips-for-a-perfect-sea-service-letter

## Q: What is the difference between a rejected application and an awaiting information letter?
WorkBoat explains that some screening errors, such as an incomplete CG-719B, missing TWIC information or a wrong fee payment, get the whole application rejected, which sends you to the back of the line when you resubmit. Other gaps lead to an awaiting information letter, which pauses your application in place while you supply the missing item. Because the NMC works first in, first out, a complete application is the fastest route.
Note: matches the NMC notice of 24 Oct 2024 on rejecting incomplete applications (nmc-application-rejection-and-top-ai-reasons.md).
Source: https://www.workboat.com/navigating-the-nmc-backlog-three-tips-for-a-perfect-sea-service-letter

## Q: My credential expired while my renewal was stuck. Can I keep working?
WorkBoat summarized the NMC bulletin of 27 Aug 2026: a 120-day temporary authorization to keep working on national endorsements of an expired MMC applies only if the MMC expired between 1 Jan and 31 Jul 2026, the renewal was submitted before it expired and by 26 Aug 2026, and the renewal now has an awaiting information letter or an approved-to-test letter for the open book renewal exam. The 120 days run from the date of that letter. It does not cover STCW endorsements or medical certificates, you must carry the expired MMC and the authorization letter, and there is no second extension. Mariners simply waiting in line with a complete application are not covered. WorkBoat put pending applications at about 10,000 to 14,000 and the NMC estimate at four months for a complete application.
Note: the NMC bulletin itself is stored in nmc-announcements-2026.md (2026-08-27 entry).
Source: https://www.workboat.com/mariners-awaiting-information-testing-receive-120-day-mmc-relief-from-coast-guard

## Q: Do the reduced Able Seaman sea time rules end in December 2026?
WorkBoat reports that they no longer end. The Coast Guard Authorization Act of 2025, signed 18 Dec 2025, wrote the reductions into permanent law: AB-Unlimited 18 months, AB-Limited 12 months, AB-Special 6 months. It points out that CG-MMC Policy Letter 04-24 (concurrent RFPNW and AB-Deck credit) and the NMC AB-Deck checklist still show a 22 Dec 2026 end date and would need updating.
Note: confirmed against the US Code. 46 U.S.C. 7307, 7308 and 7309 were amended by Pub. L. 119-60, div. G, sec. 7301(d), on 18 Dec 2025 (see us-code-46-7306-7310-able-seaman-service-2025.md). The eCFR text of 46 CFR 12.403 had not been updated as of 22 Oct 2026, and PL 04-24's concurrent credit is a Coast Guard policy, so watch for an NMC update on that point.
Source: https://www.workboat.com/ab-sea-time-reductions-are-here-to-stay

## Q: How are maritime academies handling Navita?
WorkBoat reported in August 2026 that Texas A&M Maritime Academy enrolled 220 cadets on the MM-SEAS platform before the Navita launch, to log sea service as it is earned, issue digital course certificates that the Coast Guard can verify at source, and have applications checked before submission. MM-SEAS operates separately from Coast Guard systems; Navita is the official submission route.
