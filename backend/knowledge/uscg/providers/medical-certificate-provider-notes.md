# Medical certificate (CG-719K) in practice: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of public pages from a captain's license school (MLS, article dated 18 Jun 2026 for the CG-719K guide), a state maritime academy (Maine Maritime, instructions dated Apr 2026) and a trade magazine, read in October 2026. The official sources are 46 CFR Part 10 Subpart C, the Merchant Mariner Medical Manual (COMDTINST M16721.48), the form itself (form-cg-719k-medical-certificate-application.md) and the NMC medical pages. Where anything here differs, the official text wins.

## Q: How do I do the CG-719K now that Navita is live?
MLS describes three steps. First, open a separate Medical Certificate application in Navita and fill in your own sections online (personal details, medical history, medications, signature and consents). Second, download and print the form Navita generates and take every page to your appointment, where the practitioner examines you and completes and signs their sections on paper. Third, go back to the same Medical Certificate application in Navita, upload the signed form and any supporting records, and submit. The medical application is separate from the MMC application, so do not upload the physical to the MMC application. You can submit the medical before you finish your course.
Note: this matches the Navita mariner user guide, which says the CG-719K medical history and medication pages are completed online and the practitioner completes the examination sections.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/cg-719k-application-for-medical-certificate

## Q: Should I use the CG-719K or the short CG-719K/E?
Both MLS and the official form say the full CG-719K is for anyone who will stand a watch, including every OUPV and Master applicant, and anyone who needs lookout duties. The short CG-719K/E is only for entry-level or staff officer mariners who will not stand a navigational or engineering watch or do lookout duty, and for food handlers. MLS lists choosing the wrong form as a common cause of delay.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/cg-719k-application-for-medical-certificate

## Q: Who can sign my physical?
Only a licensed physician (MD or DO), physician assistant or nurse practitioner. MLS stresses that a chiropractor or registered nurse cannot sign it, and that you should phone ahead to confirm the clinic can do it, since many clinics rarely see the form. Vision and hearing tests can be done by office staff, but the practitioner must review the results.
Note: the official form says the practitioner must be licensed by a US state, possession or territory, and US Registered Pilots must be examined by a licensed medical doctor.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-physical

## Q: What goes wrong most often on the physical paperwork?
MLS lists: using an expired edition of the form (check the date printed at the top), a signature without a date, a page the practitioner forgot to sign or initial, an examiner who is not an MD, PA or NP, a condition reported without supporting records, and letting the signed form go stale. Its main tip is to check every page before you leave the exam room. Maine Maritime adds that the applicant pages must be typed, that you should give your full middle name and your mariner reference number (not your Social Security number) if you have one, and that every one of the 34 medical history questions needs a yes or no answer.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-physical
Source: https://mainemaritime.edu/student-life/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/04/Instructions-For-Filling-Out-CG_719K-Medical-Form-4_1_26.pdf

## Q: How long is a signed physical good for?
MLS says the signed exam must reach the Coast Guard within 12 months, and suggests doing the physical and the drug test near the end of your course so both are still in date when you apply. It describes the drug test as good for about six months.
Note: this matches 46 CFR 10.304(d) (reports must be submitted within 12 months of the practitioner's signature) and 46 CFR 16.220 (drug test within 185 days).
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-physical

## Q: What do I have to list under medications?
Maine Maritime repeats the form's rule: list every prescription and over-the-counter medicine, supplement and vitamin that you filled, refilled or took in the 30 days before you sign, and anything you used for 30 days or more in the last 90 days.
Source: https://mainemaritime.edu/student-life/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/04/Instructions-For-Filling-Out-CG_719K-Medical-Form-4_1_26.pdf

## Q: What are the vision standards?
Maine Maritime summarizes 46 CFR 10.305 for its cadets: STCW deck applicants need vision correctable to 20/40 in both eyes and at least 20/200 uncorrected in both eyes, plus a passing color vision test without tinted lenses. Engine applicants need 20/50 correctable and 20/200 uncorrected in one eye, and only need to tell red, green, blue and yellow apart. If you need glasses to meet the standard, the certificate will say you must wear them and carry a spare pair. MLS adds that a national deck (captain's license) applicant needs one eye correctable to 20/40, and that a color vision problem can lead to a restriction rather than a refusal.
Note: both are consistent with 46 CFR 10.305: national deck officers need 20/40 in one eye; STCW deck endorsements need 20/40 in both eyes (mariners who held an MMC before 1 Jan 2017 keep the one-eye standard).
Source: https://mainemaritime.edu/student-life/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/04/Instructions-For-Filling-Out-CG_719K-Medical-Form-4_1_26.pdf
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/cg-719k-application-for-medical-certificate

## Q: What is a medical waiver and when do I need one?
Maine Maritime explains that many conditions need no waiver at all. Others are approved with a waiver that adds a limitation or a reporting duty to the certificate, for example an annual medication update for ADHD treatment, or a requirement to wear glasses and carry spares. MLS says a waiver may carry a limitation such as daylight-only operation, that you carry the waiver letter while working, and that you must report any change in a waived condition. MLS says the Coast Guard weighs how persistent a condition is, how likely it is to cause sudden incapacitation, and how treatable it is.
Note: waivers are granted under 46 CFR 10.303; the Medical Manual chapters on each condition list what the treating provider should send.
Source: https://mainemaritime.edu/student-life/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/04/Instructions-For-Filling-Out-CG_719K-Medical-Form-4_1_26.pdf
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-disqualification

## Q: Will diabetes, heart problems, sleep apnea or mental health conditions stop me?
MLS says these are usually reviewed case by case rather than automatic bars. Its examples: for diabetes it cites an A1C below 8 with documented medical supervision; for heart disease after a stent or bypass it says the Coast Guard typically looks for about 18 months without further procedures plus a stress test and a cardiologist letter; treated sleep apnea is often approved; anxiety or depression may be waived if stable, but a serious or worsening history can disqualify. Opioids and other impairing drugs are treated as at least temporary disqualifiers, and any marijuana use fails the federal drug test even with a state card. MLS suggests getting the physical done before paying for a course if you are worried.
Note: the Medical Manual does use an HbA1c below 8 percent as a guideline for diabetes. The Medical Manual's coronary artery disease chapter does not set a fixed 18-month wait; it asks for a treating provider assessment, stability and objective testing. Treat the 18-month figure as the school's experience, not a rule.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-disqualification

## Q: What happens if the NMC wants more medical information?
Maine Maritime explains that a yes answer on the history questions can trigger a request for more information about the diagnosis, treatment and how well you manage the condition. You usually have 60 days to respond, the application is on hold while the NMC waits, and if the deadline passes the application times out and you must start again. MLS adds that a denial can be challenged by a written request for reconsideration under 46 CFR 1.03-40 within 30 days of the letter.
Note: NMC FAQ and appeal pages describe the reconsideration process; see nmc-appeal-reconsideration-page.md.
Source: https://mainemaritime.edu/student-life/wp-content/uploads/sites/5/2026/04/Instructions-For-Filling-Out-CG_719K-Medical-Form-4_1_26.pdf

## Q: How common are medical certificate delays?
WorkBoat reported the NMC's 2022 notice: more than 10 percent of medical applications arrive with missing information, the NMC issued 7,882 awaiting information reasons for medical certificates in 2021, and the most common causes were vision results outside the standard (or abnormal results without the follow-up tests) and conditions that need a treating provider report, especially diabetes, sleep apnea, substance use, heart conditions and seizures.
Note: the NMC notice itself is stored as nmc-top-medical-awaiting-information-reasons-2021.md.
Source: https://www.workboat.com/uscg-medical-certificates-10-are-missing-information

## Q: How long is a medical certificate valid?
MLS says up to five years for national-only mariners. The school's page does not mention STCW.
Note: 46 CFR 10.301(b) gives 5 years for national mariners but only 2 years for anyone serving on vessels where STCW applies (1 year if under 18). Pilots also have separate rules.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/cg-719k-application-for-medical-certificate
