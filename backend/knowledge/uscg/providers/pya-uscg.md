# PYA: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of PYA public pages, written for a sea-time logging assistant. For US credentials the binding rules are 46 CFR and National Maritime Center (NMC) guidance; PYA rules apply only to the MCA and Transport Malta processes it serves.

Scope note: as of 2026-10-25 the PYA site has no pages about the USCG, US crew, US credentials or US-flag vessels. The notes below cover PYA testimonial guidance only as it bears on Americans who want to use yacht time toward a USCG credential, and they say plainly where PYA is silent.

## Q: Does the PYA verify sea service for the US Coast Guard?
No. PYA says it is authorised by the UK Maritime and Coastguard Agency (MCA) and Transport Malta to verify yacht sea service testimonials. None of its pages mention the USCG or US credentials. A PYA verification stamp is therefore not a USCG document, and the NMC sets its own evidence rules.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/sea-service-verification

## Q: Can an American use a PYA-verified testimonial in a USCG application?
PYA does not say. Under the official USCG rules (46 CFR 10.232), service by someone other than the owner must be shown by letters from the owner, operator or master, with vessel name, official number, tonnage, routes and dates. A PYA testimonial signed by the captain may contain much of this, but the user should check that it carries every item the NMC asks for, or get a separate USCG-style letter or CG-719S (for vessels under 200 GRT).
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/sea-service-verification

## Q: Who can sign a PYA sea service testimonial?
The captain, chief officer, chief engineer, owner or manager. A captain cannot sign their own testimonial; the owner or manager must confirm a master's service. For USCG purposes the official rule is a little different: an owner may attest their own service with proof of ownership, and others need a letter from the owner, operator or master.
Source: https://www.pya.org/news/top-tips-on-getting-a-valid-sea-service-testimonial

## Q: Whose definition of sea service does PYA use?
PYA checks each testimonial against the MCA definitions of sea service, published separately for deck and engineering. These MCA categories (actual sea service, standby, yard time) do not map directly onto USCG days. The USCG counts days by hours worked: 8 hours is a day, and on vessels under 100 GRT not less than 4 hours may be accepted.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/sea-service-verification

## Q: How long does PYA verification take and what does it cost?
The process is fully digital and can take up to 25 working days. Full members get unlimited verification and a PYA Service Record. Non-members pay 50 euros per testimonial, with a minimum of two on the first submission. This applies to MCA and Transport Malta use only.
Source: https://www.pya.org/services/sea-service-verification

## Q: What are PYA's tips for getting a valid testimonial?
Enter accurate start and end dates, make sure they do not overlap service already recorded, and do not claim more sea service than your total employment period. Check everything before sending it to the signatory, and answer queries quickly. Most usefully, ask for the testimonial before leaving the vessel, especially on seasonal contracts. The same habits help when building USCG evidence, because chasing a former captain later is the usual cause of gaps.
Source: https://www.pya.org/news/top-tips-on-getting-a-valid-sea-service-testimonial

## Q: Does PYA list a US seaman's discharge book?
No. Its knowledge base lists discharge books from the UK MCA, Malta, Cayman Islands, Marshall Islands, BVI and Cook Islands, and notes the Isle of Man now issues a record of sea service instead. No US issuer is listed. PYA also says a Red Ensign discharge book only proves employment and rank, not the nature of the work, so testimonials are still needed.
Source: https://www.pya.org/knowledge-base/seaman-discharge-book-and-others

## Q: Can a discharge book or PYA Service Record be filled in after the fact?
PYA says discharge book entries record joining and leaving dates and ports and cannot be backfilled, so you should get one when employment starts. The PYA Service Record, by contrast, is built from checked testimonials and can be completed retroactively. USCG sea service also never expires, so older yacht time can still be documented if you can get signed evidence.
Source: https://www.pya.org/knowledge-base/seaman-discharge-book-and-others

## Q: What does PYA say new crew need before starting?
Its FAQ suggests STCW Basic Training, security awareness and a seafarer medical such as the ENG1 or an equivalent, plus a yachting CV. It also warns crew to check that local immigration and employment rules allow them to work. For Americans, note that a USCG credential needs its own USCG medical certificate; the PYA FAQ does not discuss this.
Source: https://www.pya.org/faq

## Q: What does PYA not cover for American crew?
PYA does not cover: USCG credential requirements, the CG-719S or CG-719B forms, how foreign-flag yacht time counts for USCG, GT versus GRT tonnage limits, 4-hour versus 8-hour days, time-and-a-half for two-watch vessels, TWIC, USCG medical certificates, Navita applications or NMC processing times. Its FAQ points to srb@pya.org for personalised advice, but that advice is MCA-based. Use the official NMC notes for all of these.
Source: https://www.pya.org/faq
