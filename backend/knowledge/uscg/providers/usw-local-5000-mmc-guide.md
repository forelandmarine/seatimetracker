# USW Local 5000: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of a union's step-by-step guide for new Great Lakes seafarers. The binding rules are 46 CFR and National Maritime Center (NMC) guidance; where the union page differs, the official position wins.

Context: this guide is written for entry-level ratings on Great Lakes freighters, not yacht crew, but most of the steps (TWIC, drug test, medical, application, fees) are the same for anyone applying for a first Merchant Mariner Credential. We tried the SIU guide (seafarers.org) first but it was blocked (HTTP 403).

## Q: What do I need before I can work on a US-flag commercial vessel?
The union says you need a TWIC from the TSA and a Merchant Mariner Credential from the Coast Guard, including a USCG medical certificate. Both the MMC and the medical certificate are needed to be hired, and they often arrive in separate envelopes.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: Do I get the TWIC first?
The union says yes: apply through the TSA, book an enrolment centre appointment, give your occupation as merchant mariner, and pay 125.25 dollars. It says you cannot continue the MMC application without it. The official NMC guidance points applicants to a policy letter on when a TWIC is or is not required, so check that before assuming every applicant needs one. The fee is set by TSA and may have changed.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: In what order should I do the forms?
The union recommends starting with the CG-719P drug test, because lab results take longest, then the CG-719K medical exam, then the CG-719C conviction statement if you have any record, and the CG-719B application last. It suggests asking the clinic whether the drug test and physical can be done in one visit. A 5-panel DOT drug test typically costs 50 to 150 dollars and the physical about 75 to 200 dollars, according to the union.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: Should I use the short entry-level medical form?
The union notes there is a shorter CG-719K/E form for entry-level applicants, but recommends the full CG-719K anyway. Its reasoning is practical: the full exam keeps your options open for later upgrades. It adds that most companies accept an exam done within a year of employment.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: What is the CG-719S and do I need it?
The union describes the CG-719S small vessel sea service form as optional for its members, because small-vessel time generally does not count toward Great Lakes freighter jobs. For yacht and small-boat crew it is the opposite: officially, the CG-719S is the form for service on vessels under 200 GRT, one form per vessel, so it is usually central to a yacht crew application.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: What fees do I pay for a first rating endorsement?
The union lists a total of 140 dollars on Pay.gov: 95 dollars for an original rating endorsement evaluation plus 45 dollars for issuance, with the examination fields left blank. Print and keep the receipt. Fees for officer endorsements with exams are different, and NMC fee tables are the authority.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: Where do I send the application?
The union says to send it to a Regional Exam Center (it suggests Toledo, by email), not to the NMC, and to track it on the Homeport status page.
OUT OF DATE: since 14 September 2026 applications go through the Navita system, with fees on Pay.gov. Follow the current NMC instructions rather than emailing a REC.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: What are the most common mistakes?
The union lists: not disclosing a criminal record fully on the CG-719C, sending the application to the wrong office, errors on the application form, and losing the Pay.gov receipt. It warns that conviction statement errors alone can add several weeks. Officially, the top NMC hold reason for professional qualifications in September 2026 was the sea service form, so yacht crew should check their sea service evidence most carefully.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: Can a DUI affect my maritime career?
For Great Lakes work, the union warns that a DUI or OWI can stop you clearing Canadian customs, because Canada treats it as a serious offence. It suggests applying for Canadian rehabilitation before applying for a TWIC or MMC. This is a practical employment issue for vessels calling at Canadian ports, separate from whether the Coast Guard issues the credential.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: How long does processing take?
The union page does not give a processing time. Officially, NMC processing was estimated at about 4 months in July 2026, so apply well before you need the credential.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: What documents should I send to employers once approved?
The union suggests scans of both sides of the TWIC, pages 2 to 4 of the MMC and the medical certificate. It also reminds members that the medical certificate fits in a pouch at the back of the MMC and should not be lost.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials

## Q: Does this guide cover STCW?
No. The union page does not mention STCW Basic Training or any STCW endorsement, because its members work on domestic Great Lakes routes. Yacht crew heading to foreign-flag yachts will normally need STCW Basic Training, which is covered in other notes in this library.
Source: https://usw5000.org/credentials
