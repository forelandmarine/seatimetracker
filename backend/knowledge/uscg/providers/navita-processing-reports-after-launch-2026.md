# Processing times after the Navita launch: what mariners and others reported (Sept to Oct 2026)

These are our own short summaries. Nothing here is official. The official position is in nmc-announcements-2026.md (latest estimate 21 Jul 2026: complete applications within about 4 months, not counting time waiting on the mariner) and navita-page.md, nmc-faq-navita.md and navita-mariner-user-guide.md. How NMC measures "processing time" is explained in gao-23-106629-merchant-mariner-credentialing-2023.md.

What official sources say about the period: a 12 Oct 2026 web.archive.org capture of the NMC home page still lists the 21 Jul 2026 "Processing Timeline Update 2" as the latest processing notice, and shows a "Tell Us About Your Navita Experience" survey. The monthly NMC performance PDFs for 2026 could not be read (HTTP 403, and the last archived copy is from April 2026). The Coast Guard has a public listening session on Navita at STAR Center, Dania Beach, Florida, on 19 Nov 2026 (see nmc-announcements-2026.md).

Reliability: low to moderate. Forum posts are anonymous, unverified and few. They are useful only as "this is what some mariners saw", never as a processing time to promise anyone.

## Q: How long are medical certificates taking through Navita?
Two forum posters reported fast medical certificates in October 2026. One said a routine renewal with no health issues, submitted 29 Sep, was issued 5 Oct, and called it the quickest Coast Guard turnaround they had seen in 18 years. Another said about three weeks from submitting to receiving the certificate by email. The first poster added that the certificate arrived from an unfamiliar NMC email address and landed in junk mail, and that the Navita status did not change to issued. Reliability: two anecdotes, simple cases only.
Source: https://forum.gcaptain.com/t/navita-timeframes/77928

## Q: How long are MMC renewals and upgrades taking?
No forum poster had reported a completed MMC renewal, raise of grade or new endorsement filed through Navita by 20 Oct 2026. One mariner who filed an able seafarer deck upgrade on 6 Oct had heard nothing by 17 Oct. Another filed a standard renewal on 3 Oct and still saw only "received" on 20 Oct. An experienced poster expected new systems to slow things at first and suggested waiting 60 days or more before worrying. His own application, uploaded the old way on 7 Sep, moved to safety and suitability vetting on 22 Sep and to "ready to be evaluated" by the evaluation branch on 1 Oct, and was still waiting on 18 Oct.
Source: https://forum.gcaptain.com/t/navita-timeframes/77928

## Q: I submitted in Navita but got no email from NMC. Is that normal?
Several mariners reported the same thing. One submitted a renewal on 17 Sep and had no acknowledgement by 5 Oct; a reply said the old system had taken almost six weeks just to acknowledge an application. The status in Navita may sit at "submitted" or "received" for weeks. Reliability: anecdotes; check the Navita case feature or call NMC before resubmitting, because duplicates slow things down (official advice in nmc-announcements-2026.md).
Source: https://forum.gcaptain.com/t/nativa-processing-time/77750

## Q: Does NMC answer the help ticket (case) feature in Navita?
In early October 2026 a mariner asked whether anyone had ever had a reply to a Navita help ticket; nobody in the thread confirmed a reply, and one poster said they could not log in at all even after deleting their account twice. The same thread reposted the NMC notice about the 19 Nov 2026 listening session. Reliability: anecdotes; AMO's guidance (below) is that cases, phone or chat are the right channels.
Source: https://forum.gcaptain.com/t/new-uscg-help-ticket-feature/77739

## Q: What does the AMO union tell members about Navita?
In a 9 Oct 2026 post AMO said: Navita is Phase 1 of a three-phase rollout; accounts use login.gov; if you forget login.gov details you can delete and recreate the account, which takes about 24 hours; set up two kinds of two-factor sign-in. It advises practicing the CG-719K and CG-719B in Navita without submitting, then using quick reference guides. AMO said its role as a third party (proxy) has been cut back: on medical applications it can be added but cannot review or act, and it can no longer step in at the last minute to speed things up. It said errors could make waits much longer, recommended applying six to eight months before expiry, and suggested using NMC chat so you can keep a transcript. Reliability: union member guidance, practical and recent, not official.
Source: https://www.amo-union.org/navita-account-creation-and-medical-certificate-applications/

## Q: Is Navita making things faster, according to the press?
The only critical press piece found is a gCaptain editorial of 21 Oct 2026 by an anonymous US Master. It argues the first release mainly turns typed answers into a PDF without a back end, that it checks a file is present but not what is in it, that its fee calculator gave the wrong amount in the author's testing, and that only an individual (not an employer or union) can be named as a third party. It repeats the summer backlog figures (over 19,000 applications, 8 to 12 months) and claims, from unnamed sources, that staff re-enter data into the old system. gCaptain's editor noted that the internal workflow claims were not verified. Reliability: opinion with unverified claims; do not quote its claims as fact.
Source: https://gcaptain.com/editorial-the-coast-guard-spent-3-8-million-on-a-pdf/

## Q: My approved course certificate expired while my application sat at NMC. What can I do?
A forum thread (30 Sep to 1 Oct 2026) described an applicant whose OUPV exam-substitute course passed its one-year limit while the application was pending, so NMC asked for the course to be retaken before issuing the upgrade. An experienced poster advised asking NMC for "reconsideration" (the first step of an appeal), then appealing to Coast Guard headquarters if needed, or taking the exam at an REC instead. Reliability: forum advice, but it matches the official appeal route in nmc-appeal-reconsideration-page.md and the one-year course rule in nmc-faq-courses.md. Long processing times make this more likely, so apply soon after finishing a course.
Source: https://forum.gcaptain.com/t/uscg-incompetence/77703

## Q: What does Navita mean for commercial fishermen?
A maritime injury law firm blog (Stacey & Jacobsen PLLC) (15 Sep 2026) repeated the launch facts (paper being phased out over 12 months, first in first out, apply up to eight months before expiry) and warned fishermen not to plan on finishing applications from the boat because of poor connectivity. It gave no processing times. CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: it says every commercial fishing vessel needs at least one person holding an MMC. The Coast Guard's own overview says most fishing vessel operators are unlicensed and a licensed operator is only required on vessels over 200 gross tons (see fishing-vessel-operator-training-4502g-status.md). The official text wins. Reliability: low for licensing rules.
Source: https://www.maritimeinjurylawyersblog.com/navita-has-officially-launched-what-it-means-for-fishermen-applying-for-credentials/
