# Mariners Learning System: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of MLS web pages, read in October 2026. MLS is a training company; 46 CFR and current National Maritime Center (NMC) guidance are binding where anything here differs.

Access note: the site rate-limited automated fetches (HTTP 429) on 2026-10-25; the pages listed were read by direct download.

## Q: How many CG-719S forms do I need?
One form per vessel, and each form covers at most five years. Ten years on one boat therefore needs two forms, and two boats always need two forms even if the dates overlap. MLS warns that listing more than one vessel on a single form gets the whole form rejected.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/documenting-boating-experience-form-cg-719s

## Q: What must the CG-719S show for each vessel?
Vessel name and official or state registration number, gross tonnage, propulsion power and type, what you did aboard, the waters, and approximate dates by month and year. Above 25 GRT you must give the tonnage or the length, breadth and depth so it can be calculated. Waters should be specific, such as a named bay or sound, not just "Atlantic Ocean". Your days must also be split into Great Lakes, inland and near coastal, and those three must add up to your total.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/documenting-boating-experience-form-cg-719s

## Q: Who signs the form, and what if it is my own boat?
You always sign. If you own the boat you may attest your own time but must attach proof of ownership showing your name and the registration number, such as state registration, documentation, insurance or a bill of sale; a company-owned boat needs proof of company ownership such as articles of incorporation. If you are not the owner, the owner, operator or master signs with their address and phone number. If you were the captain but not the owner, you cannot attest your own service and the owner must fill in the days and sign.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/documenting-boating-experience-form-cg-719s

## Q: Does the person signing my sea time need to be a licensed captain?
No. MLS calls this one of the biggest myths. The owner, or their representative such as a charter company, signs. Club boats, rentals and bareboat charters abroad all count if the owner or company signs the form or gives a letter. Tip from MLS: fill the form in yourself first so the owner only has to sign.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-sea-time

## Q: Is a day 4 hours or 8 hours?
MLS explains that the rule defines a day as 8 hours and never credits less than 4 hours, but in practice the NMC commonly accepts 4 hours as a day for OUPV and Master applicants on small vessels. Only one day can be counted per calendar date however many trips you make. Periods under 4 hours earn nothing.
Note: 46 CFR 10.107 sets a day at 8 hours, with the Coast Guard able to accept not less than 4 hours on vessels under 100 GRT.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/documenting-boating-experience-form-cg-719s

## Q: How much sea time do I need and how recent must it be?
OUPV: 360 days since age 16 with 90 in the last 7 years. Master: 360 to 720 days depending on route and tonnage. For an OUPV Near Coastal you need 90 days beyond the boundary line, rising to 360 for Master Near Coastal. Time only on inland waters gives an inland licence.
Note: the 7-year window matches Policy Letter 02-25.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/pages/sea-time-requirements
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-sea-time

## Q: How far back can I claim sea time?
Back to your 16th birthday. The catch is proof: owners move or die and boats are sold, so old time without a signature you can still obtain will probably not count. MLS adds that the Coast Guard checks for inconsistencies, such as days claimed on a lake that was frozen that month, and may delay or refuse the application.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/captains-license-resources/captains-license-sea-time

## Q: What are the most common CG-719S mistakes?
Blank required fields, putting "owner" as your position (use "operator" instead), claiming engine-room service on a boat without one, two vessels on one form, waters that do not match the service claimed, made-up or wrong registration numbers, including other people's information, and adding the days up wrongly. If a form is rejected you send a corrected one, but the sea time on it is not credited until then.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/documenting-boating-experience-form-cg-719s

## Q: How do I document sea service from a company or a professional yacht job?
Use a sea service letter on the company's or vessel's letterhead, dated, listing for each vessel the name, official number, type, propulsion, tonnage, horsepower if relevant, start and end dates and underway days, plus your position. It must say whether days were 8 or 12 hours; 12-hour days require that the Certificate of Inspection authorises a two-watch system. The CG-719S is only for vessels of 200 GRT or less, so larger yachts need a letter instead.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: MLS says officer endorsement letters must break down days within the last three years. Under Policy Letter 02-25 recency for national officer endorsements is now 90 days in the last 7 years, so a breakdown for that period is what matters.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/documenting-boating-experience-form-cg-719s

## Q: How is my Master tonnage decided, and how do I move from 50 to 100 ton?
The Coast Guard reads your whole sea service record and sets the tonnage from the size of boats you ran. MLS's rough guide: 360 days on boats under 17 GT gives 25 ton, 360 days on 17 GT or more gives 50 ton, 360 days on 34 GT or more gives 100 ton. For 50 to 100 ton Inland you need 90 days on 51 GRT or more or 180 days on 34 GRT or more; Near Coastal doubles that to 180 or 360 days. No exam is needed to raise tonnage on the same route, but changing from Inland to Near Coastal does need an extra exam and more sea time.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/25-50-or-100-ton-masters-general-information

## Q: Can I use my Master licence abroad?
MLS says the Master licence can be used in international waters, but a charter must start and end in US waters with the same paying passengers. Check whether the other country requires its own licence.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/25-50-or-100-ton-masters-general-information

## Q: How do I file the application now?
MLS describes the current Navita process: sign in through Login.gov, open a Merchant Mariner Credential (719B) application, choose the transaction type, add each endorsement with tonnage and route, answer the TWIC, criminal and drug questions, consent to the National Driver Register check, sign electronically, pay on Pay.gov and upload supporting documents as PDFs. The oath no longer needs notarising (since 17 December 2024). A third-party proxy must be an individual, not a company.
Note: this matches the official switch to Navita on 14 September 2026.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/application-for-licensing-form-cg-719b

## Q: What small mistakes in Navita hold applications up?
Choosing the wrong transaction type changes which requirements you are asked for. Wrong Social Security or Mariner Reference Numbers cause delays. A screenshot of the Pay.gov confirmation will not upload; you must download the actual PDF receipt, and the application stays incomplete until it is uploaded. Be specific about the exact endorsement you want.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/blogs/knowledge/application-for-licensing-form-cg-719b

## Q: What usually delays a captain's application?
MLS names sea time problems, such as missing signatures or wrongly added totals, as one of the most common causes, along with incomplete CG-719B or CG-719S forms, missing CG-719K medical paperwork, expired CPR, incomplete drug test records and open medical reviews. It gives no processing time and says it depends on NMC workload.
Note: the NMC reported the sea service form as the top "awaiting information" reason for professional qualifications in September 2026, and estimated about 4 months for complete applications in July 2026.
Source: https://marinerslearningsystem.com/pages/captains-license-application-process
