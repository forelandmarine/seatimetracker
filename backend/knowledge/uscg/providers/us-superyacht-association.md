# U.S. Superyacht Association: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of USSA public pages, written for a sea-time logging assistant. The binding rules for US mariner credentials are 46 CFR and National Maritime Center (NMC) guidance, not anything below.

Scope note: the USSA site is now at ussuperyacht.com (singular); the domain ussuperyachts.org did not resolve. As of 2026-10-25 the public USSA site has no pages on crew licensing, USCG credentials, STCW, MCA tickets or US-flag yacht manning. Those questions should be answered from NMC and 46 CFR notes elsewhere in this library.

## Q: What is the U.S. Superyacht Association?
It is a non-profit trade association for the American large-yacht industry, founded in 2006 and based in Fort Lauderdale, Florida. Its members are mostly businesses (yards, marinas, service companies, brokers) rather than individual crew. Its stated focus areas are advocacy, education, marketing and events.
Source: https://ussuperyacht.com/about

## Q: Does the USSA publish guidance on USCG licences for yacht crew?
No. Its public pages do not explain USCG credentials, sea service documentation, the CG-719S form or how yacht time counts toward a licence. For those topics, rely on the NMC and 46 CFR Part 10 and 11 notes in this library. The USSA lists the Coast Guard only as one of the federal agencies it talks to.
Source: https://ussuperyacht.com/advocacy

## Q: Does the USSA explain USCG versus MCA licences, or STCW for Americans on foreign-flag yachts?
Not on its public site. Nothing on its advocacy, about or Refit America pages compares USCG and MCA tickets or covers STCW for US citizens working on foreign-flag yachts. Older USSA captains' briefings are reported to have touched on STCW and MLC, but that material is not online in usable form, so we have nothing to cite.
Source: https://ussuperyacht.com/advocacy

## Q: Can a private yacht over 300 GT fly the US flag?
According to a 2018 USSA press release, a law signed on 13 August 2018 allowed private yachts over 300 gross tons to register in the United States as recreational vessels. Before that, the old yacht definition capped recreational registration at 300 GT, so bigger yachts had to register as commercial vessels or get a special Act of Congress. The release said that, as an interim measure, an MCA-compliant private yacht over 300 GT could fly the US flag while the Coast Guard drafted a US Large Yacht Code.
OUT OF DATE: the release gave 2020 as the target date for the Large Yacht Code. Check current Coast Guard rules before telling a user what code or manning standard applies to a large US-flag yacht today.
Source: https://www.newswire.com/view/content/us-flag-registry-now-a-reality-for-large-yachts-20602838

## Q: Does the USSA say what licences crew need on a US-flag yacht?
No. The 2018 flag release talks about more opportunities for American crew but does not set out manning levels, required credentials or STCW. Manning and credential requirements for US-flag vessels come from the Coast Guard, so use the official notes.
Source: https://www.newswire.com/view/content/us-flag-registry-now-a-reality-for-large-yachts-20602838

## Q: Is the USSA working on crew visas for foreign-flag yachts visiting the US?
Yes, at a general level. Its Refit America page says it works with the Coast Guard, Customs and Border Protection and the State Department to simplify vessel entry, streamline crew visa processing and reduce pilotage burdens. It gives no detail on how, and it is not a source of visa rules.
Source: https://ussuperyacht.com/refit-america

## Q: What is the 2026 visa bond rule the USSA warned about?
In April 2026 the USSA posted an advocacy update saying B-1/B-2 visitor visa applicants from a list of designated countries may have to post a refundable bond of 5,000, 10,000 or 15,000 US dollars. Bond holders must arrive and leave through designated commercial airports, not by sea, charter or private aircraft. The USSA expected limited impact on yacht crew because most come from visa waiver or non-listed countries, and the update did not say whether C-1/D crew visas are affected.
Source: https://ussuperyacht.com/news/new-cash-bond-tourist-visas

## Q: Does the USSA run crew training or careers programmes?
Its advocacy page lists Careers and Training as one pillar of its Refit America initiative, alongside small shipyard grants and maritime prosperity zones. This is aimed at the shore-side refit and repair workforce. The public pages do not describe a crew certification or licensing programme.
Source: https://ussuperyacht.com/advocacy

## Q: Has the USSA news feed covered crew licensing or USCG changes recently?
No. Its 2026 news list (19 items to September 2026) is mostly member business news, plus advocacy items on the visa bond and killer whale protection zones in British Columbia. There is nothing on USCG credentials, the Navita system or STCW. Users wanting licensing news should check NMC announcements instead.
Source: https://ussuperyacht.com/news
