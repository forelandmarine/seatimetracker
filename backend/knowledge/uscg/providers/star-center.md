# STAR Center: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of what STAR Center publishes. STAR Center itself says its interpretations are not authoritative: 46 CFR and the National Maritime Center (NMC) are binding.

## Q: Who runs STAR Center and can non-union mariners attend?
STAR Center in Dania Beach, Florida is part of the American Maritime Officers (AMO) training plan. Its upgrade pages give AMO members one registration form and non-AMO (commercial) students a separate online application, so commercial students can enrol, but AMO plan participants get waitlist priority. Prices for commercial students are given on request.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/stcw2010-deck.upgrade.html

## Q: Which courses does STAR Center list for Chief Mate or Master (3000 GT or more)?
Required: Advanced Shiphandling (2 weeks, includes Search and Rescue), Advanced Stability, Advanced Meteorology, Leadership and Managerial Skills (1 week each), and Management of Medical Care (half a day, not needed if you hold Medical Person in Charge). ECDIS, ARPA and GMDSS are needed only if you will serve on ships fitted with that equipment. Optional task courses (Advanced Cargo, Advanced Navigation, Marine Propulsion Plants, Advanced Celestial, 1 week each) are only needed for tasks not signed off on board. That is roughly 5 weeks of required training, up to about 9 weeks with all options.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/stcw2010-deck.upgrade.html

## Q: How are the management level deck assessments recorded?
STAR Center says the NVIC 10-14 framework has about 130 tasks. Some are covered in the required courses, the rest can be done on board or in an approved course, and all are logged in the NVIC 10-14 Record of Assessment, which goes to the Coast Guard once a Qualified Assessor has signed it.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/stcw2010-deck.upgrade.html

## Q: What courses are needed for First Assistant or Chief Engineer (management level) at STAR Center?
Required: Leadership and Managerial Skills, Engine Resource Management (skip if already taken for gap closing or your original licence), and Management of Electrical and Electronic Control Equipment, 1 week each. NVIC 15-14 tasks must also be completed, either on board or in optional STAR courses: Generals and Motors (5 days, all propulsion types), Steam (5 days) and Gas Turbine (3 days). The page was last updated in June 2024.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/stcw2010-engine.upgrade.html

## Q: Who can sign my STCW assessments, and what changes in 2028?
STAR Center says assessments must be signed by a Coast Guard approved Qualified Assessor under 46 CFR 10.405. Until 31 Dec 2028 the Coast Guard still accepts assessors who meet the professional requirements without formal approval, and assessments signed before 1 Jan 2029 by suitably experienced officers (at least one year as Master or Chief Mate on seagoing ships of 1600 GRT or 3000 GT) are accepted if submitted by 30 Jun 2029. After 2028, assessors must be NMC approved.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/stcw2010-engine.upgrade.html

## Q: How does an officer become a Qualified Assessor through STAR Center?
STAR Center offers an online QA course for eligible AMO members through its non-resident training system, built with the Ship Operations Cooperative Program. After finishing, the officer applies to the Coast Guard for QA approval under NVIC 19-14 Change 4. QA approval letters must be renewed every five years with evidence of recent assessment experience or training.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/qa.html

## Q: What is STAR Center's OICEW program for hawsepipe engineers?
It is a 17-week block for mariners who already hold the national Third Assistant Engineer (motor, unlimited) endorsement and want the STCW OICEW (III/1) endorsement. Courses are Engine Resource Management, Auxiliary Machinery, Motor Plants, Electrical Machinery and Basic Electronics, Control Systems, Medical Care Provider and Proficiency in Survival Craft. STAR Center quotes 1080 days in the engine room, including 720 as QMED or equivalent, to get the 3AE first, and four NVIC 17-14 tasks must be signed on board before starting.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/oicew.html

## Q: What does STAR Center say I need to renew Basic Training and Advanced Firefighting?
With 360 days of sea service on vessels that run regular fire and abandon ship drills, plus 1-day revalidation courses for Basic Training and Advanced Firefighting within 5 years of renewal, the Coast Guard renews the STCW safety endorsements, and Survival Craft is renewed with Basic Training. Without that sea time you need refresher courses instead: Basic Training refresher 3 days, Advanced Firefighting refresher 1.5 days, Survival Craft refresher 1 day.
Note: from 1 Oct 2029 (CG-MMC Policy Letter 01-26) original and renewal Basic Training also need an approved violence and harassment prevention course.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/stcw2010-renewal.html

## Q: How do I renew a Tankerman PIC endorsement according to STAR Center?
Either 90 days of tank vessel service in the last five years plus two cargo transfers of the endorsed type, or an approved tankerman course within five years. STAR runs 1-week and 2-week Tankerman PIC courses for mariners without recent tanker time.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/stcw2010-renewal.html

## Q: How do I submit applications now that Navita has launched?
STAR Center published an AMO quick guide (October 2026). Navita went live on 14 Sep 2026; you sign in with Login.gov (many mariners already have one from TWIC), choose the Mariner role, fill in your profile, and all applications go through Navita. The ASAP portal and the IASKNMC email have been switched off, though NMC phone and chat still work. The guide advises setting up two sign-in recovery methods, because losing access can force you to delete and recreate the Login.gov account, which takes about 24 hours.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/info/navita-application-procedure.pdf

## Q: How does the medical certificate (CG-719K) work in Navita?
Per the STAR guide, you start the 719K inside Navita (choose 719K, not 719E, and tick STCW if you hold STCW endorsements), sign it electronically, then print it and take it to an MD, DO, nurse practitioner or physician assistant (not a chiropractor). After the exam you upload the completed, signed form to the open application and submit. During early rollout the guide suggests uploading any manually completed 719K as well, as the Coast Guard had not issued transition guidance.
Source: https://www.star-center.com/info/navita-application-procedure.pdf
