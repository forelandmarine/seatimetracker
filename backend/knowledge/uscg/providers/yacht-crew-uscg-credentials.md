# Yacht crew and USCG credentials: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of public articles from a Fort Lauderdale yacht and commercial training school (MPT), a yacht crew magazine (Dockwalk, which ran the same MPT article) and a boating newspaper column (The Log), read in October 2026. The official sources are 46 CFR Part 11 Subpart C (STCW officer endorsements), 46 CFR 11.317 and 11.321, NVIC 13-14, the NMC STCW checklists and the Marine Safety Manual Vol III section on oceangoing yachts (msm-vol3-part-b-ch2-section-r-oceangoing-yachts.md). Where anything here differs, the official text wins. None of these sources discusses USCG time-and-a-half on yachts; for that see workboat-mm-seas-credentialing-columns.md and nmc-faq-time-half-credit.md (yachts are normally not two-watch vessels).

## Q: I am paid to skipper a private yacht abroad. Do I need STCW?
MPT and Dockwalk say yes: anyone paid to work on a vessel is a commercial mariner whatever the vessel, so a paid skipper taking a 50-foot yacht from Fort Lauderdale to Mexico is on an international voyage, and a foreign port state inspector can ask for STCW credentials. A US national license (for example the 100-ton Master) is a domestic-waters credential; for international voyages it needs an STCW endorsement added.
Note: the US issues STCW endorsements on the same MMC as national endorsements. Whether STCW applies to a particular yacht and voyage depends on the flag and whether the yacht is commercial; check with the flag state.

## Q: How do I add STCW to my 100-ton Master's license?
MPT explains the route as the STCW II/3 endorsement for Master of vessels under 500 GT limited to near-coastal waters, under 46 CFR 11.317 with assessments from NVIC 13-14. Training adds Medical First Aid Provider, Advanced Firefighting, Proficiency in Survival Craft, and Basic Training within the last five years; ECDIS, ARPA and GMDSS are only needed if the vessel has that equipment. Radar Observer is not itself required but the radar assessments are, so most people take a radar course. Sea time: 360 days as officer in charge of a navigational watch under your national license if you have been sailing as mate, or an extra 180 days if you have been sailing as master.
Note: this matches Table 1 to 46 CFR 11.317(d): no extra service for Mate under 500 GRT holders, 6 months for Master under 200 GRT, 12 months for Mate under 200 GRT. The current 11.317 also requires Leadership and Managerial Skills training. A Coast Guard proposed rule of 1 Oct 2026 (91 FR 62383) would remove that LMS requirement for this endorsement, but it is only proposed (see federal-register-2026-training-nprm-and-form-revisions.md).

## Q: Should an American yacht crew member go the USCG route or the MCA yacht route?
MPT says nationality no longer decides it. The USCG route is the American one; the MCA (UK) route has no nationality requirement; both apply the STCW Code in their own way. For a USCG deck career MPT outlines: about 6 months as deckhand for RFPNW, then Able Seafarer-Deck after a further 12 months holding RFPNW, then officer in charge of a navigational watch at 36 months total sea time, then Chief Mate and Master of 500 to 3,000 GT after further service as officer. On the MCA yacht side it outlines Officer of the Watch (Yachts) and Chief Mate (Yachts) under 3,000 GT after 36 months, then Master of Yachts 500 GT and 3,000 GT after 12 and 24 more months. For captains of charter boats or yachts under 200 tons, MPT notes there are quicker options (the national Master and OUPV licenses).
Note: USCG has no "Master of Yachts" endorsement; yacht crew use the ordinary national and STCW endorsements. MCA yacht certificates are issued by the UK, not the USCG. Check the NMC STCW checklists for exact USCG service and training.
Source: https://www.mptusa.com/news/how-to-choose-your-first-credential-what-when

## Q: Can I use my MCA Master of Yachts ticket to run a charter yacht in US waters?
The Log answered a reader who held an MCA 200-ton Master of Yachts and wanted to run a six-pack charter in California on a foreign-built yacht with a MARAD waiver. Its answer was no: any vessel carrying even one passenger for hire in US waters must be under the command of a master holding a USCG license, and the MCA certificate has no standing in US waters. The MARAD waiver only lets a foreign-built boat carry passengers; it does not change who may operate it. Insurers may set stricter rules.
Note: consistent with 46 CFR Parts 11 and 15: a vessel carrying passengers for hire needs a USCG credentialed operator (OUPV for up to six passengers on an uninspected vessel, Master for inspected vessels). The USCG does not convert MCA certificates, though foreign sea service can be credited under 46 CFR 10.232.
