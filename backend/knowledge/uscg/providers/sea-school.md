# Sea School: paraphrased fact notes

These are our own summaries of Sea School's public pages, read in October 2026. Sea School is a training provider: 46 CFR and current National Maritime Center (NMC) guidance are binding where anything here differs.

## Q: How do I document sea time on my own boat?
Sea School says you sign the CG-719S yourself as owner and attach proof of ownership, such as the state registration or Coast Guard documentation. On someone else's boat the owner signs, and the owner does not need to hold a licence. A company or charter operator can sign the form or write a sea service letter on letterhead showing vessel name and number, tonnage, route, your position and dates. Use one CG-719S per vessel.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/how-to-document-sea-time-for-a-captains-license

## Q: What counts as a day on a small boat?
On vessels under 100 GRT, Sea School says a day needs at least 4 hours underway in one calendar day, and the hours do not have to be continuous. You can only claim one day per calendar day, however many trips or boats. Recreational boating, fishing, crewing on other people's boats and paid deckhand work all count, on any waters. On vessels of 100 GT or more the normal day is 8 hours.
Note: this matches 46 CFR 10.107, where a day is 8 hours but the Coast Guard may accept 4 hours on vessels under 100 GRT.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/how-to-document-sea-time-for-a-captains-license
Source: https://seaschool.com/courses/master-mate-upgrade-200-grt

## Q: I have no logbook. Can I still claim my sea time?
Sea School says logs, GPS tracks and receipts are not required for small vessel service and the signed form is accepted. You may reconstruct your history month by month from memory, using seasons and typical trip frequency, but the form is signed under penalty of perjury, so estimates should be honest and on the conservative side. It recommends keeping a simple log from now on with date, vessel, hours underway and waters.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/how-to-document-sea-time-for-a-captains-license

## Q: What sea time does Sea School list for OUPV and Master under 100 GRT?
OUPV: 360 days since age 16, with 90 on ocean or near coastal waters for a Near Coastal route. Master Near Coastal: 720 days with at least 360 on ocean or near coastal waters. Master Inland or Great Lakes: 360 days. Sea School states that 90 days must fall within the last 3 years.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: CG-MMC Policy Letter 02-25 (September 2025) now accepts 90 days within the last 7 years for all national officer endorsements, including OUPV and Master.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/how-to-document-sea-time-for-a-captains-license

## Q: What are the common mistakes on sea service forms?
Sea School lists putting several vessels on one form, counting outings shorter than 4 hours, claiming two days for two trips on the same date, leaving out proof of ownership for your own boat, and sending a DD-214 instead of an official military sea service transcript. Failing the recency rule is the other common problem. Military time is credited at 60 percent of time aboard.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/how-to-document-sea-time-for-a-captains-license

## Q: What is the difference between OUPV and Master 100 ton?
OUPV covers uninspected vessels up to 100 GRT with no more than six paying passengers. Master covers inspected and uninspected vessels up to its tonnage (25, 50 or 100), but carrying seven or more passengers also needs an inspected vessel with a Certificate of Inspection. The licence alone does not lift the six-passenger limit on an uninspected boat.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: the Sea School comparison page says an OUPV Near Coastal reaches 200 miles offshore. An OUPV Near Coastal route is limited to 100 miles offshore; 200 miles applies to Master Near Coastal under 200 GRT.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/oupv-vs-master-100-ton-captains-license

## Q: What does a captain's licence cost in total?
Sea School quotes Coast Guard fees of $100 for the original endorsement plus $45 for issuance, $145 in total, paid on Pay.gov. All-in, including course, TWIC, drug test and physical, it estimates about $1,300 to $1,600 for OUPV and $1,600 to $1,900 for Master 100 ton (figures seen October 2026).
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/oupv-vs-master-100-ton-captains-license

## Q: Can I upgrade an OUPV to Master later?
Yes. Sea School says you build the extra sea time, take an approved upgrade course and amend your existing credential rather than starting again. Credentials must be renewed every five years.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/oupv-vs-master-100-ton-captains-license

## Q: What sea time do I need to go from Master 100 to Master 200 GRT?
Sea School lists Master 200 Near Coastal as 720 days with 360 near coastal, at least 360 days as Master, Mate or OUPV, and either 180 days on vessels over 101 GT or 360 days over 67 GT. Master 200 Inland is 360 days with 180 as Master, Mate or OUPV and 90 days over 101 GT or 180 days over 67 GT. Mate 200 Near Coastal is 360 days with 90 days over 101 GT or 180 over 67 GT. The upgrade course is 3 days (24 hours), priced at $700 in Florida and South Carolina and $975 at Bayou La Batre (October 2026). Applicants must be 19 and a US citizen for Master.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL: the page states 90 days in the last 3 years for recency. Policy Letter 02-25 accepts 90 days in the last 7 years.
Source: https://seaschool.com/courses/master-mate-upgrade-200-grt

## Q: Can I take the Coast Guard exam instead of a course?
Sea School says you may sit the exam at a Coast Guard Regional Exam Center instead, but not every centre offers it and you must be fully prepared on your own. Otherwise its approved course ends with an approved exam, and the completion certificate goes in with your application to the NMC.
Source: https://seaschool.com/courses/master-mate-upgrade-200-grt

## Q: Do I need all my sea time before I start the course?
No. Sea School says you do not have to document all your sea time before class, but recommends you can finish it within one year of completing the course. It cannot extend the date on a course certificate; hardship cases must be raised with the Coast Guard.
Source: https://seaschool.com/faq

## Q: What are the application steps and forms?
Sea School's list: choose the endorsement, get a TWIC, complete the CG-719B, add the CG-719C conviction statement if needed, pass a physical on the CG-719K, pass a drug test and join a random testing consortium, pay the fees, add CG-719S sea service and attach course certificates. Its forms page links to these forms, to application packets and to Pay.gov.
OUT OF DATE: since 14 September 2026 the CG-719B is completed online in Navita, which replaced the ASAP portal and IASKNMC email route. The drug test must be within 185 days.
Source: https://seaschool.com/faq
Source: https://seaschool.com/united-states-coast-guard-forms

## Q: My licence expired a while ago. Do I have to start again?
Sea School cites the Coast Guard's April 2024 memo giving a 6-year grace period after expiry in which you can renew without re-sitting the original exams. This agrees with Policy Letter 01-24. You still cannot work on an expired credential.
Source: https://seaschool.com/faq

## Q: How long does a TWIC take and what does it cost?
Sea School's August 2026 figures: $125.25 new or in-person renewal, $117.25 online renewal, $93 reduced rate with a valid HME or FAST card, $60 replacement. The card lasts five years. TSA advises enrolling at least 60 days before you need it, and Sea School plans on six to eight weeks.
Note: Policy Letter 11-15 exempts mariners who serve only on vessels with no security plan from holding a TWIC; Sea School's article does not mention this.
Source: https://seaschool.com/blog/how-to-get-a-twic-card-2026-guide-for-mariners
