Subj:  PROFESSIONAL EXAMINATION FOR MARINERS RAISING THE GRADE OF OFFICER
  ENDORSEMENTS FROM MASTER LESS THAN 500 OR 1,600 GROSS REGISTERED
  TONS TO THIRD MATE OF UNLIMITED TONNAGE

1.  PURPOSE. This policy allows mariners holding national officer endorsements as Master of Ocean
  or Near-Coastal Self-Propelled Vessels of Less Than 500 or 1,600 Gross Registered Tons (GRT)
  (Master 500/1600 GRT) to qualify for an endorsement as Third Mate of Ocean or Near-Coastal Self-
  Propelled Vessels of Unlimited Tonnage (Third Mate) without examination. The Coast Guard is
  publishing this policy in alignment with Administration priorities to ease burden on industry and
  provide ways to streamline mariner career progression.

2.  ACTION. The Coast Guard will use this policy when evaluating applications for a national officer
  endorsement as Third Mate from mariners holding a national endorsement as Master 500/1600 GRT.

3.  DIRECTIVES AFFECTED. Deck and Engineering Guide for the Administration of Merchant
  Marine Examinations.

### 4. BACKGROUND.

  a.  As specified in 46 CFR 11.903(a)(2) , mariners holding an endorsement as Master 500/1600 GRT
  must pass a professional examination to qualify for an endorsement as Third Mate.

  b.  The required examination subjects for deck officer endorsements are specified in 46 CFR
  11.910. The Third Mate examination includes some subjects that are not required on the
  examination for Master 500/1600. Accordingly, mariners holding an endorsement as Master
  500/1600 GRT are required to complete an examination when seeking an endorsement for Third
  Mate.

  c.  However, the examination subjects for Mate of Ocean or Near-Coastal Self-Propelled Vessels of
  Less Than 500 or 1,600 Gross Registered Tons (Mate 500/1600 GRT) and for Third Mate are the
  same. Mariners holding an endorsement for Mate 500/1600 GRT do not have to complete an
  examination when seeking an endorsement for Third Mate.

### 5. DISCUSSION.

  a.  The Coast Guard will allow mariners holding an endorsement for Master 500/1600 GRT Near
  Coastal or Oceans to qualify for Third Mate for the same route without further examination  .

  b.  This policy change will lessen the burden on mariners without jeopardizing marine safety. While
  there are some differences in the subjects required for Master 500/1600 GRT and for Third Mate,
  mariners holding an endorsement as Master 500/1600 GRT will have been tested on similar or
  superior subjects in connection with their current or previously held endorsements. For example,
  the examination for Third Mate Oceans includes determining a vessel’s latitude by meridian transit
  of the sun, while the examinationfor Master 500/1600 GRT Oceans includes determining the
  vessel’s latitude by meridian transit of any celestial body.

  c.  Mariners who hold Master 500/1600 GRT Near-Coastal who are applying for Third Mate Oceans
  will be required to take the examination for an increase in scope of Master 500/1600 GRT from
  Near Coastal to Oceans. As is specified in the Deck and Engineering Guide for the Administration
  of Merchant Marine Examinations, the required examination modules for an increase in scope
  from Near- Coastal to Oceans are Q124 -  Navigation General: Oceans  (70 questions / 70%
  minimum passing score) and Q127 -  Navigation Problems: Oceans  (15 questions / 80% minimum
  passing score).

  d.  Mariners who hold an endorsement as Master 500/1600 GRT and have been approved to test for
  Third Mate for the same route will not be required to complete the Third Mate examination. the
  National Maritime Center will issue the new endorsement with no action required from the
  applicant. Mariners who are approved for an increase in scope from Near Coastal to Oceans will
  have a revised approved to test letter issued with no action required from the applicant.

  e.  This policy will become  effective immediately.

### 7. ENVIRONMENTAL ASPECT AND IMPACT CONSIDERATIONS.

  a.  The development of this guidance and the general policies contained within it have been
  thoroughly reviewed by the originating office in conjunction with the Office of Standards
  Evaluation and Development, Commandant (CG-REG). Pursuant to Department of Homeland
  Security Directive 023-01, Rev.1, associated implementing instructions, and Environmental
  Planning COMDTINST 5090.1 (series), which guide the Coast Guard in complying with the
  National Environmental Policy Act of 1969 (42 U.S.C. 4321-  4370f), we have determined that this
  action is categorically excluded (CE) from further environmental analysis under paragraphs #A3,
  #L5 and #L56 in Appendix A, of Table 1 of DHS Directive Instruction Manual 023-  01, Rev. 1.
  Our rationale for selecting these CEs is as follows. “Promulgation of rules, issuance of rulings or
  interpretations, and the development and publication of policies … and other guidance … that
  implement, without substantive change, statutory or regulatory requirements” or “…that interpret
  or amend and existing regulation without changing its environmental effect “ are covered under
  DHS CE #A3. Promulgation of this policy and its associated guidance implements, without
  substantive change, the applicable Commandant Instruction or other federal agency regulations,
  procedures, manuals, and other guidance documents. Accordingly, Coast Guard CE #L5 applies. In
  addition, the policy letter includes elements that pertain to training, qualifying, licensing and
  disciplining maritime personnel. These latter aspects of the policy letter and its associated
  guidance are covered under Coast Guard CE #L56.

  b.  This guidance will not have any of the following: significant cumulative impacts on the human
  environment; substantial controversy or substantial change to existing environmental conditions;
  or inconsistencies with any Federal, State, or local laws or administrative determinations relating
  to the environment. All future specific actions resulting from the general policies in this guidance
  must be individually evaluated for compliance with the National Environmental Policy Act
  (NEPA), DHS and Coast Guard NEPA policy, and compliance with all other environmental
  mandates.
