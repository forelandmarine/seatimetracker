Ref:

(b) 46 CFR 7.105 (Boundary Line from Marquesas Keys, FL to Rio Grande, TX)
(C) Title 46 United States Code (46 U.S.C.) 2101(3) (General definitions)
(d) 33 U.S.C. 151(b) (High seas and inland waters demarcation lines, applicability of
other statues; limitation; positions)
(e) 33 CFR 2.05-10 (Territorial Sea Baseline definition)
1. This policy letter provides guidance to Regional Exam Centers when evaluating applications for
sea service credit where service is claimed for operation within the territorial seas and contiguous
zone of the Gulf of Mexico.
2. Licensed officers on board U.S. flag vessels must demonstrate sea service experience in order
to qualify for various grades of licenses. Such service must take place on certain routes (Inland
Waters, Rivers, Near coastal, Oceans, Great Lakes, etc.) before a license is endorsed to authorize
future service in the same or higher grade. Reference (a) defines these routes, and expressly defines
the term "Oceans" in relation to the specific Boundary Lines set forth in reference (b). Additional
information on Boundary Lines is provided in references (C) and (d).
3. For the Gulf of Mexico, the Boundary Line corresponds to the 12 mile line which marks the
seaward limits of the contiguous zone. This is much further offshore than the Boundary Line on
either the Atlantic or Pacific coasts. When evaluated for a raise in grade or an increase in scope,
mariners sailing within 12 miles of the U.S. Gulf Coast are penalized, with respect to establishing
sea service on Oceans waters, in comparison to many mariners serving on vessels operating in other
geographic areas.
4. For the purposes of crediting sea service in the Gulf of Mexico for the issuance of merchant
marine licenses and Merchant Mariner's Documents, the waters between the Territorial Sea
Baseline defined in reference (e) and the Boundary Line, are considered Oceans. For all other
purposes, the Boundary Line published in reference (b) applies.
