# NMC FAQ: NVIC 01-26 approved training

Source: https://www.dco.uscg.mil/Portals/9/NMC/pdfs/faq/nvic_01_26_faq.pdf

> National Maritime Center frequently asked questions on nvic 01-26 approved training.


[p. 1]

  Frequently Asked Questions:
  NVIC 01-26

1.  Am I required to use the format used in model courses of the International Maritime

### Organization (IMO)?

No.  The Coast Guard does not require course providers to use the format of IMO model
courses.  However, as described in NVIC 01  -26 Enclosure (1), the Coast Guard highly
encourages providers to follow the IMO  model course structure when practical.  Using this
format:

•  Helps ensure that required elements like scope, objectives, entry standards, and assessment
  methods are clearly presented.

•  Makes it easier for NMC reviewers to verify that your course meets applicable
  requirements.

•  Can reduce the likelihood of a dditional information (AI) requests and shorten overall
  processing times.

•  Provides a robust Detailed Teaching Syllabus contained in Part C of the IMO Model Course
  and ensures all content is covered.

If you choose not to use the IMO format, your submission must still include all required
elements described in NVIC 01-26, organized in the specified structure provided below.

### 3. Do I need to list the specific CFRs, STCWs, or NVICs my course satisfies?

Yes.  You should clearly specify the regulatory requirements your course is intended to satisfy.

In your Cover Letter (Folder 00), identify in general terms the approval requested, e.g.,  “STCW
Basic Training” or “examinations for endorsement as Master 100 GRT Near Coastal”.

In your Detailed Teaching Syllabus (DTS) (Folder 03), clearly map learning objectives and
topics to those requirements.

NOTE:  46 CFR 10.402 pertains to course or program structure and format; it is not an
acceptable reference for satisfying the regulatory training requirements of a course or
program.

### 4. For an Original course submittal, do I need to submit exams and assessment forms?

Yes.  NVIC 01-26 Encl (1) section 3.a.(8) requires the submission of assessment instruments,
including:

•  Written examinations and answer keys.

•  Quizzes and their answer keys.

[p. 3]

•  Practical assessment checklists, with a detailed explanation of how each assessment will be
  performed.

•  Simulator scenarios and evaluation criteria, if used.

•  Any other tools used to determine if learning objectives have been met.

For courses substituting for Coast Guard exams, these assessments must be of sufficient
difficulty that a successful student would likely pass a Coast Guard exam on the first attempt,
per 46 CFR 10.403(a)(4)–  (5).

### 5. What level of detail is required for “Learning Objectives”?

Learning objectives indicate the expected outcome of the training.  They are assumed to be
prefaced with the statement “The expected training outcome is that the trainee will be able
to...”

The learning objectives should use appropriate action verbs that indicate what the trainee will
be expected to perform to evidence achieving the objective.  Appropriate action verbs for
practical demonstrations would include “demonstrate”,  “perform”,  or “carry out”.  For
knowledge-based competencies and knowledge, understanding and proficiency (KUP),
appropriate action verbs would include “describe”,  “explain”,  “list”,  and “identify”.  A taxonomy
of action verbs can be found in Appendix 4 of the Annex to IMO Circular MSC -
MEPC.2/Circ.15/Rev.3, Guidelines for the Development, Review and Validation of Model
Courses.  This document is available under circulars for the “MSC -MEPC” committees.Please
note, you will need a public account to view this document.

The detailed outline should group learning objectives as they will be presented in the course
using headings indicating the topics and sub-topics they will apply to.  The format used in any
of the several model courses from the IMO are examples of how a detailed outline or detailed
teaching syllabus should be constructed.

### 6. Are lesson plans and instructor notes still required, and how will they be used?

Yes, they are still required, but they are no longer required to be submitted.  As explained in
NVIC 01-26 Enclosure (1), section 3.b, lesson plans and instructor notes “should be prepared
by the training provider and made available for inspection or audit but need not be submitted
with a request for approval.”  This means they must be maintained at the training site.  They
may be reviewed during audits to confirm the course is being delivered as approved.

[p. 4]

### 7. How long is my course or program approval valid?

In accordance with  46 CFR 10.402 (d),  an approval is valid for 5 years , unless:

1.  The training provider ceases operation;

2.  The training provider gives notice that it will no longer offer the course;

3.  The owner or operator fails to submit any required information.

8.  If I submit a renewal request before my course approval expires, does my current
  approval remain valid?

Yes.  NVIC 01-26 Enclosure (1), section 7.b states:  “If a training provider submits an
application for renewal of a training course or program prior to its expiration, the prior
approval will remain valid until final agency action on the application for renewal in
accordance with 46 CFR 1.03-15 or 1.03-40.”  This means your existing approval remains valid
and will not lapse while the renewal is under review and any subsequent reconsideration and
appeal actions are completed.  Providers should submit renewal requests at least 90 days
before expiration.

9.  Do we need to record changes to documents NOT submitted to the NMC (like lesson
  plans)?

Yes.  Any alterations to documents such as lesson plans, exams, and other internal training
materials are maintained within the requirements of your Quality Standard System (QSS) and
should be tracked in your internal Record of Changes or similar document.  These documents
should be available for NMC audit and remain consistent with the approved course curricula.

10. When do I need to notify the NMC and obtain authorization for changes to an
  approved course?

Per 46 CFR 10.402(e) and NVIC 01-  26 Encl (1) section 5, you must notify the NMC and obtain
authorization before making significant changes, such as:

•  Changes to comply with new or amended policies and regulations.

•  Major changes to course structure or detailed outline or DTS:  Adding or removing
  modules, changing contact hours, or substantially altering examinations or assessments.

[p. 5]

•  Significant delivery changes:  Adding distance or blended learning or changing the mix of
  classroom and practical training.

•  Site-related changes:  New training locations, or materially different conditions for practical
  exercises.

•  Changes to approved training equipment:  Such as a new lifeboat, davit, or simulator.

### 11. Are my training and exam sites part of my course approval?

Yes.  All locations where you deliver approved training or administer exams are part of your
approval.  You must list all locations in your application and provide details for any new site.
Per NVIC 01-  26, site changes are considered significant and must be submitted to the NMC for
review and approval before use.  Requests may be sent to NMCCOURSES@uscg.mil .

12. How many a  dditional information (AI) opportunities will the NMC provide, and what
  is the response timeframe?

For both original approvals and renewals, the NMC will generally issue only one AI letter per
application.  Providers have 45 days from the date of the letter to submit a complete response.
If the response is late or unresponsive, the NMC may deny or close the application.

### 13. What types of AI responses will be treated as non- responsive?

An AI response may be considered non-  responsive if it is:

•  Partial:  Does not address all items in the AI letter.

•  Generic and/or disorganized:  Uses broad language that does not specifically address the
  identified issue(s).

•  Scope-changing:  Introduces a “significant change” that should have been submitted as a
  modification to an approved course or program.

[p. 6]

14. Where do I submit student course completion certificates to meet the new 5-  day
  requirement?

The implementation of the electronic submission requirement for mariner course completion
data in 46 CFR 10.403(a)(7) is delayed.  Do not send completion certificates to the NMC
Course Approval inbox at this time.  Continue normal record-keeping until further notice.  The
Coast Guard will provide additional guidance to training providers to facilitate meeting the
requirement prior to the effective date.

### 15. How does the 5- day completion rule work for students who need a retest?

When the electronic submission requirement for mariner course completion data is
implemented, it applies after the successful completion of all course requirements, including
any retests.  You must submit a student’s completion data within 5 days of the successful retest
date.

### 16. How do we handle recordkeeping for foreign students without an MRN or Real ID?

For students without a USCG Mariner Reference Number (MRN), providers must record their
full name, date of birth, and place of birth, or a valid passport/foreign national ID number.
This information must be sufficient to uniquely identify the student and support electronic
submissions when required.

### 17. How do I update my training provider’s contact information with the NMC?

E-mail all updates to NMCCOURSES@uscg.mil .  The e-mail should clearly identify your
provider name and code,  and include the updated point of contact, e-mail, phone number, and
mailing address.

### 18. Can audit findings or prior NMC feedback affect future course decisions?

Yes.  The NMC may consider a provider’s entire compliance history (including audit findings,
QSS implementation, and responses to prior AI letters) when reviewing applications.  A failure
to implement previously directed corrective actions may result in the denial of a future
application.
