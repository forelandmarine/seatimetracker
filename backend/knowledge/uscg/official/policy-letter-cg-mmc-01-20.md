Subj: AFFIDAVITS USED IN CONJUNCTION WITH MARINER CREDENTIALING
Ref: (a) Title 46 Code of Federal Regulations 10.229
1. Purpose. To provide guidance on the affidavit submitted to the Coast Guard when requesting
a duplicate Merchant Mariner Credential (MMC) or Mariner Medical Certificate.
2. Background
a. In accordance with reference (a), mariners who request a duplicate MMC or medical
certificate must submit an affidavit describing the circumstances resulting in the loss of their
credential as part of the application.
b. Duplicate credentials may be issued after the Coast Guard confirms the validity of the
credential.
3. Directives Affected. None.
4. Discussion.
a. As provided in reference (a), a mariner submitting an application for a duplicate
credential to replace a lost MMC or mariner medical certificate must provide an affidavit
describing the circumstances of the loss. The standard credential application process is
abbreviated because no additional authority is being given to the applicant.
b. The affidavit may be submitted in writing or electronically. When submitted, this
information will be presumed to be true and correct to the best of the applicants ability. This
will be presumed whether the affidavit is signed and submitted thru the mail or electronically
within an email or an attachment to an email.
As part of the Coast Guard's responsibility under Title 46 United States Code (USC)
7101 and 7302, the Coast Guard may verify the information within the affidavit as part of the
evaluation of the application for a duplicate credential.

d. Consistent with Title 46 Code of Federal Regulations (CFR) 5.57(b), when submitting an
application for a duplicate credential a mariner is considered to be acting under the authority
of their credential. Any fraudulent action taken as part of that application may subject the
mariner to the suspension and revocation of their MMC.
e. Furthermore, when submitting the affidavit to the Coast Guard, the mariner is affirming
that all information provided is true and correct to the best of their knowledge. Where the
Coast Guard determines the mariner has submitted materially false information within the
affidavit the Coast Guard may refer the matter to the U.S. Attorney for action under Title 18
USC 1001.
5. Action.
a. This policy is effective immediately. The National Maritime Center shall be guided by
the information in this policy letter.
b. This policy is posted along with other CG-MMC policy letters on the Coast Guard's
Homeport internet website at http://homeport.uscg.mil by selecting the following links:
Library > Policy > Policy Letters > credentialing.
6. Disclaimer: This guidance is not a substitute for applicable legal requirements, nor is it itself
a rule. It is not intended to nor does it impose legally-binding requirements on anyone. It
represents the Coast Guard's current thinking on this topic.
