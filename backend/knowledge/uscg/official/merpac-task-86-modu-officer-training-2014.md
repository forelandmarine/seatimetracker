# MERPAC Task Statement 86 report (2014) and the 2014 MODU and OSV training ANPRM

Sources:
- MERPAC Task Statement 86 Report: https://www.regulations.gov/document/USCG-2013-0175-0022 (PDF: https://downloads.regulations.gov/USCG-2013-0175-0022/attachment_1.pdf)
- ANPRM, Training of Personnel and Manning on Mobile Offshore Units and Offshore Supply Vessels Engaged in U.S. Outer Continental Shelf Activities, 79 FR 20844: https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2014/04/14/2014-08359/training-of-personnel-and-manning-on-mobile-offshore-units-and-offshore-supply-vessels-engaged-in-us
- Docket: https://www.regulations.gov/docket/USCG-2013-0175 (7 public comments, including an IADC letter of 29 May 2014 that only asked for more time to comment)

## How the assistant should use this (our summary)

- Industry offshore courses and USCG endorsements: MERPAC noted that offshore workers of all kinds take industry safety orientation such as SafeGulf, HUET, RigPass and BOSIET. These are industry or IADC/OPITO-style programs for persons other than crew. They are not Coast Guard approved courses and do not count toward OIM, Barge Supervisor or BCO endorsements; those need the Coast Guard approved stability, survival suit and survival craft, and firefighting courses in 46 CFR 11.470 to 11.474 (see nmc-approved-courses-rivers-fishing-modu-extract-2026.md for who offers them).
- The Coast Guard said in 2014 that the training in 46 CFR 11.470, 11.472 and 11.474 "is not equivalent to the STCW's basic safety training requirements for maritime crew." An OIM who also needs an STCW Master or Chief Mate endorsement has to meet STCW Basic Training separately.
- MERPAC's views (2014, advisory): the current OIM, BS and BCO structure is adequate; no Master (MODU) endorsement is needed; existing equivalencies for unlimited Masters and Chief Mates are adequate; a Chief Engineer should be required only on self-propelled MODUs; 1.5 days of credit should be allowed for 12-hour watches on DP units; Able Seaman-MODU should be removed for non-self-propelled units; and industry training certificates should be enough proof for non-mariners rather than USCG credentials.
- None of this became a rule. Do not present the recommendations as current requirements.

## ANPRM extracts (79 FR 20844, 14 Apr 2014)

On MODU industrial officers (section III):

> The Coast Guard issues officer endorsements for three categories of industrial officers who work on U.S.-flagged MODUs. These are the offshore installation manager (OIM), barge supervisor (BS), and ballast control operator (BCO). Regulations for training and sea service requirements for the OIM, BS, and BCO are found in 46 CFR 11.470, 11.472, and 11.474, respectively. Depending on the type of unit the three categories of industrial officers are working on these officers may also hold a maritime credential as a Master or Chief Mate, which would subject them to compliance with the STCW basic safety training requirements. Coast Guard regulations contained in Sec. Sec. 11.470, 11.472, and 11.474 require some safety-related training courses for these three categories of industrial officers, which include well control/blowout prevention for the OIM, as well as survival suits/survival craft, and firefighting training for all three categories of industrial officers. However, the training in Sec. Sec. 11.470, 11.472, and 11.474 is not equivalent to the STCW's basic safety training requirements for maritime crew.

On officers on MODUs (section IV.C):

> We also seek information on any current method or program for training a person holding an unlimited Master's endorsement to prepare them to obtain an OIM endorsement. This includes suggestions on academic degrees, in addition to engineering degrees and sea service requirements, or other creditable experience in lieu of those expressly stated in 46 CFR 11.470. We welcome suggestions regarding the application of credit toward the OIM requirements for any training courses or programs received while obtaining a Master's endorsement (e.g., firefighting, stability, and survival suit training) and ask for data on the number of OIMs currently holding a Master's endorsement.
>
> The subjects that will appear on an examination for obtaining a U.S. credential with OIM, BS, and BCO MODU endorsements are specified in 46 CFR 11.920.

Questions asked (section V.C), in part: Q-C4 whether the officer endorsement structure for MODUs is still valid; Q-C5 whether to create Master (MODU), Chief Mate (MODU) or Mate (MODU) endorsements; Q-C8 whether a Master or Mate (unrestricted) must start over to meet 11.470, 11.472 and 11.474; Q-C9 equivalencies for an OIM holding unlimited Master and a BS/BCO holding unlimited Chief Mate; Q-C10 whether, on a self-propelled US MODU, the Master with an OIM endorsement required by 46 CFR 15.520(d) actually fills the OIM role; Q-C12 whether new credentialed positions are needed (crane operator, ROV operator, maintenance supervisor).

## Full text: MERPAC Task Statement 86 Report

[p. 1]
Merchant Personnel Advisory Committee (MERPAC)
Task Statement 86 Report
Recommendations on the Coast Guard's Advance Notice of Proposed Rulemaking (ANPRM) for
Training of Personnel and Manning on Mobile Offshore Units (MOUs) and Offshore Supply Vessels
(OSVs) Engaged in U.S. Outer Continental Shelf (OCS) Activities
Summary
MERPAC met on September 11th, 2014 to review and finalize recommendations prepared by its working
group that reviewed the ANPRM regarding training and manning for MOUs and OSVs. MERPAC focused
its recommendations specifically on the manning and training aspects of the ANPRM and did not
address the information gathering and operational aspects of the ANPRM.
After the discussion, the following recommendations were accepted by the full Committee and
presented to the Coast Guard for consideration.
Report and Recommendations
MERPAC answered the following questions from the ANPRM and made additional recommendations.
Q-A1. Is Table 1 (adapted from information in IMO Resolution A.1079(28)]) a good representation of the
levels of training appropriate for the categories of persons other than crew listed?
Currently, offshore personnel of all categories take safety orientation programs such as Safe Gulf,
Helicopter Underwater Escape Training (HUET), RigPass, Basic Offshore Safety Induction Emergency
Training (BOSIET) or industry accepted trainings such as the Petroleum Education Council's Core
Compliance program. There are other programs as applicable with respect to industrial safety
requirements, too.
Table 1 is a good representation as laid out. However, there may be further discussion required to
refine categories of persons other than crew and their required training, particularly for non-MODU
vessels. For example, on an OSV with contract personnel who are not crew members but may be there
for a period of more than 3 days, they would not be a member of a fire team and firefighting may not be
necessary, though fire prevention would be.
Q-A2. What suggestions do you have regarding the inclusion or modification of the relevant maritime
safety training in the table?
In addition to the comments above, HUET training should be included for all categories.
Q-A3. Should any key maritime crew or persons other than crew on board be required to take crowd
management training, and crisis management and human behavior training courses (similar to maritime
crew and persons other than crew on passenger vessels)? For what size complement? For what type of
vessel? How do existing FLOATELs/ASVs ensure the safety of Regularly assigned persons otherthan crew
with designated responsibility for the safety and survival of others. Training in offshore orientation;
familiarization training or sufficient information and instruction in personal survival techniques and
workplace safety. Training in personal survival, fire prevention and fire-fighting, elementary first aid,
[p. 2]
personal safety and social responsibilities (as set out in tables 5.3.1 to 5.3.5, BT of Resolution A.891).
Additional training in accordance with their duties and responsibilities-STCW training in survival craft
and rescue boats, fast rescue boats, adv. Fire-fighting, and medical first aid. [Person in charge (PIC)
medical care, if assigned]. Familiarization training on unit-specific equipment large numbers of
embarked persons other than crew in case of emergency?
Presently, A.1079(28) recommends that the OIM possess crowd control and crisis management training.
Category C personnel who are given crowd control responsibilities in an emergency should receive the
crowd control and crisis management training as well.
Q-A4. Is there any specialized safety training that should be required on OSVs that is particular to the
various functions these vessels can perform?
No maritime training, only training required for industrial safety (i.e. divers, etc.).
C. Officers on Mobile Offshore Drilling Units
Q-C4. Is the current structure of officer endorsement (licensing) for MODUs still valid and does it cover
the current and anticipated future needs of the offshore drilling/production industry?
Yes, MERPAC feels it is appropriate and adequate.
Q-C5. Should the Coast Guard consider issuing a Master (MODU)-specific endorsement? Is there need
for a ‘‘Chief mate (MODU)'' or ‘‘Mate (MODU)'' endorsement?
No, MERPAC does not feel that this necessary.
Q-C9. What are your suggestions regarding the acceptance of equivalencies of the education (degree),
and individual course and/or program requirements for: (a) An OIM (who holds an unlimited Master's
officer endorsement); and (b) A BS/BCO (who holds an unlimited Chief mate's officer endorsement)?
MERPAC feels that the existing equivalencies in the regulations are adequate.
Q-D1. Should the Coast Guard require a Chief engineer aboard a MODU? If so, how many assistant
engineers should be required and what would be the associated costs and benefits?
On a self-propelled MODU, a Chief Engineer is already required. On a non self-propelled MODU, there
should not be a requirement for a Chief Engineer. The Maintenance Supervisor position is adequate and
should be considered as suggested by A.1079(28). The Maintenance Supervisor is used by other flag
states and should be used instead of Chief Engineer (MODU).
Q-D2. Should the Coast Guard require a Chief mate aboard a MODU? If so, how many additional mates
should be required and what would be the associated costs and benefits?
A Chief Mate should only be required on self-propelled MODUs (where they are already required).
Q-D3. Are there any other manning issues regarding both self-propelled and non-self-propelled MOUs
that industry recommends the Coast Guard address?
MERPAC recommends that sea service credit at 1.5 days per day worked should be acceptable for those
mariners working 12 hour watches. Many watchstanders on DP MOUs work 12 hour watches on a vessel
in DP mode and should be awarded 1.5 day credit.
For non self-propelled MOUs, MERPAC recommends removing the requirement for Able Seaman-
MODU. This credential is currently not authorized under regulation.
Q-D4. Are there any manning issues regarding OSVs that industry recommends the Coast Guard
address?
[p. 3]
No.
GENERAL COMMENTS
While MERPAC feels that Basic Training as defined by A.1079(28) is appropriate for those levels
indicated in Table 1, it strongly recommends that certification in the form of training certificates be
considered adequate for proof of training and that non-mariners not be required to receive credentials
from the USCG/NMC. To require credentialing would necessitate TWICs, which could be highly
problematic for foreign crews. Additionally, this would create a substantial additional burden upon the
already stretched resources at the National Maritime Center.
To facilitate compliance with BSEE SEMS rules, MERPAC recommends that it should be the responsibility
of the employer to ensure that their personnel are appropriately trained and qualified. As such, any
requirement to verify training has been met should fall upon the employer and not the OSV/MOU crew
members. Validation of this training should be done at the leaseholder's dispatch location.
