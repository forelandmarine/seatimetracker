# Seafarer certification: annual statistics (yacht focus)

Source: https://www.amsa.gov.au/seafarer-certification-annual-statistics

## What the page covers

AMSA publishes point-in-time counts of valid certificates it has issued, for near coastal (domestic) and STCW (international) seafarers, with detailed PDF reports by certificate, age and gender. AMSA says: many seafarers hold more than one certificate; some hold two of the same certificate during renewal overlap; some data was inherited from other agencies when the national system started; and naming changes mean some certificates are grouped.

## Notes for the assistant

- Fewer than about 25 AMSA-issued yacht CoCs were active at the end of 2025, against about 2,545 STCW deck CoCs. The yacht ladder exists and is used, but rarely. Most Australians working on superyachts overseas hold flag-State (often UK MCA) yacht CoCs, which these tables do not count.
- No Watchkeeper Deck <3000 GT (Yachts) row appears in either year. It may be zero or grouped elsewhere; AMSA does not say.
- Certificates of recognition are excluded, so these tables cannot tell how many MCA yacht CoC holders hold an AMSA certificate of recognition. AMSA's FOI disclosure log lists a 29 October 2025 release "Maritime Qualification Statistics: data regarding active seafarer certificates" (reference 2025/8022), available only by emailing freedomofinformation@amsa.gov.au; its content was not seen.
- No AMSA annual report from 2020 onward was found to publish oral exam numbers or pass rates (the 2024-25 annual report was checked). Older annual reports (2015-16 and earlier) reportedly gave oral exam totals and pass rates, but those were not checked and are too old to quote as current.
