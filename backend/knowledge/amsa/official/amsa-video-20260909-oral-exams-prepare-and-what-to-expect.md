# AMSA oral exams: how to prepare and what to expect (video transcript)

Source: https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=2pjH78rAJPE (embedded at https://www.amsa.gov.au/qualifications-training/oral-exams/prepare-your-oral-exam)

## What the video description says it covers

What an AMSA oral exam involves, how to book, how to use the syllabus and examiner checklist as study tools, what examiners look for, common knowledge gaps and mistakes, how to structure answers, tips for online and face-to-face exams, and what happens after the exam and how decisions are made.

## Transcript

### What the exam is (00:00)

If you are preparing for an oral examination with the Australian Maritime Safety Authority, AMSA, or plan to get a certificate of competency that requires one in the future. This video will explain what to expect and how to prepare for your exam effectively. An oral examination with AMSA is a final competency assessment before you can be issued an international certificate under the International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers, STCW, or a high complexity domestic certificate under Marine Order 505. During the exam, the examiner will confirm your identity, explain the process and outline how the exam will run. This is a normal part of the assessment, and helps you settle in before the formal questions begin. Once the introduction is complete, the examiner will move into the main part

(01:03) of the exam. The assessment will involve a series of questions and real-world scenarios. During the exam, the examiner will check whether your training and your sea service have given you the knowledge, decision making skills and practical ability to operate a commercial vessel safely.

### Booking (01:03 to 04:00)

Before you can book an oral examination, your sea service must first be assessed by AMSA. The application process differs for STCW and near coastal certificates. Use the AMSA 419 form for STCW certificates of competency or recognition, and the AMSA 426 for near coastal. If your application meets the requirements for the certificate you are applying for, you will receive an eligibility notice by email.

(02:00) This notice will explain how to book and pay for your examination. Most oral examinations are held online using Microsoft Teams. Face to face examinations are also available at selected AMSA offices including Sydney, Melbourne, Devonport, Hobart, Adelaide, Fremantle, Darwin, Newcastle, Townsville and Brisbane. Not all exam type[s] and levels are offered at these locations. Be sure to check which AMSA office offers your exam before booking. Some registered training organisations may request a group of examinations to be held at their site, commonly known as a block booking. Once you receive your eligibility notice, you can choose the available time that

(03:05) suits you. In busy periods, the wait time for an available booking slot can be up to three months. We recommend booking your oral exam as close to completing your course as possible. Candidates who sit their exams soon after training typically perform better. The examination itself is usually scheduled for about 90 minutes. This examination should not be treated as a practise run. It is important to prepare thoroughly before you book your test. Failing the exam can delay your ability to get work and start earning money, so aim to pass the first time and avoid the long waits involved in resitting the exam.

### How to prepare (03:05 to 07:00)

Now that you know the purpose of the examination, let's talk about how to prepare effectively. In the oral examination, examiners expect you to show strong

(04:02) foundation knowledge for the qualification you are working towards. Start with two essential documents. The syllabus for your certificate of competency and the examiner checklist. Both can be downloaded from the AMSA website at www.amsa.gov.au/oral-exams. On the checklist, each topic is marked A, B or C. A means the topic is safety critical and essential to pass. B means the topic requires thorough operational knowledge. C means the topic needs general knowledge. Then review the syllabus. The syllabus sets out the skills and knowledge you must meet for the certificate you are applying for. Your preparation should begin well before you book the oral examination.

(05:00) Ideally you should start preparing during your course and while gaining sea service across a variety of vessel types if possible. During the examination the examiner may ask about the same concept in different ways. So focus on understanding, not memorising a scripted answer. This is not a memory test. The examiner is looking for applied understanding, how you would act in real situations. There is a lot of content to cover in a 90 minute exam. A successful exam depends on answering succinctly and with relevance, without unnecessary detail. Taking a moment to think before you respond helps you stay focused and clear. This mirrors real world communication on board vessel, where concise, accurate information is essential. When you are given a scenario, structure your answer. Think about what you would do or check, why it matters, what subsequent action you would take, what you would delegate,

(06:00) reporting requirements, and record keeping. If you need a moment to think, that's okay. Sometimes you might choose to draw or sketch something to support your answer. That's okay too. If you didn't hear a question properly, or you're not sure what was asked, don't hesitate to ask the examiner to repeat or rephrase it. This is completely acceptable and ensures that you respond accurately. Make sure you prepare using proper, reliable sources of information, your course textbooks, AMSA resources, and guidance from your RTO. In an oral examination, your spoken explanation is what is assessed. So as you study, practise speaking out loud. Set up mock examinations with colleagues, course mates, friends or family. You can also record yourself answering questions, then listen back. Study groups can help too. Your peers may have experience on different vessel types and that can strengthen your understanding.

(07:00) Remember, a certificate of competency is not tied to one vessel type. You may be asked to respond to scenarios on different types of vessels and in the capacity for which you're being assessed. Examiners want you to succeed. If there are gaps in your knowledge, it is better to find them in the examination than at sea, where the safety risks are real for crew, passengers, the vessel and the marine environment.

### Common knowledge gaps (07:00 to 09:30)

Over time, examiners have identified some common knowledge gaps among candidates who do not pass. For deck certificates one of the most significant areas is the international regulations for preventing collisions at sea, COLREGS. Many candidates can recite the rules but struggle to demonstrate how they apply them in real situations.

(08:01) Other common gaps include understanding relevant legislation and marine orders, knowing the correct operational areas for domestic commercial vessels, understanding the principles of the International Convention on Load Lines, and applying stability principles correctly, including the proper use of the stability book. For near coastal candidates in particular, a common issue is focusing too much on advanced stability topics such as free surface effect, before mastering the basics of stability. For engineer certificates, the gaps examiners see are less about complex theory and more about practical real world decision making in the engine room. Candidates often struggle to clearly explain step by step how they would

(09:03) respond to alarms and emergencies, such as boiler low water, gauge glass failures, fires or machinery trips. Other common areas include diagnosing basic machinery faults like high exhaust temperatures, smoke or loss of power, understanding statutory certificates, safety management systems and maritime legislation, explaining the purpose and operation of main engine systems, alarms and protection devices and demonstrating good watch keeping practises, safe electrical working procedures and sound decision making under pressure.

### On the day (09:30 to 11:40)

On the day of your examination aim to be well rested. Feeling a little nervous is normal, you have worked hard to get to this point.

(10:00) If your examination is online, set up early. Test Microsoft Teams, your camera, your microphone, and your internet connection. Your recommended connection speed is at least 10 megabits per second. If your examination is face to face, arrive early. Give yourself extra time for travel, parking, and settling in. Have your photo identification and your eligibility notice ready to show the examiner. For online examinations, choose a quiet, well-lit room. Clear your desk, reference materials are not permitted during the exam. Only these items are allowed. Your laptop or computer, a white board or blank paper, a pen, a ruler, a calculator and a drink of water. Join the meeting at least 15 minutes early. If you don't join before the scheduled start time, it may be recorded as a failure. Keep your upper body and hands visible at all times. At the start you will be asked to pan your camera 360° to show the room.

(11:04) Do not use a smartphone during the examination, even as a mobile hotspot. If your Internet connection fails and the examination cannot continue, you may need to book again and pay again. Examiners are trained to notice signs that examination conditions are not being followed. This can include off screen prompts, unusual eye or hand movements, or unexpected background sounds. If the examiner believes the conditions are not being met, they reserve the right to stop the exam and record the attempt as a fail. The same standards apply to every candidate.

### After the exam (11:40 to 13:00)

If you do not succeed, you will be given valuable feedback at the end of your exam. Use that feedback to update and review your study plan using the syllabus and checklist before you [re]book.

(12:01) After three unsuccessful attempts, the fourth attempt may require you to attend face to face. If you are assessed as competent, [then] for near coastal certificates, your application will continue to be processed once your pass result is lodged by the examiner. [For] international certificates under the International Convention on Standards of Training, Certification and Watchkeeping for Seafarers, STCW, you will receive your pass results so you can formally apply for the certificate using the AMSA 419 form available from the AMSA website. Ensure you provide copies of all required supporting documents as listed on the AMSA 419 personalised checklist. Remember incomplete applications or those missing documentation [will] not be accepted.

### Summary (13:00 to end)

To summarise, here are the key points to remember as

(13:02) you prepare. Start preparing well before you book the oral examination. Use [the] syllabus and the examiner checklist as your study guide. Develop a real world understanding of all aspects of your job, not memorised answers. Build experience across different vessel types, especially for deck certificates. Use study groups, practise mock exams and consider recording yourself speaking so you can improve. Practise explaining your answers clearly because your spoken explanation is what the examiner assesses. Visit www.amsa.gov.au/oral-exams to download the checklist and syllabus for your certificate.

(14:02) Prepare well, join the examination ready and demonstrate your competence with confidence. All the best and good luck with your oral examination.

## Notes for the assistant

- The A, B, C topic marking is described for examiner checklists. AMSA publishes no examiner checklist for the four MO 74 yacht certificates (only syllabus Appendices 11 and 12), so yacht candidates have no A, B, C marking from AMSA. Do not invent one.
- "Build experience across different vessel types" and "a certificate of competency is not tied to one vessel type" matter for yacht candidates: expect scenarios beyond the yacht they served on.
- Sea service assessment comes first, then the oral, then (for STCW, which includes yacht certificates and certificates of recognition) the form 419 application with the pass result.
