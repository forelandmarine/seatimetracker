# Superyacht Australia: paraphrased fact notes

Our own summary of Superyacht Australia's crew pages (now hosted on australianmarine.org, which groups Superyacht Australia with AIMEX and other marine associations) and a news report on its crew guide. It is not their text. The old superyachtaustralia.com.au address did not resolve. The pages do not cover AMSA certificates; for those use Marine Orders 70, 74 and 76 and AMSA's pages.

## Q: Who is Superyacht Australia and what does it publish for crew?
It is the national industry body for the Australian superyacht sector. Its crew page offers a downloadable careers booklet covering what a superyacht is, seasons and cruising areas, mandatory qualifications and certificates, onboard departments and roles, and Australian training providers and recruiters.
Source: https://australianmarine.org/superyacht-careers-information/new-superyacht-crew/

## Q: What did Superyacht Australia's 2023 guide say about getting started?
As reported in September 2023: entry crew need the STCW basic safety qualification (the guide put it at about 8 days including theory and practical) and a mandatory certificate of medical fitness. It named two Australian training providers, Crew Pacific (Cairns) and Superyacht Crew Academy (Sydney Northern Beaches), and recruiters on the Gold Coast and worldwide.
POSSIBLY OUT OF DATE: the report is from 2023 and course lengths vary; Crew Pacific listed its 2026 STCW course as 5 days. For an AMSA certificate, the medical must be an AMSA one (Marine Order 76, form 303), not a UK ENG1.
Source: https://www.marinebusinessnews.com.au/?p=22968

## Q: I am foreign crew joining a superyacht in Australia. What visa do I need?
Superyacht Australia's existing-crew page summarises the Temporary Activity visa (subclass 408), Superyacht Crew stream, checked against Home Affairs on 15 September 2026. You must be 18 or over, have a crew contract or employer letter, a supporter or sponsor (captain, owner or operator), enough funds, health insurance, and meet health and character tests. The vessel must be a luxury sail or motor vessel of 24 m or more, used for sport or recreation and carrying no cargo.
Source: https://australianmarine.org/superyacht-careers-information/information-for-existing-crew/

## Q: How long does the 408 superyacht crew visa last and what does it cost?
Usually up to 12 months, and it cannot be extended (you apply again). Indicative, October 2026: from about AUD 535 for the main applicant, plus any health, police or biometric costs. Under 3 months from offshore needs a supporter's invitation letter; longer stays or onshore applications need an approved temporary activities sponsor. Apply in ImmiAccount. If the job ends, you must apply for another visa or leave within 28 days.
Note: the visa is about immigration only. It does not give you an AMSA certificate or recognition of a foreign CoC.
Source: https://australianmarine.org/superyacht-careers-information/information-for-existing-crew/

## Q: Which medical should I get for Australian yachts, an AMSA medical or a UK ENG1?
Superyacht Australia's 2023 careers booklet says an AMSA medical is mandatory for crew on Australian commercial yachts, must first be done in Australia, cost roughly AUD 650 for the first appointment and is renewed every two years (it put later renewals at about AUD 100, in Australia or overseas). It recommended booking with an AMSA medical inspector through Sonic HealthPlus. It described the UK ENG1 as cheaper but limited for commercial work in Australia, and said the AMSA medical is treated as equivalent to the ENG1.
POSSIBLY OUT OF DATE: in August 2026 AMSA widened its network of approved medical providers, so Sonic HealthPlus is no longer the only option; use AMSA's provider search (web-news-20260825-wider-network-approved-medical-providers.md). Prices are indicative only. AMSA does not accept the ENG1 for its own certificates or for a certificate of recognition (web-stcw-overseas-cert-get-stcw-certificate-recognition.md). Whether the MCA accepts an AMSA medical in place of an ENG1 is an MCA question and is not confirmed in this library.
Source: https://australianmarine.org/wp-content/uploads/Careers-Brochure-eBook-Version.pdf

## Q: What did the booklet say about STCW basic safety training?
It said every crew member needs STCW basic safety training, which it put at about 8 days and AUD 2,300 to 3,000, valid for 5 years with refresher training to keep it current. It listed Crew Pacific (Cairns) and Superyacht Crew Academy (Warriewood, NSW) as Australian providers; the Academy offers basic safety plus deckhand, interior and marine engineering courses.
POSSIBLY OUT OF DATE: 2023 figures. To work on an Australian-flag vessel the training must be AMSA approved and you apply to AMSA for a Certificate of Safety Training (now online in myAMSA, web-news-20260914-apply-cost-myamsa-now.md). The booklet does not cover officer certificates, sea time or the Marine Order 74 yacht certificates.
Source: https://australianmarine.org/wp-content/uploads/Careers-Brochure-eBook-Version.pdf
