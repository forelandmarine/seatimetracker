# Maritime Union of Australia: paraphrased fact notes

Our own summary of an MUA news article (2025, based on its file path and content). It is not the MUA's text. Several older MUA guidance pages on recertification now return "page not found" and are not used here. Marine Orders 70 and 73, Marine Notice 2025/05 and AMSA's pages win.

## Q: What are the two Integrated Rating levels?
II/4 III/4, matching the STCW navigational watch rating and engine room watch rating (with training record books and sea time), and II/5 III/5, matching able seafarer deck and able seafarer engine.
Source: https://eas.mua.org.au/MUA/Content/News_Articles/2025/amsa-integrated-rating-training-update.aspx

## Q: How much more sea time do I need to move from II/4 to II/5?
The MUA says a further seven months of sea time after II/4 III/4. It urges members to upgrade as soon as they have it, or at their next revalidation.
Source: https://eas.mua.org.au/MUA/Content/News_Articles/2025/amsa-integrated-rating-training-update.aspx

## Q: Does it matter which level I hold?
On Australian-flagged ships, minimum safe manning documents accept either level. On foreign-flagged ships the flag state decides, and some require II/5 III/5. The MUA reports AMSA told it about 250 Australian IRs (around 16%) held only II/4 III/4. Members struggling to find work on II/4 are told to contact a branch official, as AMSA has some room to help.
Source: https://eas.mua.org.au/MUA/Content/News_Articles/2025/amsa-integrated-rating-training-update.aspx

## Q: Can I revalidate my ticket early without losing time?
Yes. The MUA says AMSA accepts revalidation up to 6 months before expiry and adds the unused time to the new certificate, so revalidating 4 months early gives a certificate valid for 5 years and 4 months. This matches Marine Notice 2025/05.
Source: https://eas.mua.org.au/MUA/Content/News_Articles/2025/amsa-integrated-rating-training-update.aspx

## Q: Who trains new Integrated Ratings?
The article names METL as the only provider mentioned and says it can only train to II/4 III/4. Provisional IRs then need a job at sea to progress, and the MUA expected many II/4 trainees to graduate at the end of 2025 needing berths.
