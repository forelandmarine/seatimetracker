# Superyacht trade press on AMSA and MCA yacht certificates: paraphrased fact notes

Our own summary of trade press articles. It is not their text. Every article here is OLD (2011 to 2019) and most predate Marine Order 74 taking effect (1 January 2016) or the 2023 Marine Order 505 Sailing Master route. They are kept because crew still repeat these claims. The binding rules are MO 70 Division 4 (certificates of recognition), MO 74 and its Schedule 3 (yacht sea service), and AMSA's pages web-intl-yachting-qualifications-essentials.md, web-stcw-overseas-cert-get-stcw-certificate-recognition.md and web-stcw-prepare-sea-service-evidence-stcw.md. No 2020 to 2026 provider or press explanation of using MCA testimonials for AMSA certificates was found.

## Q: Is it true that AMSA does not recognise MCA (UK) yacht or STCW qualifications at all?
In 2014 an AMSA representative told an Australian superyacht conference that the system then did not work for yachting: there was no AMSA-approved STCW course for yacht crew, no recognition of UK MCA STCW qualifications and no yacht career path. AMSA proposed approving STCW courses, a bridging course and certificates of recognition for equivalent foreign certificates.
OUT OF DATE: this describes the position before MO 74 (in force 1 January 2016). AMSA now issues certificates of recognition for STCW certificates from recognised countries, and the United Kingdom is on AMSA's recognised country list (web-equivalence-country-eligibility.md). A recognition for an equivalent yacht certificate is only valid on Australian registered vessels that meet the MO 74 definition of a yacht.
Source: https://www.superyachtnews.com/operations/australian_crew_tickets_amsa_mca

## Q: Did AMSA review which MCA yacht certificates it would accept?
In April 2015 the press reported that AMSA had set up an industry committee to compare the MCA yacht stream with AMSA's, looking at OOW (Yachts), Chief Mate (Yachts <3000 GT), Master (Yachts <500 GT) and Master (Yachts <3000 GT). Basic safety training, Yachtmaster 200 GT and Ocean, navigational watch rating, GMDSS and ENG1 were said to have been reviewed already. An industry lobbyist said MCA-only captains were then limited to private yachts in Australia.
OUT OF DATE: the article reports a review in progress, not an outcome. The outcome is what AMSA now publishes: certificates of recognition under MO 70 Division 4 (officers need a sea service assessment and an AMSA oral exam first) and the MO 74 yacht certificates. AMSA does not accept the ENG1 for a certificate of recognition; an AMSA form 303 medical done in Australia is required (web-stcw-overseas-cert-get-stcw-certificate-recognition.md).
Source: https://www.superyachtnews.com/business/mca_and_amsa_look_towards_cocs

## Q: Someone told me MCA and AMSA each recognise the other's tickets, so moving between them is easy. Is that right?
In 2019 a north Australian superyacht crew agent said she had tried and failed to get AMSA recognition for her RYA Yachtmaster Offshore and had to start again, and warned crew that the two systems are not easily interchangeable. She called on AMSA and the MCA to explain equivalence more clearly.
CONFLICTS WITH OFFICIAL (in part): an RYA Yachtmaster Offshore or Ocean with a full commercial endorsement is now a listed prerequisite for AMSA's Sailing Master Offshore NC certificate under MO 505 (from 2023; web-domestic-sailing-master-offshore-nc.md), and AMSA does issue certificates of recognition for some STCW certificates. The warning still holds in one way: a certificate of recognition is not automatic. Officers need a sea service assessment, an oral exam and an AMSA medical, and an MCA holder who wants a full AMSA yacht certificate (not a recognition) must complete the full approved diploma course (web-intl-yachting-qualifications-essentials.md).
Source: https://www.superyachtnews.com/operations/in-response-to-the-increasing-demand-for-rya-yachtmaster-offshore-coc

## Q: Can sea time on foreign-flag superyachts count toward an AMSA certificate?
The 2011 Sail-World article said Australia did not then accept the MCA Yachtmaster for commercial command in Australia and that getting superyacht sea time counted toward higher Australian tickets was a long process.
OUT OF DATE: written five years before MO 74. AMSA's current yacht guidance says sea service must meet MO 74 Schedule 3, must be backed by letters from employers or owners (discharge certificates or letters from the vessel alone are not accepted), and service on vessels that spend a lot of time in port will not be accepted. The stored official text does not say the service must be on an Australian-flag yacht, but AMSA says qualifying service is generally accepted on commercial vessels operating at sea (web-stcw-calculate-sea-service-stcw.md), MO 74 defines a yacht as one in commercial use for sport or pleasure, and MO 70 s17 lets AMSA discount service of little relevance. Service on a purely private yacht may therefore be questioned. AMSA checks and validates the evidence, so a testimonial format (such as an MCA or PYA style testimonial) should be backed by an employer or owner letter that shows the vessel details, dates, capacity and time underway. Use AMSA's STCW sea service letter template (template-stcw-sea-service-letter.md) where possible.
Source: https://www.sail-world.com/-84412
