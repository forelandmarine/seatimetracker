# Charles Darwin University: paraphrased fact notes

Our own summary of the CDU 2026 course information sheet for MAR20324 Certificate II in Maritime Operations (Coxswain Grade 1 Near Coastal). It is not CDU's text. Where this differs from Marine Order 505 or AMSA's own pages, the Marine Order and AMSA win.

## Q: What does AMSA need from me for Coxswain Grade 1, besides the course?
CDU lists five things: the MAR20324 qualification, a short range operator certificate of proficiency from ACMA (or a higher radio qualification), qualifying sea service under Marine Order 505, the medical standard in Marine Order 505, and a pass in the final assessment set by Marine Order 505 (the AMPA). CDU tells students to confirm their own case with AMSA.
Source: https://www.cdu.edu.au/course-docs/2026/MAR20324CIF2026v1.pdf

## Q: How much sea time should I have before starting the Coxswain Grade 1 course at CDU?
CDU recommends having at least half of the qualifying sea service done before you start. It quotes the licence requirement as 30 days with a completed and approved task book (so 15 days to start the course) or 150 days without a task book (so 75 days to start). This matches Marine Order 505 Schedule 3.
Source: https://www.cdu.edu.au/course-docs/2026/MAR20324CIF2026v1.pdf

## Q: What proof of earlier sea time does CDU want at enrolment?
A record of the sea time already done goes on the student file, for example an approved record book or a statutory declaration from the skipper(s) you served under.
Note: this is CDU's enrolment evidence. For the AMSA application itself, AMSA lists the record of sea service form (AMSA 771), the task book sea service record, a letter from the operator, owner, master or chief engineer, or an approved sea service log book.
Source: https://www.cdu.edu.au/course-docs/2026/MAR20324CIF2026v1.pdf

## Q: Do I need first aid before the course?
Yes. CDU requires a current HLTAID011 Provide First Aid certificate, and the first aid unit in the qualification is credit transfer only.
Source: https://www.cdu.edu.au/course-docs/2026/MAR20324CIF2026v1.pdf

## Q: How long is the course and how is it run?
20 days of face-to-face training: one 4-week block at the Casuarina campus (Darwin) or four 1-week blocks for remote delivery, Monday to Friday 8:30 am to 4:30 pm. Attendance at every session is compulsory, and on-water training uses a CDU or hired vessel. 2026 start dates were in March, May, August and October.
Source: https://www.cdu.edu.au/course-docs/2026/MAR20324CIF2026v1.pdf

## Q: What does it cost?
Indicative, October 2026: full fee about AUD 6,560 for 2026, or free under Fee Free TAFE for eligible NT resident domestic students (limited places). Fees change each year.
Source: https://www.cdu.edu.au/course-docs/2026/MAR20324CIF2026v1.pdf

## Q: What does the qualification cover?
13 core units: survival and abandonment, firefighting, work health and safety, environmental practices, regulations for vessels up to 12 m, passage planning and navigation up to 12 m, vessel handling up to 12 m, seamanship, inboard and outboard motors, main propulsion and auxiliary systems, basic servicing and maintenance, and first aid. If you leave early you get a statement of attainment for the units completed.
Source: https://www.cdu.edu.au/course-docs/2026/MAR20324CIF2026v1.pdf
